## 2. Calibration

**I would pay US$10 on the visual evidence.** This looks like a budget commercial indie with a release-quality interface. Against *Hades*, my test is whether I can watch the enemy load the hit while choosing my dodge. Containment passes in the opening drill and loses me in the Interlock crossfire. Against *The Ascent*, my test is whether I remember a workplace after fighting across it. I remember the open berth leaking across my path. I remember Shielding Gate chiefly as a grille in violet light. The distance to premium lies in those ordinary seconds of play. ## 3. Ten most damaging defects, ranked

1. **The next attacker gets lost before its warning does.** At Interlock, white chain branches, hit numbers and the green objective boundary cross the bodies that could have fired the red shots. Dodging remains possible, choosing a counterattack becomes guesswork. Give the firing organ a directional pulse through release, dim friendly branches immediately beside it, and place damage numbers beyond the attack origin. [Interlock storyboard][story] · [Four overlaps][overlap]
2. **Small predators lose their facing in shadow.** In the Workshop I can find the violet contact above the smoke by its health bar, but I cannot quickly pick out the attacking end. In Swarm Junction the Crawler’s stripe survives after its feet disappear. Put a broad light plane on the leading shell and separate the two forefeet in the loaded pose. Preserve the dark belly. [Workshop fight][workshop] · [Swarm crowd][crowd]
3. **The hero changes identity at the first menu.** The title offers a cyan face behind a deep rim and one enormous yellow shoulder. The Breacher selector gives me a black slit, white forearm and small yellow cap. I recognize the equipment class more readily than the person. Carry the same visor shape, shoulder proportion and dark gap behind the weapon into all four rigs. [Title][title] · [Breacher selection][rig] · [Rig variants][screens3]
4. **The lower HUD conceals incoming bodies.** In Crew Checkpoint the lower pack reaches the action bar while health bars and parts of torsos remain above it. I lose the legs that tell me whether a creature is advancing or bracing. Collapse the inactive weapon descriptions in combat and bias framing toward lower threats within the marine’s safe bounds. [Kiting views][kite] · [Low-tier combat][low]
5. **Several incidents lose their cause outside the frame.** Shielding Gate retains a bent leaf without its hinge, and the Loading Bay puts a clean deck circle below a cropped rupture. Neither gives me a reliable landmark while circling. Extend the connected hinge and peeled hull into their respective approaches, preserving the dodge lane. [Incident views][incident] · [Arena views][arena]
6. **A body can be identifiable without looking alive.** The Brute’s broad tan masses and slate blade identify the family, but its face and loaded joint almost merge. The marine’s knees travel under a steady gun platform. Give the Brute a distinct jaw-to-shoulder break and make a hard stop or heavy shot travel through the marine’s hip, shoulder and stock. [Brute poses][brutemotion] · [Marine poses][marine] · [Whiteout][white]
7. **Too much of an ultimate’s consequence stays on the floor.** Railbreaker’s first contact and displacement read; by the third frame the victims and beam occupy separate bands and the 420 carries their connection. Meltdown repeats a flame glyph beside several upright bodies. Carry a directional recoil through the victim and let heat climb a struck shell before the floor residue takes over. [Railbreaker sequence][rail] · [Meltdown][pyro]
8. **Some rooms look like isolated props on a clean display deck.** The Freight load has a broad blank face, and its barrier offers little evidence of how the load fell. The Junction’s metal and growth share a purple middle value. Give Freight a torn restraint leading to its anchor and let wet secretion cross one dry, broken rib at the Junction. Both changes would make the accident intelligible while moving. [Spawn views][calm] · [Greyscale incidents][grey]
9. **Some ship conditions need their label to explain their effect.** Coolant reads as intersecting translucent circles, and the sealed Cargo exit resembles a closed door. Connect the first pool to a leaking coupling and show the lock physically spanning the shutter. Seeing the cause would help me predict the affected space. [Condition samples][conditions] · [Sealed Cargo exit][sealed]
10. **Rig selection presents two different launch decisions together.** While inspecting Arc I see “Equip Arc” next to “Quick start · Cryo.” The selection and button focus can each be correct while pointing to different runs. Keep the inspected rig as the stable primary action and label the alternate “Repeat last rig · Cryo.” [Arc button states][screens3]

## 4. Five things to preserve

1. The open berth, broken front and reflective trail tell one story from source to consequence across a playable lane. [Awakening Bay spawn][calm]
2. The rig diagrams explain inputs spatially. Bulwark’s two posts, approaching targets and Ram arrow make the combination understandable before I equip it. [Breacher selection][rig]
3. The hostile fan and illustrated death lesson teach the same evasive action. The Spitter card even gives cover a role in the diagram. [Opening drill][drill] · [Death debriefs][screens5]
4. Health and ammunition retain fixed positions and legible local backing when the Workshop goes dark. [Workshop fight][workshop]
5. Each rig changes a different shape of space. The rail cuts a line, the ice wall interrupts one, flame occupies a patch, and Arc can visit targets in sequence. Preserve that distinction as the effects get richer. [Ability and build samples][builds] · [Glacier][glacier] · [Railbreaker sequence][rail]

## 8. One month, five investments

1. **Put the next hit into the attacker’s silhouette.** Pair a creature artist with an animator on Crawler, Stalker and Brute. Give each a visible leading joint, loaded pose and exposed recovery. Use the dark Workshop and the Interlock peak as the art references. [Workshop fight][workshop] · [Interlock storyboard][story]
2. **Finish one marine at three distances.** Give one artist ownership of title, selection and play. Keep the visor aperture and asymmetric shoulder legible at 480×270, then carry recoil and braking through that shoulder to the planted foot. [Title][title] · [Breacher selection][rig] · [Marine poses][marine]
3. **Keep occupied floor visible through the busiest second.** Tune friendly light, damage-number placement, lower HUD footprint and emergency fill together. The target is being able to trace a hostile shot back to a facing body while keeping the marine in view. [Interlock storyboard][story] · [Crew Checkpoint kiting][kite] · [Emergency lighting][conditions]
4. **Recompose three rooms around physical evidence.** Bring the Gate hinge and torn hull into the fighting lane, and give the Freight wreck a sheared support and dragging restraint. The open berth already demonstrates how economical that storytelling can be. [Incident views][incident] · [Spawn views][calm]
5. **Spend the next VFX pass on the victim.** Keep the rigs’ distinct footprints. Give Railbreaker a readable landing, Arc a locked limb, Pyro a travelling ignition and Cryo a suspended step. Those are four different reasons to want to land another hit. [Railbreaker sequence][rail] · [Whiteout][white] · [Meltdown][pyro] · [Arc Overdrive][arc]
