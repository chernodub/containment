## 2. Tier and price

**I would pay US$9.99.** The title, rig selection and death debrief give me confidence that this is a small commercial release. The distance to *Hades* is clearest in the split second after a hit, when I want to see exactly what happened to one animal. Against *Ruiner*, I miss being able to recognise a place from the few metres of floor still visible during a fight. The art supports a purchase at that price. It does not yet sell the premium action-game claim. This is a judgment from real campaign frames and staged strips. Continuous timing and performance are outside the score. ## 3. Ten most damaging defects

1. **I lose small enemies from the headcount before I lose the danger warning.** In Transmission the pale backs register, but several dark front ends disappear at the left bank. Head-on, the Stalker gives me one clear blade. The missing second blade is precisely what distinguishes it from a shooter. I would give the inside face of the far blade a cool midtone, open the Crawler’s hook against its belly, and keep the Spitter’s blunt end visible when it turns. Judge all three against the darkest playable deck. [Crowd][crowd] · [Stalker][endst] · [Crawler][endcr] · [Spitter][endsp]
2. **The strongest powers do not consistently lead the eye to the victim.** Nova’s cyan perimeter is the first and largest legible shape. Slam’s peak intersects the top edge. At 300 ms its light-coloured victim finally reads to the right. Meltdown’s orange sideways recoil also works better in the later sample. Give the first illuminated facet, rising debris and subsequent shadow to the same in-frame animal. Leave the ring quiet on that side until the silhouette clears it. [Nova][nova] · [Slam peak][slam] · [Slam later][slam300] · [Meltdown later][melt300]
3. **Some floors stop naming their room as soon as the landmark leaves the camera.** I can follow Checkpoint’s toothed curve. A dark stretch in Transmission reads as an ordinary corridor, and the straight edge of its illuminated grate repeats a shape visible elsewhere. Players spend a considerable part of the pictured fights in those lanes. Continue each machine’s physical connection across the retreat route, with visibly different junctions for berth rails, cable branches and bus clamps. [Checkpoint lane][laneck] · [Transmission lane][lanetr] · [Centres][centre] · [Native fights][native]
4. **The Brute’s pose promises more weight than its joints deliver.** The hand takes the body and the heavy chest dips, then the wrist and stacked back retain much the same shape. Repetition makes the animal look like an articulated model. Flatten the bearing palm, slide the shoulder armour over the upper arm, and delay the head’s rise until the chest has cleared the planted wrist. [Stride][stride] · [Attack poses][brpose] · [Lineup][heavy]
5. **I see how to cross the final fork before I see where to stand.** The illuminated elbow of tread is easy to trace. Its end, a shot crossing it, the marine’s own light and the surrounding black deck occupy one small patch. Put a bright interrupting edge on the outside of the sill, cast its shadow into the bay and show the preview shot terminating against that edge. [Fork][fork] · [Breacher finale][story]
6. **At close contact the marine’s slate leg can look printed on the attacker.** The visor and pauldron rescue facing, which is worth keeping. When the rest of a boot becomes a flat patch, its weight and depth are ambiguous. Preserve a grounded sole on the last visible foot and confine the through-body fill to the part needed to locate the player. [Native crowd overlap][contact] · [Warden stun sequence][warden]
7. **Dark heavy surfaces lose the joints that explain their role.** The Warden’s pale dorsal teeth hold together, but the unplated arm and belly can become a single brown mass. The Matron’s sac has ribs while the leg cluster beneath it still points in several directions at once. Give the Warden a broad wrist plane and the Matron one readable head-to-foreleg overlap that survives a quarter-size frame. [Warden retake][retake] · [Heavy lineup][heavy] · [Matron cycle][matron]
8. **The edge shooter competes with the hotbar.** At Safety Interlock I can trace the red line back to the bottom edge, then have to search beside the ability slots for the actual Spitter. Move its directional marker inside the safe play area by one icon width and carry its blunt head shape into the marker. [Interlock edge][edge]
9. **The hatch and grille communicate a barrier more clearly than an enemy decision.** In Manual Control, a pale slab is conspicuous while parts of the waiting escorts look like dark cutouts in it. In the Coolant retreat a body has already left view. A player can miss the reward for acting. Put each foremost escort against a lit gap and have the retreat reverse across that gap before the hatch darkens. [Manual wait][manual] · [Guardian hold][held] · [Native retreat][retreat]
10. **The interface demotes the information that changes the next decision.** Two small lines carry the later cost of a route. The infirmary’s 42 and 77 HP figures carry more decision value than its large medkit picture. On death, prior loss, route and retry live in three different places. Promote a single consequence beside each choice and put the relevant last repair beside entry suit in the debrief. [Route order][order] · [Infirmary][forecast] · [Depleted death][depleted] · [Shop][s6]

## 8. Five investments for one month

1. **Give the three small families a readable front and side in the darkest fight.** Pair a creature artist with an animator on the far Stalker blade, the Crawler’s open hook and the Spitter’s blunt sac. Use the actual near-boot and Transmission views to judge the work. [Head-on studies][endst] · [Queue][queue] · [Dark crowd][crowd]
2. **Finish three signature impacts from contact through recovery.** For Nova, Slam and Meltdown, keep one visible animal as the centre of the contact flash, lifted material and landing. Protect the hostile red sweep through the entire effect. [Nova][nova] · [Slam][slam300] · [Meltdown][melt300] · [Cryo overlap][cryo]
3. **Make four fighting lanes and the final refuge readable from floor geometry.** Start with Passenger, Checkpoint, Transmission and Overload. Continue the berth, pivot, conductor and bus into retreat space. Show an incoming shot breaking on the lip of the final bay. [Native fights][native] · [Checkpoint][laneck] · [Transmission][lanetr] · [Fork][fork]

5. **Spend an environment and illustration pass on the opening minute.** Connect one failed berth, one track across the floor and the first creature’s silhouette into a sequence the eye can follow.
