## 2. Calibration

**A solid commercial-indie combat system inside a campaign that still feels one editing pass short of release.** The biggest gap to premium quality is how reliably a player can see the consequence of a good decision while lining up the next one. In the practice room I can tell the whole story with the sound off. The Brute commits, I step out, I shoot, I earn 20 credits. In Swarm Junction an equally good shot can stop **15 arrivals**, yet the clearest evidence is a sentence in the upper-right corner while the wounded Matron sits under the action bar. That is a much better fight than the player is likely to remember. See `onboarding/onb14-drill-during-awakening-bay.png`, `encounters/play16-brood.png` and `play/pyro/play-r16-swarm-junction-040.json`.

## 3. Ten first-hour problems, ranked

1. **At peak pressure the warning is easier to find than a safe place to put a boot.** In `native-heavy/cryo-pressure-1.png`, a 20-health marine, a curling ice trail, incoming shots, enemies and hit numbers occupy the same crossing. In `builds/v2-full-cryo-armory-armory-04.png` several victims disappear behind the dock. A clear red sector helps only if I can also read its owner and my landing. Suppress repeated friendly numbers near the boots, dim frost at the nearest committed edge and move an imminent attacker’s direction marker to the playfield side of the dock.
2. **The ordinary rifle hit has too little physical punctuation.** In `motion/scenes/pack-crawler-contacts.png` the F3 miss leaves a tiny deck skip, F8 turns the struck body and F12 carries the change into its stride. In the matching Stalker sheet I need the headings to find that sequence quickly. This is the weapon players fire thousands of times. Give the first surviving round in a burst one stronger shoulder displacement and a visibly missed foot plant, then let subsequent hits inherit the stride. Preserve attack timing.
3. **Standard often collects its debt at the relay.** Cryo took 132 damage in Transmission, took the infirmary route and entered Safety-interlock on 85 HP with zero armor, medkits or grenades. It solved the opening pincer and died during the hold. That can teach “arrive richer” more readily than “change sides after the heavy commits.” Give the first relay one conspicuous shoulder of hard cover and stage the first ranged volley so stepping around it earns a firing window. Keep the current hit caps. See `play/cryo/trace.ndjson` and `play/cryo/storyboard-safety-interlock-station.png`.
4. **Winning a beat can still leave the player shooting the wrong thing.** The Annulus staging and sealed frames show an actual gate and waiting flank. The escort frame still pairs a retreat marker with alarming red floor marks, and the brood frame puts “15 arrivals stopped” far from the collapsing body. The open-gate capture even retains the instruction to beat the flank to its arrival. Bind the objective verb to the actual gate state and send a short, visible sequence from the dead leader or sac to the retreating bodies or withering pods. See `encounters/play16-{escort,brood}.png` and the three `play16-pincer-*-containment-annulus.png` frames.
5. **Useful specialist guns are too easy to leave undiscovered.** The novice cleared with zero pistol, plasma or rocket shots. Standard Arc fired 2,027 rifle rounds and 43 plasma rounds, yet those plasma hits removed about 3,111 armor. A player who never swaps loses a satisfying answer to a real problem. At the first plated heavy, show the plate catching a rifle burst, offer the plasma key at the target and make the fractured plate reveal flesh. Later give a pair beyond rocket arming distance the same treatment. See `play/summary.md`, `play/arc/trace.ndjson` and `weapons/gallery-weapon-plasma-crew-checkpoint.png`.
6. **The end of a solved fight can outlast its decision.** Arc 2718 spends an estimated 163.3 of 923.7 active seconds in cleanup. PLAY16’s paired Standard sample reduced 8.83 to 7.30 seconds per room, a real gain. The estimate can include dangerous survivors, so trimming every tail would erase good play. When the objective is complete and only fleeing escorts remain, open the exit immediately and let a player who wants their half-credit kills stay behind. See `play/summary.md`, `PLAY16-REPORT.md` and `src/game/waves/EncounterDirector.ts`.
7. **The right lesson can lose to an unrelated prompt.** Passenger Vault asks me to break the Spitter’s sightline in the upper right and keep a lane open at the bottom. Pyro’s accurate “Q · Detonate 1” sits beside a target while a much larger placement ring draws my eye to empty floor. The drill can still offer Skip after paying its reward. Give the active lesson priority over generic prompts, subordinate unrelated placement previews while teaching and retire Skip on payout. See `encounters/play16-cover.png`, `onboarding/onb14-mastery-pyro-awakening-bay.png` and `src/ui/hud/MasteryHints.ts`.
8. **The elite that most needs a fast lateral response has the least convincing rank read.** In `elite-pairs/gallery-elite-pair-stalker-crew-checkpoint.png` I can compare two quiet animals and still find the bar faster than the enlarged hook. In the Relay Racks greyscale crowd I would read the lane first and discover the attacker’s rank after moving. Carry the elite’s asymmetric blade outside the torso silhouette throughout anticipation, and hold that shape through lock. See `crowds/value14-crowd-relay-racks-relay-racks-01-grey.png` and `ATTACK16-HANDOFF.md`.
9. **A second seed gives me different fights but few reasons to choose a different itinerary.** Both Arc runs take salvage at all three recorded junctions, install Pilgrim Pylon and meet the Warden in Armory. The Atrium opener genuinely changes, yet the player has little ownership of that variation. Let one early offer explicitly choose between two later approaches, such as a shielded firing lane with a plate-breaking reward or a brood breach with a group weapon refill. Pay it off visibly two rooms later. See `play/{arc,arc-2718}/report.json` and `RUN15-REPORT.md`.
10. **The route card makes the player assemble the price of a decision.** The sector-12 Ice Road card distributes its 180-credit cost, relay opener, bulkhead advantage, timed lock, later charge and eight-second upgrade over separate lines. The alternative offers an immediately understandable 35 health. This biases a hurried player toward whichever card takes less reading. Lead with the resulting health or credit balance and named upgrade, put the immediate encounter second, and fold the delayed receipt underneath. See `screen-extra/run15-junction-3-diagnostic-gallery.png`.

**Standard is losable through understandable interactions, and the added difficulty comes chiefly from attrition.** The lead changed rest from .80 to .72 and pickups from .55 to .42. On the same ten simulated seeds per rig, competent clears moved from 34/40 to **28/40**, split **7/5/7/9** for Breacher/Cryo/Pyro/Arc. Deaths spanned rooms 9–18, at most three in one room, with no stalls. The four-point rig spread deserves attention before another global difficulty change. In the native Cryo loss, six flank arrivals had already been canceled about three seconds into Safety-interlock. The storyboard then shows health dropping to 29, 20 and 10 around the relay. Brute contact dealt 40.3, Spitter shots dealt 71.4, and the fatal shot dealt just 6.5. Cryo died with 328 credits and Whiteout active. The player had solved a real tactical problem and still brought insufficient recovery into the next one. See `BAL16-REPORT.md` (lead follow-up), `play/cryo/play-r12-safety-interlock-station-006.json` and `play/cryo/{report.json,trace.ndjson}`.

**Story is a credible first expedition.** The novice Cryo clear absorbed 563.8 Crawler and 238.5 Stalker damage. Manual-control alone cost roughly 227 damage, yet the run reached the ending. The unchanged Story BAL16 pass cleared 35/40 simulations, with two of its five deaths in Manual-control. The novice has 300 ms reaction time, aim error and consumable hesitation, but already understands healing, aiming and shopping. These figures support forgiveness. They cannot predict a beginner’s clear rate. All six gallery runs ended in victory or death with zero runner errors. See `play/summary.md`, `play/story-cryo/report.json` and `BAL16-REPORT.md`.

**Five weapon jobs exist in the traces, with two doing most of the work.** Rifle sustains tracking, shotgun converts proximity into pellet damage, pistol finishes or covers a reload, plasma strips plate and rocket spends scarce ammunition on groups. Summing recorded weapon-hit damage including armor across each Standard trace puts rifle plus shotgun at **78.1–82.0%**. Arc 1729 fired 182 pistol shots, 2,027 rifle, 243 shotgun, 43 plasma and 29 rockets. Plasma dealt roughly 3,111 of its 3,856 recorded damage to armor. The 29 rockets registered 65 damage contacts. Those are distinct jobs in action. The Story policy explicitly limits its weapon choices and cleared using just the baseline pair. Shot counts would underrate plasma’s work, and contacts alone would overrate an eight-pellet shotgun. The earlier WEAPONS15 final Standard cohort likewise attributed 82.8% of direct damage to the pair. See `play/*/trace.ndjson`, `src/game/combat/catalog.ts` and the final section of `WEAPONS15-REPORT.md`.

**The rhythm changes with rig and seed.** On 1729, Pyro used Q 149 times and dealt about 39,479 ability damage plus 18,113 status damage. It finished 135.7 active seconds ahead of Breacher while taking about half the damage. Breacher’s largest costs were Crawlers (391.0) and Spitters (349.4). Arc 2718 took 368.8 less damage than Arc 1729 and still ran 38.7 seconds longer. The Atrium changed from a coolant pincer to an emergency-lighting relay, and Transmission from a conduit pincer to a coolant crossfire. Both Arc Atriums cost zero damage. Opening variety is real even when the bot finds an equally safe answer. Sustained retreat, opportunistic shotgun use and predictable purchases remain effective across many rooms. First V came in room 5 for Pyro and room 6 for the others. See `play/{pyro,breacher,arc,arc-2718}/report.json`.

**All six ship conditions have mechanics.** Coolant changes traction and shove distance. Venting can pull a light enemy to its death. Live conduits shock either side and extend Arc chains. Darkness rewards the first hit and delays Spitter aim. Brood eggs put three targets on a ten-second clock. Sealed bulkheads concentrate ingress and can impose a timed exit. The active conduit lanes and pink egg clusters explain themselves most quickly. A shutter or dark room asks me to infer more of the upside. Five condition types appear across the native summaries. Hull venting is supported here by rules and staged views. The run also offers delayed junction rewards and optional return contracts paying after sectors 7, 14 and 19. None of these six receipts demonstrates a human exploiting a vent or pursuing a contract. See `conditions/cond15-*-active-cargo-breached-loading-bay.png`, `RUN15-REPORT.md` and `src/game/roguelike/{shipConditions,contracts}.ts`.

## 6. What commercial peers still do better, in order

1. **Let me watch the target and choose my next step in the same glance.** *Enter the Gungeon* is my benchmark. Containment can show a good red boundary while the attacker or the landing beside it disappears behind a friendly effect.
2. **Make an ordinary round worth firing for its own sake.** *Nuclear Throne* gets an extraordinary amount of information into a small impact. Here the common rifle exchange still benefits greatly from numbers and a labelled strip.
3. **Turn mastery into an unmistakable release of tension.** *Hades* makes a punish window feel like time I earned. The Warden comes close. An interrupted Matron deserves the same emphasis as the wave it prevented.
4. **Give a second expedition a decision I can recount afterward.** Ice Road versus Shard Harvest qualifies. Two different opening labels followed by the same shopping policy rarely do. The best route decisions should be as memorable as the best augment.
5. **Teach timing by letting me recognize and repeat it.** The Brute drill is the model. A rig cue that competes with a placement ring, or a final room that quietly forgives a missed lesson through healing, gives the player less to bring into the next run.

## 8. One more month, five investments

1. **Protect the next decision at the marine’s feet.** Pair a combat designer and UI artist on the final ten seconds of the Cryo death. Keep the shooter, committed edge and one viable crossing readable through Whiteout with ordinary HUD settings. Carry that treatment into every rig.
2. **Finish one rifle burst before polishing another ultimate.** Animate a miss, first surviving hit, broken stride and kill through a moving Crawler pack, then a Stalker pack. At the normal camera, a fresh player should be able to point to the animal they hit with numbers off.
3. **Give two midgame relays a learnable answer to attrition.** Build the first volley around one recognizable blocker, then ask the player to cross after a heavy commits. Preserve the earned risk of holding the ring and compare how fresh Cryo and Breacher players recover from a mistake.
4. **Make suppression and retreat end the fight in the player’s field of view.** Start with the sealed Annulus hatch, Atrium escorts and Swarm pods. Connect the intervention to a visible change of behavior, retire the old objective immediately and let the exit become usable when pursuit is optional.
5. **Let the first route teach a weapon and the second run test a different one.** Offer two legible future fights with different supply support. Give plasma a plate to peel and a rocket a group worth saving it for. Name the chosen consequence when it arrives. This uses the existing guns, conditions and junction system to give the player a story they chose.
