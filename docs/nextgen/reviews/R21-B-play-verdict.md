## 2. Calibration

**Solid commercial indie, with a better rig system than its crowd combat.** The biggest gap to the next tier is the instant *after* a successful dodge. In the ordered Swarm frame I can name the first attacker and choose a side. In the live fight I would hesitate to spend my next shotgun shell on that body because another creature, a relay clock and a second warning occupy the same patch of floor. In the best encounters in *Hades*, recognizing an opening lets me attack more boldly. Containment earns that confidence in duels and spends it too quickly in crowds. This judgment uses final16 at `9aefc2a3`, native campaigns, staged mechanics and motion strips. Input latency, motion in the hand and the audible mix remain unjudged. ## 6. What the commercial leaders visibly deliver better

1. **An opening I can spend.** The best *Hades* exchanges let me commit a slow attack because I recognize both the missed strike and the enemy's recovery silhouette. Containment gives equally actionable information for the incoming blow only.
2. **A dangerous route I can read in one glance.** In *Enter the Gungeon*, the gap between incoming lines is itself a route. Here the hostile lane, recovery target, relay and elemental field can each be individually legible while the free patch between them disappears. [Native Swarm](/Users/chernodub/.t3/projects/containment-game/artifacts/nextgen/final16/play/arc/r16-swarm-junction-pressure.png)
3. **An act-level skill curve.** A hard combination feels earned when I have beaten each part, then an easy pair, before encountering both in a confined space. Relay Racks can make that jump in one room. The two bosses have strong verbs but too few variations on the second meeting.
4. **A run whose choices stay recognizable in the fight.** The guardian route and transformative augments are strong exceptions. The decision to buy an appointment-specific supply in sector 8 rarely changes how I read sectors 9–12. A recurring piece of enemy behaviour tied to that choice would do more than another boon card.
5. **Distinct physical payoffs at combat scale.** Armour giving way, a resisted freeze and a clean recovery should look different with the numbers hidden. Audio design already covers family tells, wall stun, armour crack and pod opening. Generic recovery has no dedicated cue. These are observations about cue coverage. Listening would be necessary to judge mix or impact. [Audio design](/Users/chernodub/.t3/worktrees/containment-game/t3-321d321e-bd530bb17d002105/docs/nextgen/AUDIO18-REPORT.md)

## 8. Five investments for one month

1. **Finish the dodge-and-return-shot exchange.** Give the earliest attacker a readable limb before the strike and a distinct recovery pose after it.
