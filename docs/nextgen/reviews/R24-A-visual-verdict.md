## 2. Would I pay?

**Yes, $8 US on the visual pitch.** This looks like a modest commercial indie with a release-quality interface and several prototype-quality enemies. The best moment in the gallery is the red Warden charge aimed through a shutter in Relay Racks. I understand the machine, the threat and the opportunity in one frame. The subsequent wash of ice makes the actual break harder to see. Compared with *Hades*, I miss being able to anticipate a hit from how an enemy carries its weight. Compared with *The Ascent*, I miss knowing what a room was used for after its main prop leaves the frame. My motion judgment concerns the sampled poses and transitions. [Relay storyboard][relay] · [Attack poses][loaded] · [Twenty campaign fights][campaign]

## 3. Ten defects, ranked

1. **The Warden's skull reads as a lid.** The huge pale shape and yellow lamp tell me where the boss is. They give me little sense of what will hit me. Cut a jaw opening beneath the ram plate, carry the light into that opening, and separate the planted forelimbs with a visible wedge of floor. Judge it in the charge and from behind the marine. [Lineup][heavy] · [Charge and stun][boss]
2. **Flashfire's most expensive-looking moment is its weakest drawing.** At peak, a rounded white insert sits inside a solid orange triangle. I can trace the cast direction, but the silhouettes at its far edge lose their individual contact points. Shorten the solid section to the muzzle and give the first two struck bodies separate, asymmetric bursts. Keep the holes visible between them. [Peak and release][flash] · [Four rig overlaps][overlap]
3. **Several encounters take place on interchangeable ground.** The centre of Passenger, Freight and Breached Loading Bay repeats a ladder-like diagonal beside loose boxes. Each has a different grade, yet at a glance I remember the same accident three times. Give Passenger a visibly numbered row of evacuation berths, Freight a torn cargo cradle, and the breach a buckled deck seam running into darkness. [Centres][centres] · [Incidents][incident] · [Grey centres][grey]
4. **Enemy bodies announce commitment too late.** The Stalker's full extension reads, but in the waiting pack the floor outlines do most of the work of distinguishing a feint from a committed swing. Put one foot down, rotate the attacking shoulder beyond the body contour and hold that loaded silhouette before releasing it. Give the feint a shorter return along a different arc. [Waiting pack][rules] · [Carrier sequence][carrier] · [Loaded poses][loaded]
5. **Whiteout still shows its construction at native size.** Three soft dark depressions surround the three bodies, and the scalloped perimeter forms one continuous cut edge. I see the red sector clearly but lose the shape of the deck around it. Run thin streaks of frost across the clearings and interrupt the outer bank at vents and obstacles. [Native Cryo][ice] · [Dense fight][white]
6. **The Breacher's portrait teaches the wrong silhouette.** The selector shows a forearm behind a broad shield. The game camera shows a dark pod behind the head and the same gun-leading posture as the other rigs. Turn the shield face outward and let the braced forearm lead the ram. This would identify the rig even with colour removed. [All rigs][rig] · [Breacher comparison][marine]
7. **The title gives its largest creature the least information.** The black foreground shape has excellent scale but just one bright spot from which to infer a face. It weakens the threat that should sell the first click. Keep the silhouette staging and catch the underside of the jaw and one overlapping shoulder with reflected hatch light. [Title pair][title]
8. **The final crossing asks the player to assemble a map during a fight.** Cyan arrows, a dashed circumference, a remote Cover label and a red firing lane all compete near the bank. Put the cover symbol on the first sheltered step and show a shot terminating on its lip in the departure view. [Rule preview][rules] · [Live bank choice][bank]
9. **Napalm looks repetitive when the camera gives it room.** The native Manual Control frame shows upright, similarly tapered flames spaced across a clean round pool. The red projectile reads well, yet the fire feels stamped onto steel. Cluster tongues along two spreading rivulets, vary their lean and leave an irregular soot edge. [Native Pyro fight][pyro] · [Napalm in a pack][napalm]
10. **A successful interruption is easier to read in the corner than in the world.** The Workshop frame says “3 arrivals canceled” in the upper right. Beside the marine, the dark sacs resemble other brood debris, so a successful tactical act has little visual payoff. Collapse one luminous seam inward and leave the spent breach visibly split. [Reserve cancellation][reserve]

## 4. Five things to preserve

1. **The filled warning belongs to one imminent attack.** Even with several enemies around the player, the red sector and its outlined owner give a direction and a source. [Ordered crowd][order]
2. **Railbreaker conveys force through its victims.** Two pale rails and the bodies displaced above their path tell a complete hit story in a still. [Native Railbreaker][rail]
3. **The death debrief teaches a rematch.** A Spitter can land the fatal shot while a Brute did the damage that mattered. The screen shows both, diagrams the avoidable attack and offers a same-rig retry. [Death card][rules]
4. **The marine has a dependable visual anchor.** The pale helmet, dark gun, lit patch of deck and small floor arrow make him quick to reacquire, including against dark green armour. [Breacher pair][marine] · [Campaign][campaign]
5. **The ending gives the decision room to land.** The broken ship, lit dish and “All aboard lost / New Earth warned” communicate the cost before presenting the statistics. [Ending pair][end]

## 8. Five investments for one month

1. **Give the Warden one convincing charge cycle.** Couple a readable jaw and shoulder wedge to a planted foot, compression, release and wall impact. Carry the same physical consequence into the shutter break. The regular Brute rebuild is already in progress. [Lineup][heavy] · [Relay sequence][relay]
2. **Finish the player-fire and frost transitions.** Spend the effects artist's time on Flashfire contact, Whiteout's outer bank and broken Napalm edges. Keep the red warning readable during the brightest frame, and make each struck animal explain where energy went. [Flashfire][flash] · [Whiteout][ice] · [Napalm][pyro]
3. **Make three ordinary lanes identifiable in greyscale.** Start with Passenger, Transmission and Diagnostic. Bring a berth number and blanket, a damaged dish feed, and a recognisable patient cradle into the player's route. Light each object from a plausible local source. [Incidents][incident] · [Grey centres][grey]
4. **Animate the decision before the hit.** Give the Carrier and small enemies clearly different waiting, feint and loaded profiles. Use the Stalker's horizontal thrust as the standard for how much a silhouette can say in one frame. [Carrier][carrier] · [Stalker][stalker] · [Loaded pack][order]
5. **Finish one suit across the whole presentation.** Use the Breacher to align the portrait, title light, shield attachment and in-play bracing pose. Once the same figure reads in all three places, apply that treatment to the other rigs. [Title][title] · [Breacher comparison][marine] · [All four rigs][rig]
