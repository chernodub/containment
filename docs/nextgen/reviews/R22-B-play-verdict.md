## 2. Calibration

**Solid commercial indie. The biggest gap to the genre leaders is how little initiative a player earns by reading a crowd correctly.** I can see where to step out of a Brute sweep. If I keep the shotgun trained on its shoulder, I can profit. If I dodge, identify the Brute again behind another body, then turn to shoot, the opening can already be over. In *Hades*, the confidence to start a heavy attack after recognizing a miss is part of the pleasure of learning the enemy. Containment delivers that confidence most convincingly when a Warden hits a wall.

## 3. Ten first-hour problems, ranked

1. **A successful dodge often buys too little time to turn and fire.** The isolated Brute is unmistakable in `play26/play26-exposed-swarm-junction.png`. In late Standard its ordinary recovery lasts about 171 ms, and a rifle hit at base damage earns 2.4 extra damage. Give the first heavy that misses a committed swing a roughly 450 ms opening and make its first weapon punish interrupt or stagger it. Keep the brief Crawler openings as rewards for players who already have their aim set.
2. **The steepest damage spike can arrive in an ordinary corridor.** Cryo lost 93.4 suit in Relay Racks, then 200.7 in Service Shaft Landing, including 90.3 to Stalkers and 81.3 to Crawlers. A player who learned to dodge a lunge can still land among the small bodies. Stage the first Shaft lunge across an open escape lane, then send Crawlers across that same lane on the repeat. See `play/cryo/report.json` and the Shaft cell of `twenty-native.png`.
3. **At low health the player can see danger and still lose the attacker.** In the native Workshop images, Arc has 1 HP with a bright wedge underfoot and bodies cut by the upper edge. Cryo has 18 HP with ice, feet and a red fan in the same narrow passage. During a committed attack, locally soften friendly frost and debris behind the striking limb and preserve a visible strip of floor on the escape side. [Arc](/Users/chernodub/.t3/projects/containment-game/artifacts/nextgen/final17/native-heavy/arc-pressure-1.png) · [Cryo](/Users/chernodub/.t3/projects/containment-game/artifacts/nextgen/final17/native-heavy/cryo-pressure-1.png)
4. **The waiting pack looks busy while giving the player few new decisions.** In `windups/loaded-crowd-relay-racks-relay-racks.png`, the first red attack reads immediately. The waiting Brutes occupy floor, but staying in motion answers their brace, shove and many feints alike. Let one waiting Stalker visibly pace toward a specific escape lane and commit from there when its turn arrives. Its movement would give the player a reason to change course before the warning.
5. **The Warden’s flank promises a larger target than the player can hit.** Its body reaches about 2.99 m from centre against a 2.0 m collider. Its forebody projects over the ramp in the Crew Checkpoint stun frame. A shot at the rear armor can look squarely aimed and miss. Pull the tail’s heavy silhouette inward or give that visible armor a weapon-only hit region. Keep the navigation radius, since enlarging it closes routes. See `heavies/trio-crew-checkpoint-colour-480.png` and `docs/nextgen/CREATURE18-HANDOFF.md`, WARDEN25.
6. **The finale teaches cover earlier than it makes cover worth taking.** The preview bolt hits the reactor, but its label is tiny against the dark bend and the long way costs about 2.3 seconds of walking. Pyro entered the choice at 57 HP and zero armor and reached the short bank at 28 HP. Light the sheltered bend and time a visible near-lane volley so a player with a spent dash can weigh that specific shot against the detour. See `play26/play26-final-fork-overload-floor.png` and `play/pyro/trace.ndjson`.
7. **The first armor break teaches a key better than a material.** Every Standard bot fired three plasma shots at the Passenger Vault target, a choice its policy prioritizes. At 480×270, the overhead stagger is easier to distinguish than the before-and-after shoulder. Shed a conspicuous plate toward the lit side and leave a different shoulder profile for the follow-up shot. See `screen-extra/play22-plate-passenger-vault.png` and `screen-extra/play22-plate-break-passenger-vault.png`.
8. **Repeated guardian appointments spend the surprise of the first one.** In the Pyro report, Armory, Relay and Manual Control all book a Warden. Baiting the ram into a wall is a good first lesson. Give the return fight a different reason to aim the charge, such as breaking a screen that otherwise shelters a Spitter. See `play/pyro/report.json` and `review-sheets/frames-09.png`.
9. **Holding a relay can bury the required movement cue.** In the native Arc Swarm cell the relay, attackers, warnings and the action dock converge on the marine. The clock pauses when the player leaves the ring, so a blind retreat has a cost. Bring the first flanker across the far half of the ring and leave a readable firing position on the near half. See `twenty-native.png` and `src/DepthGame.ts`, `tickFloorObjectives`.
10. **A build’s most interesting trade can read like another upgrade.** Shop and draft screens give good terms, yet an Ice Road player is giving up both a projectile wall and its manual explosion. Show the lost move beside the new one on the choice card, using the same incoming volley in both diagrams. This lets the player anticipate the cost before the next cast. See `review-sheets/screens-06.png` and `src/game/abilities/augmentCatalog.ts`.

## 6. What the commercial leaders deliver better, in order

1. **Time to act on a correct read.** The best *Hades* exchanges give a deliberate slow counterattack a place in the rhythm. Here that privilege belongs mainly to the boss stun and the practice drill.
2. **A visible way through danger.** In *Enter the Gungeon*, the space between threats tells the player where to go. In the native Workshop and Swarm frames, separate cues are visible but the safe patch, the marine’s feet and the target can all coincide.
3. **A second encounter that changes the first lesson.** The Matron can change the population of the room. Repeated Warden fights could change the room itself or make a familiar bait dangerous from a new direction.
4. **A build choice recognizable by its consequences several rooms later.** Ice Road and Live Wire meet that standard. More of the expedition could acknowledge them through how a pack approaches, a route opens or a firing position becomes valuable.
5. **A physical and audible answer to “what did I just do?”** The shotgun catch and boss slump work visually. Plate loss and ordinary recovery remain slight. AUDIO18 specifies distinct family, armor, wall-stun and pod cues. The adapter plays the exposed alert on Warden wall stun. Generic melee recovery lacks that dedicated cue. Perceived impact and masking need listening. ## 8. Five investments for one month, in order

1. **Give one ordinary enemy a punish players will deliberately seek.** Start with the Brute. Pair a longer missed-swing recovery with a body-local cue and a first-shot stagger. Watch desktop players in Relay and Workshop: after dodging, do they turn back, and can they say what their shot bought?
2. **Make one escape lane readable in the hardest three fights.** Work on Cryo’s Shaft and Workshop and Arc’s contested Swarm hold. Move one approach, reduce friendly effects behind committed limbs, and pan toward playable space at a pinned corner. Preserve the attack budget and compare what players can see before increasing pressure.
3. **Make the Warden’s body trustworthy and its second appearance different.** Fit the rear silhouette or weapon target to the apparent mass, preserve navigable clearance, then let one later charge break useful cover. The same ram can teach mastery twice.
4. **Give the final crossing two credible winners.** Tune one vulnerable approach with a visible incoming volley and an inviting covered bend. Observe hurt players with a ready dash and healthy players with a spent dash. A player who can explain why the longer walk saved them is better evidence than an even split of bank choices.
5. **Carry one build decision through an entire act.** Use the booked Matron or Warden to preview an ammunition purchase, rehearse its use on the approach, then make the first successful pod interruption or plate break unmistakable on the guardian. The run gains a payoff the player can trace back to a choice made nine rooms earlier.
