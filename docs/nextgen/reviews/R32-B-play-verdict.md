Seven is a solid commercial indie release, five a competent prototype, and ten best in class.

## 2. Calibration

**I would pay US$9.99 for this as a compact desktop action campaign.** The rigs justify another run. The largest gap to a premium roguelike is that a good decision can change the simulation without giving the player a clear account of what they earned. The best exception is Glacier. I can see the wall stop a shot, decide to give up cover, then see the shatter where the wall stood. I want that clarity at the guardian door and console too. This judgment covers the desktop frames, traces and reviewed source at `394661d1`. Input feel, audible separation and unaided human discovery remain unverified. ## 6. What commercial genre leaders deliver better

1. **A readable next action under pressure.** In *Hades*, the body finishing a swing often tells me whom to punish as I dodge. Here I can escape a Crawler footprint and still lose its owner.
2. **A win that changes the next few seconds.** Baiting the Relay ram delivers this. A guardian retreat behind the camera is much harder to connect to an opening in the encounter.
3. **Upgrades that change both opportunity and cost.** Ice Road deserves a crossing where its speed is desirable and a later volley where I miss the wall. A power’s second use sells the build.
4. **A repeat run with a different tactical memory.** The Relay loader and shutter gunner are a start. In *Enter the Gungeon*, remembering which threat owns a lane can change the route through an entire room. A different side of a hatch is a weaker memory.
5. **A loss that suggests a different run.** The fatal diagram gives a specific step to try. The same precision applied to the last repair or supply decision would make the expedition history worth reading.

## 8. Five investments for one month

1. **Finish the Crawler contact in an actual mixed fight.** Work from the native Swarm knot. Give the striker room through recoil and keep the marine’s near boot visible. Watch fresh desktop players point out the attacker and pick their next target.
2. **Tie guardian retreat to a visible enemy commitment.** Put the pending group at the door where the player is already looking, bind its retreat to the wall bait or brood interrupt, and let the safe lane persist long enough to cross.
3. **Make the console’s two positions readable from cover.** Preview both from the first escort’s approach. Observe whether players can explain why they stayed or stepped out, including on a clean open-grille clear.
4. **Give each exclusive a second meaningful encounter.** Pay off its benefit in Transmission and expose its sacrifice at a later hold. Start with the two hardest effects to recognize in native frames, Ember Relay and Pilgrim Pylon.
5. **Connect the early interrupt, late interception and recovery decision.** Reuse one readable shooter-to-cover crossing in sectors 3–4 and the late pair. On death, show what that shooter cost and which repair the player actually took or passed up.
