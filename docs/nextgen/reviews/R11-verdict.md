Calibration (both): an advanced prototype with a commercially presentable vertical slice; credible as a free release;
not yet a convincing commercial indie game; far from premium/AA. Scale: 7 = solid commercial indie, 10 = best in class.
These reviewers are harsher than the R6–R10 reviewer, so the numbers are not directly comparable with earlier rounds.

Lead reading — what holds the game back, in order:
1. Trust in the central figure and the camera: marine facing vs aim marker vs beam direction disagree in some frames, torso turns translucent in ability frames, marine or weapon leaves the screen edge, fights happen under the action bar.
2. The dangerous small attack is the least visible thing on screen (320–460 ms windows, thin footprints, weak body commitment).
3. Fights ask one question (retreat and shoot); late difficulty comes from 60–69% elites with control resistance.
4. Ordinary gunfire and hits lack physical consequence; ability effects read as flat graphics (Flashfire, Railbreaker, Cryo Nova, the Overdrives).
5. Rooms have functions but no history; light fixtures are the loudest objects; infestation is thin strands.
6. Creatures are a body on thin legs; Stalker weakest; elites and the two boss-class variants are not distinct enough.
7. Rewards are administrative cards; only eight rig-specific augments, none transformative; credits go unspent; the route screen offers no decision; onboarding teaches keys, not survival.
8. Ending image repeats the standing marine instead of staging the cost.

Must not break (both reviewers): interface hierarchy and ability diagrams, death receipts, four distinct rig vocabularies, honest attack geometry with recovery windows, landmark rooms (Armory carousel, Service Shaft, reactor), corpses and status reactions.
