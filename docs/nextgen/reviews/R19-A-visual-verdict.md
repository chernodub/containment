## 2. Would I pay?

**Yes, US$10 on the visual pitch.** This looks like a budget commercial indie with a better-than-average expedition interface. My *Hades* benchmark is being able to pick the attacker from its body while already planning my escape. Here the red lane often does that work for the creature. My *The Ascent* benchmark is knowing what people did in a room from the machinery beside my feet. Coolant Plant reaches that standard. The middle of Residential Gallery gives me two stripes of arrows. Those differences would matter to me for much more of a run than the polish on the first screen. This judgment covers the captured play and sampled poses. [Swarm wind-ups] · [Centres] · [Campaign]

## 3. Ten most damaging defects, ranked

1. **A warning can lose its owner.** Two white-outlined Crawlers and their sectors meet beneath the marine at Swarm Junction. In the native Overload Floor fight the arc, field edge and bodies meet in a similar knot. I know to move, but choosing an interrupt takes another look. Keep the committed head and striking limb legible where bodies overlap, and taper friendly effects across that small area. [Swarm wind-ups] · [Campaign]
2. **The small predators have stronger backs than faces.** The Crawler’s pink stripe and Stalker’s paired hooks survive reduction. Their leading edges disappear, so an animal preparing to move sideways can look as if it is coming straight at me. Extend one leading jaw or hook beyond the body and leave a deck-coloured gap behind it in the loaded pose. [Crawler] · [Stalker] · [Relay wind-ups]
3. **The camera spends space above a threat that enters from below.** The Armory carousel and empty upper arc are visible while the Cryo pack crosses the ammo dock and continues beyond the frame. I lose the attackers’ approach. Shift that composition toward the nearest committed pack while retaining full-body marine clearance and the local numeral backings. [Armory fight]
4. **Some room centres lose the cause of the accident.** The Gate shoe has rails running out of frame. The Residential centre puts its displaced furnishings on the far upper edge. A player circling either room spends more time on lane markings than on the story. Bring a jammed section of gate carriage alongside the shoe and carry domestic wreckage into the gallery’s lane margin. [Centres] · [Incidents] · [Kiting]
5. **Large props still share one material.** The Freight load has a readable lit top and dark base, but its casing, restraint and nearby wall have much the same hard edge and broad fill. The Armory carousel has enough geometry to identify the machine and little surface evidence of weapons being handled there. Paint wear at contact points, exposed steel at breaks and dull rubber on grips, at a scale that survives the gameplay camera. [Freight] · [Armory]
6. **The reactor crossing is harder to read than its circles.** The cropped salmon boundary and pale ring dominate the staged crossing. The route between the marine and the relay all but disappears. Carry a sequence of powered floor lamps from the approach to the relay and lower the contrast on the far side of the field. [Crossing] · [Finale storyboard]
7. **The sustained fire looks cool in the middle.** Soft orange spots and isolated perimeter flames identify the radius, but little ties the heat to the deck or to the victims’ feet. Paint an irregular char bed, split the central spots into rising tongues and let their light catch the nearest lower plate. Keep the existing opening around a hostile lunge. [Napalm] · [Meltdown]
8. **Boss vulnerability is carried by symbols.** In the Warden slump I spot the broken yellow arc before the exposed crown. The Matron’s bright round brood patch takes attention from her jaw and pod release. Give the crown a clean profile against the shoulder and pose the Matron so the lifting carapace exposes the emerging pod against dark floor. [Warden] · [Matron] · [Pods]
9. **The shooter absorbs less recoil than the target.** A Brute visibly pitches over under a heavy shot while the marine keeps nearly the same shoulder-to-stock spacing. That makes weapons with very different consequences feel alike in the pose strips. Plant the rear foot, drive the stock into the shoulder, and let the hips recover one beat after the muzzle. [Brute motion] · [Marine motion]
10. **Important consequences arrive as small annotations.** The booked guardian is smaller than the shop consumables, the self-blast lands in the checkpoint toast area, and the staged plate break largely reads as flecks and a damage number. Give the guardian the next-encounter heading, put the blast loss beside armour, and show one recognisable plate leaving the enemy’s outline. [Decisions]

## 4. Five things to preserve

1. **The player has a reliable visual signature.** The pale plates, single coloured shoulder and dark gun let me recover the marine in the native Cryo fight even when I lose individual enemies. [Campaign] · [Finale storyboard]
2. **Hostile geometry stays readable through friendly powers.** Whiteout can fill most of the screen while the red swipe still gives an exact escape edge. Preserve that ordering through any effects pass. [Whiteout] · [Overlap]
3. **The interface teaches a specific next action.** The Stalker debrief pairs the actual attack lane with a sideways arrow and “Dash was ready.” That is a lesson I could use on the next run. [Death]
4. **The first junction makes a promise the ship remembers.** Choosing the plated escort or brood changes the picture on the card, and the Coolant Plant arrival names the threat I booked. [Routes] · [Arrivals]
5. **The best environmental storytelling follows physical evidence.** Light runs from the split pipe across the frozen spill in Coolant Plant. I understand what happened before I read the room name. [Incidents]

## 8. Five investments for one more month

1. **Make each attack recognisable with the warning covered.** Start with the Crawler claw and Stalker hook, then apply the same pose work to the Warden’s exposed crown and the Matron’s pod release. Keep the current red warnings. A player who can see the attacker as well as the escape route gains an actual combat choice. [Crawler] · [Stalker] · [Swarm wind-ups] · [Warden] · [Pods]
2. **Recompose the two fights where the player loses the approach.** Bias the Armory shot toward the incoming lower pack. Light the Overload crossing along the route the player must travel. Both changes give the player more usable space in an existing room. [Armory fight] · [Crossing] · [Finale storyboard]
3. **Give four room centres a recognisable job.** Start with a domestic fixture in Residential, a scanner throat in Checkpoint, a connected gate carriage and a passenger-status bank at Manual. Spend the material work where hands, cargo or machinery would have worn each object. [Centres] · [Incidents] · [Freight]
4. **Give each finisher a consequence on the target and floor.** Prioritise an uneven, deck-lit burn, then connect Rail’s launch and landing and hold the last Arc victim in contraction. Use Whiteout’s combination of victim, surface and travelling front as the reference. [Napalm] · [Rail] · [Arc endpoint] · [Whiteout]
5. **Finish one marine heavy-shot cycle and use its strongest pose in the selector.** A planted foot, compressed shoulder and delayed recovery would improve the most frequently seen character and the first choice screen with the same work. [Marine motion] · [Brute motion] · [Rigs]
