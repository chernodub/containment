## 2. Calibration

**Solid commercial indie. The biggest gap to the genre leaders is how seldom reading one enemy gives the player control of the next exchange.** The missed Brute swing is worth turning toward. In Diagnostic Gallery the marine can do that and still have a Crawler under the gun, another lunge across the exit and little floor visible beyond the wall. The player has earned an attack against one creature. In the best rooms in *Hades*, recognizing a commitment also tells me where I can stand to deliver my next attack. Containment reaches that standard when a Warden hits a wall or a Glacier catches a volley. It reaches it less often in its ordinary packs. ## 3. Ten first-hour problems, ranked

1. **Two ordered attacks can still hide the way out.** In Arc 2718’s Shaft pressure frame the marine has 41 health, two lunge capsules cross underfoot and the left wall cuts off the retreat. The start order is fair. By the time both lines are apparent, choosing a safe side is the harder task. Stage the second Stalker’s approach across the far side of the landing, visible before the first lock, and leave a body-width inside turn until that lock lands. Keep the existing hit spacing. [Played Shaft sequence](/Users/chernodub/.t3/projects/containment-game/artifacts/nextgen/final18/play/arc-2718/storyboard-service-shaft-landing.png)
2. **Returning fire on a miss is a good move that the crowd seldom invites.** The five Standard runs staggered a Brute within 460 ms of 16 of its 40 misses. That is a real reward. All five together recorded only 88 exposed weapon hits of 12,602, across all families. A new player who dodges away from the body must find it, turn the mouse and decide whether the *next* attacker makes the shot unsafe. On the first mixed heavy beat, hold the next light attacker’s lock until the Brute’s first recovery ends, then let later fights contest the opening. Give the buried cleaver a readable edge in the dark. 3. **The ordinary late-room attrition can outweigh its featured encounter.** In Cryo’s 162.7-point Coolant Plant, 126.0 came from Crawler contact. In its 131.3-point Infested Workshop, 118.7 came from Stalkers. A player can beat the booked guardian and remember losing most of the suit to the cleanup around it. Shift a portion of the overlapping screen arrivals to a visible reserve breach that killing the brood or breaking a flank can cancel. Keep the pressure for players who ignore that objective. ## 6. What the commercial leaders deliver better, in order

1. **A route the player can see before committing.** In *Enter the Gungeon*, the gap between two attacks is often as recognizable as the attacks themselves. At the Shaft corner I can identify both red capsules and still struggle to find the passage between them.
2. **An exchange that changes who has the initiative.** *Hades* gives a player who knows the end of an attack string enough confidence to begin a slow attack of their own. Containment has that confidence in the Warden wall stun and a one-on-one Brute punish. The pack often takes it away before the return shot.
3. **A returning enemy that tests a new use of an old skill.** The first Warden teaches aiming an enemy charge into steel. A later Warden surrounded by more aliens tests crowd survival. Let that same charge open a shelter, disable a machine or expose a gunner and the player gets to demonstrate mastery.
4. **Build decisions reinforced by room outcomes.** The commercial benchmark is remembering a run because of something one *did* with a build. Ice Road and Live Wire already support that kind of story. A late encounter or optional reward that recognized the strip crossed or the tether swept would give those choices a payoff beyond the draft card.
5. **Physical feedback at normal fighting size.** A premium hit lands on an identifiable body, changes its posture and leaves a readable consequence on screen through the next input. The title, boss poses and rig portraits promise that quality. The faint damaged shoulder, half-hidden Stalker and broad residual flame still regularly fall short. The recent audio work covers danger, impact and recovery separately. Judging the mix would require listening.

## 8. Five investments for one month, in order

1. **Author three crowded exchanges around the escape and return shot.** Start with Shaft, Coolant Plant and Diagnostic Gallery. Give each a visible approach, one meaningful cutback and one enemy the marine can punish from the new position. Keep the present heavy damage bonus and hit cadence. Watch a fresh desktop player point to the escape *before* the second lock and choose whether to fire back.
2. **Make a fired shot explain what it changed.** Across the five families, focus the animation and local contrast on the loaded limb, the buried limb and the first return impact. Keep the armor fragment or wound visible through the next shot. Shorten the opaque residual over surviving actors, then listen under sustained rifle fire for the warning, miss and stagger as three distinct beats.
3. **Turn the last crossing into two defensible decisions.** Give the protected bend a credible melee exit and the near lane two visible, dodgeable firing threats. Compare what people with a spent dash and an intact dash elect to do. Ask which specific hit their choice avoided. Either bank can be the correct answer.
4. **Rebuild one repeat Warden and one Matron escort around a room feature.** Let a repeat Warden break a shooter’s shelter. Put a Matron pod where destroying it keeps the relay’s safe side open. Reuse the charge and hatch rules so the new challenge comes from understanding the room.
5. **Carry a transformative choice from rehearsal to payoff.** Let a first-run player defer the early pair, try the base interaction once, and see it offered again with the proven trade-off. Give the next junction an encounter where that choice suggests a different plan. Ask the player afterward which decision in the draft changed what they did in the fight.
