## 6. What the genre leaders deliver better, in order

1. **A destination for the dodge.** In *Enter the Gungeon* I can read a gap and plan the first shot from the other side. Here the attack outline often communicates when to leave more clearly than where to arrive.
2. **A reward visible at the target.** A *Hades* shield-bearing enemy turning its back tells me what the flank achieved. Containment has equivalent rules, but the grille, saved armour or newly exposed shooter can sit outside the part of the screen where I made the decision.
3. **An opening act that auditions the real game.** The best first rooms let a novice recover from the exact error that becomes expensive later. Containment’s clearest spatial diagnosis currently arrives after death.
4. **Rooms that make a build change my plan twice.** Choosing to sacrifice a wall for speed should change how I enter one room and how I escape the next. The purchase text promises more of that than the campaigns demonstrate.
5. **A different story to tell about run two.** A choice about which danger to face before a heal is easier to remember than a different starting wave inside the same twenty-room order.

## 8. Five investments for one month, in order

1. **Build one repeatable dodge-and-return lesson.** Prototype it first in an early room with one threat, one corner and a visible queued escort. Reuse the same physical rule in Manual Control. A fresh player should be able to point to the enemy they prevented from joining.
2. **Move a meaningful slice of Standard pressure into sectors 3–8.** Introduce one avoidable armour cost and an immediate recovery opportunity, then give Story’s first contested hold room for a lateral dodge. Preserve the clean reward for skilled play and leave paid repair alone for this pass.
3. **Make contact and consequence readable where the player aims.** Give the lead Crawler a clean strike silhouette, the shutter a persistent open firing lane, and the sealed grille a visible waiting body. Check these while moving and firing with headphones on.
4. **Give each exclusive two rooms with different demands.** Use Transmission for the immediate opportunity and a later junction for the tradeoff. A Cryo player who picked Ice Road should feel both the value of movement and the cost of losing the wall.
5. **Use fresh desktop players to settle the remaining design bets.** Observe an unaided first run through the first failed hold, then a retry with the same rig. Ask where they intended to land, which enemy hit them, what their purchase changed and why they chose a bank. Spend the last week on the first repeated misunderstanding their inputs reveal.
