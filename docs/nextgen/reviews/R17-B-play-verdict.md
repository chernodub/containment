## 6. What commercial leaders still do better

1. **Let me plan the next step during my strongest attack.** The pleasure of a dense *Enter the Gungeon* volley is seeing the gap while still aiming. In Containment, friendly fire can consume the attention needed to find that gap.
2. **Make ordinary contact worth watching.** *Nuclear Throne* is my benchmark for feeling a single round change a crowded fight. Containment has the rifle catch in the animation, but too often only the flash survives the full composition.
3. **Turn earlier choices into later expectations.** The draw of another *Hades* run includes anticipating how a reward changes the next several encounters. Coolant Plant pays off the route card once. More rooms should make that decision matter along the way.
4. **Give the climax a question of its own.** The final sacrifice has narrative weight. A final relay that costs a quarter of the preceding room’s losses, as it did for Breacher, gives the combat a weaker ending than the story. ## 8. Five investments for one more month

1. **Make the next attacker readable through every rig’s strongest effect.** Start with the actual Pyro Diagnostic and Arc Manual Control frames. Preserve the leading limb, the firing origin and one visible exit from the footprint, including when the attacker crosses the dock.
2. **Recompose rooms 18–20 as a sequence.** Give Annulus a legible resource risk, Manual Control a flank the player can dismantle in order, and the final relay a decisive last change of position. Keep the possibility of losing in all three.
3. **Teach plasma and rockets with outcomes the player can see.** Let a plate visibly shed under plasma, a rocket stop a pair, and a close blast identify its cost immediately. Repeat a missed opportunity at the next suitable fight.
4. **Give the guardian route a middle chapter.** Use an existing supply stop and one intervening encounter to prepare the chosen weapon and enemy relationship. Have that preparation alter the escort or pod opening at Coolant Plant.
5. **Give a well-timed response a moment of its own.** Finish the Warden’s wall stun, the Matron’s interruption and the rifle’s first surviving contact in crowded play. Pair each with a distinct recovery sound and audition it under sustained fire. Those are the moments in which a player learns what to repeat.
