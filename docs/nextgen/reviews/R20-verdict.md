**Honest reading: the same plateau.** No score moved more than half a point and the moves cancel. Six rounds after
the plateau was first called, the game sits at play 6.5–8, Astra visual 6–7.5, Opus 5.5–7.5. It is a solid commercial
indie; no reviewer calls it premium or close to it.

What wave 23 did and did not do, from the evidence:
- **The edge tell works.** All 47 off-view projectile onsets matched in the traces carried a shooter-specific jaw
  marker (last round 74 of 385 hit-causing warnings began off-screen with nothing). It still asks for "a second place
  to look".
- **The token cap holds but does not finish the job.** Every Standard trace peaked at two melee and one ranged
  wind-up, except where half-token Crawlers allow three; three fans can still overlap under the marine, and two
  Stalker lunges 0.52 s apart cost Cryo a quarter of its suit. "The clocks of adjacent melee attacks merge" is first in
  both Astra verdicts.
- **The interlock controls are now used** (four of five Standard runs). **The final banks are not a choice**: every
  survivor took the short exposed crossing.
- **The first plate is still endured with the rifle** by a novice (25 hits, no damage taken, rewarded anyway).

What the three verdicts agree on:
1. **Order among overlapping melee attacks** — which lands first, shown on the attacking limb, not the floor.
2. **Camera framing against room bounds** (Opus's first item; Astra agrees for the bottom approach and the dock): the
   clamp parks the marine at an edge or under the HUD.
3. **Creatures in crowds**: small ones hide their business end; pale bodies and white hit flashes reach the marine's
   value (the value cap from CREATURE23 demonstrably does nothing); the Brute is "barely taller than the marine"
   (it was the one family not enlarged in wave 17); the Matron still reads as a translucent cone.
4. **Arena centres and landmarks**: establishing views are strong, fighting lanes revert to deck markings; landmarks
   lack a light tier.
5. **Rig portraits**, third round running: "a flat, overexposed render in a stumbling pose" (Opus); "three rigs sell
   the same figure" (Astra).
6. **Numbers and small type**: stacked damage numbers cover crowds in ability frames; the Weapons tab and supply cards
   crowd decisive information into small type.

Wave 24 (from this): order overlapping melee by rule and draw only the next attack filled; a camera clamp that
prefers showing void to pushing the marine to an edge (measured against bot damage, as CAM22 was); Brute mass; a
working creature value ceiling and a damage-number budget; portraits with a different silhouette per rig; four room
stories carried into the lane with a landmark light tier; final banks and the first plate as real decisions.
