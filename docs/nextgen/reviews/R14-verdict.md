Calibration: "budget indie with release-quality decision screens and uneven field art", US$10 (Astra visual);
"near release quality as a combat system, late commercial alpha as a twenty-room game" (Astra play); "good
early-access indie", US$12–15 (Opus). The play side now sits at or above 7 in five of eight areas. The picture does
not: nothing visual is above 7.5, and the two art directors disagree about characters (6.0 vs 4.5) and crowd
readability (6.5 vs 5.5) — the Opus pass looked at denser crowd evidence and scored it lower.

What moved: onboarding (+0.9), ability spectacle (+1.0 Astra), rigs, interface, animation for the first time in four
rounds. What did not: environments, cohesion. What went down: Opus on first impression, readability, environments and
characters; build variety (the first junction is a non-decision and the itinerary is fixed — RUN15, already merged,
addresses that but was not in the gallery).

Lead reading — what holds it back now, in order:
1. Actors are pattern, not mass. The marine reads as armour but as white/black/tan blotches ("cow-print"), his title
   pauldron and visor vanish in play, rig colour covers too little; creatures carry a blown highlight that outranks
   him in crowds; small elites show no rank. Opus names the cause: the hard posterised ramp with clipped speculars on
   characters. (→ SHADE15, then an attacker/elite pass)
2. The player reads the floor, not the attacker. The red lane is clear; its owner, its escape-side edge under fire,
   and the marine under his own effects are not. (→ WARN16, MOTION16)
3. Rooms keep their best thing at the edge of the frame over 60% empty tile; materials do not separate. (→ FRAME16)
4. Bodies lack weight: deep-squat run, recoil smaller than the shell's arc, held endpoints, victims flattened by hit
   flashes. (→ MOTION16)
5. Winning an encounter looks like the crowd thinning; cover is not introduced; a boss's opening looks like a pause;
   mastery hints use the wrong verb and vanish; a fifth of play is cleanup; the quick-start button launches a
   different rig from the highlighted one. (→ PLAY16)
6. Late attrition is uneven by room and rig. (→ BAL15)
7. The title still promises richer materials than play delivers; rig-select portraits became small and frontal.

Keep: the first drill and the death lesson teaching the same move; menus that explain builds in spatial terms; the two
protected HUD numbers; the human traces (the open berth, the rabbit by the open case); powers that explain themselves
through the victim; the four rig kits and their exclusive augments; twenty distinct room grades with ink-lined props.

Not measurable in this round: performance (the host ran at load ≈ 900 during the bench) and audio.
