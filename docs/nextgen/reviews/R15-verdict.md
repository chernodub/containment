Calibration: "budget commercial indie with a release-quality interface and uneven combat art", US$10 (Astra visual);
"a solid commercial-indie combat system inside a campaign that still feels one editing pass short of release" (Astra
play); "competent early-access indie", US$12–15 (Opus).

**Honest reading: the game has plateaued.** Two full waves (weapons, run variety, ship conditions, actor shading,
warning edges, room framing, motion weight, encounter clarity, creature silhouettes, Overdrive aftermaths) moved no
score by more than half a point in either direction, which is inside reviewer noise. Play sits at 6.5–7.5, interface
at 7.5, the picture at 5.5–7.0. It is a solid indie game; it is not a premium one, and adding more does not move it.

What the three verdicts agree on:
1. **Too much is on screen and on the page.** Each wave added a prompt, a marker, a chip, a label or a line of card
   text. At peak pressure the warning, its owner, the landing spot, friendly effects, numbers and the lower HUD compete;
   lessons lose to unrelated prompts; junction cards make the player assemble the price of a choice. "One editing pass
   short of release."
2. **Actors are too small to act.** Crawlers and Stalkers are 50–60 px tall at 1080p: their striking limbs, elite rank
   and hit reactions exist and cannot be seen. The red lane reads; its owner does not. Brute blades are now the
   brightest thing in a crowd.
3. **The marine is findable but not describable.** One light mass with a halo; the title's dark visor, broad shoulder
   and the dark break behind the gun are gone in play; Cryo turns him blue.
4. **Rooms still keep their story at the frame edge** (Shielding Gate, Breached Loading Bay, Manual Control); some
   conditions need their label to be understood.
5. **Spectacle slipped where onset was traded for residue** (Flashfire, Meltdown, Napalm, Overload).
6. The staged twenty-Brute "heavy" sheets misrepresent real fights and drag the crowd-readability score; the next
   gallery should use native mixed crowds.

Direction for wave 17 — subtract and enlarge, do not add: an editing pass on prompts, cards, HUD and peak-pressure
layers (EDIT17); bigger small families and elites with honest colliders, attack planes that brighten only on the loaded
limb (SCALE17); the marine's visor, shoulder and gun break with the halo only when occluded (MARINE17); onset back in
four abilities and consequence on the struck body (FX17); three accidents inside the fight and self-explaining
conditions (ROOM17); frame-time regression (PERF17).

Keep: the room-1 drill and the death lesson, the rig kits and exclusive augments, the spatial menus and illustrated
cards, the red-only hostile language, Railbreaker / Cryo Nova / Whiteout, twenty distinct room grades, the human traces.

Not measurable: audio; hardware frame rate on a quiet machine (bench at host load 16–21: High p50 16.8 ms, p95 35.8 ms).
