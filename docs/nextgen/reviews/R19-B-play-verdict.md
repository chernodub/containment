## 3. Ten first-hour problems, ranked

1. **A damaging shooter can begin its tell beyond the fighting view.** Of 385 matched warnings that led to enemy hits, 74 began outside the conservative visibility window, including 65 projectile attacks. A bearing alone gives me little basis for timing a sidestep. Keep the camera steady. Give the earliest committed shooter a mouth-shaped marker on the visible side of the edge or dock, show its locked lane and contract the marker to release. The two nearby Crawler fans share the marine’s feet. Guessing which swing ends first wastes the recovery window. Give the first striking foreleg a clean outline through adjacent bodies, and hold the other attacker’s anticipation pose until its turn. ## 5. What the campaigns reveal

**Plasma has a real job.** In Passenger Vault every Standard run fired three bolts with zero rifle contacts on the opening plate. Four runs landed all three on armour. Pyro landed two and also dealt non-weapon plate damage. On Arc 2718’s booked Coolant Plant Warden, two bolts were aimed at its intact plate and both connected. The other 128 rifle rounds in the sector tell us very little about how long that Warden took. Across five Standard traces, plasma hit 365 of 381 distinct enemies with armour-hit receipts, for 768 plasma and 65 rifle plate contacts. A bolt can pierce multiple targets. The median shots aimed at a target through its last recorded armour hit were one to two plasma and zero rifle in each Standard run. I see a quick switch with a purpose. Story Cryo instead completed all twenty with 1,191 rifle rounds, 239 shotgun shots and zero plasma, rockets or pistol shots. Its 25-hit first plate is where the chore is demonstrable. The competent policy explicitly prioritizes visible plate and briefly defers offensive abilities. Human discovery remains unmeasured. ## 6. What the genre leaders still do better, in order

1. **An attack you can read while aiming.** In *Enter the Gungeon*, the next gap gives movement a purpose even under fire. Here the player can read “danger” well before they can read who acts first or where to exit.
2. **Encounters that change what good play looks like.** The Warden asks me to bait a wall. Too many other rooms let me back up and shoot until the next draft. A few more spaces that reward committing, crossing or holding would do more than another enemy skin.
3. **A build that changes what I hope the next room contains.** The best *Hades* builds alter target priority or the risk I want to take. Containment has augments that could do this, but rarely lets me select a destination because I installed one.
4. **A consequence with physical weight.** *Nuclear Throne* makes an ordinary hit worth watching. Here the shotgun and Warden can carry a moment without text. A broken plate, interrupted clutch or suppressed reinforcement often needs a caption.

## 8. Five investments for one month, in order

1. **Give the earliest committed attacker one readable tell.** Carry it from the striking limb to the viewport edge or the visible side of the dock as the body moves. Start with the two Interlock Spitters and the Overload pile. Watch desktop players keep firing while pointing out the next threat and choosing an exit.
2. **Make the interlock and final crossing worth choosing.** Stage the gate during the approach to the pincer, reveal what each shutter will stop and give the backup banks different cover. A player should be able to explain why they took the longer route.
3. **Finish the weapon exchange on the body.** Chip an intact plate under rifle, peel it off under plasma, then let a sidearm or shotgun visibly punish the opening. Watch the next unprompted Brute encounter to see whether the player switches voluntarily.
4. **Give each transformative pair a later fight that rewards its trade.** Start with a crossfire that Glacier can divide and Ice Road can cross. Offer a route preview that lets the player choose that fight because of the build they made.
5. **Give encounter victories a visible and audible consequence.** Focus on the first retreating escort, the collapsing clutch and the Warden’s impact against the wall. Keep the consequence on screen for one aimed follow-up and audition its cue under sustained rifle fire. A successful plan should tell the player when to advance.
