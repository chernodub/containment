## 6. What commercial genre leaders deliver better, in order

1. **A move I can plan before I commit.** In *Enter the Gungeon*, reading an opening means identifying both the gap and my landing. Containment communicates the moment to leave more reliably than the opportunity at the destination.
2. **A body that explains the hit.** In *Hades*, a shield facing me or a weapon planted after a swing gives me a target and a timing cue in one place. Here I often assemble the same information from limb, footprint and HUD.
3. **Encounters that let a purchase change my mind.** Losing a wall in exchange for a runway deserves a room where the runway is excellent and a later one where I miss the wall. The supply screen sells that choice better than the current sequence demonstrates it.
4. **A run whose decisions survive the next door.** A good route choice changes what I prepare for several rooms later. The cargo fork is easy to understand and ends after two rooms.
5. **A tempo that acknowledges a decisive play.** When I shut out a wave or save both pods, a few seconds of owned space can make the success felt more strongly than another reward message. Coolant particularly needs that release.

## 8. Five investments for one month

1. **Give the return shot one unmistakable physical lesson.** Put the Atrium aisle, Brute and two waiting bodies in one sightline. Carry its geometry into Manual Control. The success criterion is a fresh player pointing to the escorts they shut out and choosing the side deliberately on the second encounter.
2. **Spend the first week watching fresh desktop players, then tune the opening and Interlock together.** Watch one unprompted attempt and one retry. Ask where they meant to land, which threat they left alive and whether stepping off sacrificed charge. Use the first repeated misunderstanding to choose between changing geometry, timing or instruction. Keep the existing Story lesson.
3. **Give the Crawler, shutter and reserve one clean moment of contact.** Rework the three worst native angles at normal combat scale. The player should be able to identify the striking claw, the newly exposed gunner and the blocked creature while continuing to aim. Listen to the actual warning and impact mix during these sessions.
4. **Give each exclusive an immediate use and a later cost.** Reuse Transmission for the first demonstration and an existing later hold for the tradeoff. Make both base-kit and upgraded solutions viable, then watch whether players choose a different firing position after the purchase.
5. **Let one route decision and one featured kill persist.** Spend the remaining encounter budget on a cargo outcome visible in Relay and a Coolant guardian outcome that changes its next wave. Those two consequences would do more for a second expedition than another small escort-health adjustment.
