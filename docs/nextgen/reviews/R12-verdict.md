Calibration: "polished prototype approaching a modest commercial indie presentation" (visual); "late prototype / early
commercial alpha with several release-quality interface elements" (play); "polished vertical slice, below solid
commercial indie" (Opus). Would pay US$8–15. Not premium, not AA.

What moved: combat readability, enemy counterplay and feel (+1 each); small gains in effects, motion, cohesion.
What did not: environments, characters. What got worse: pacing/difficulty.

Lead reading — causes, in order:
1. No value hierarchy (Opus): everything in one mid-grey band under even light. → LIGHT13 (done, three rooms), LIGHT14.
2. Difficulty tuned to a bot, not to people: Standard applied 2× incoming damage (Stalker lunge 44/100), deaths in
   1–5 s of chained small hits, the bot heals only below 40 HP, sim and native see different things; Pyro far weaker
   than Breacher; the first rooms can be cleared without learning to dodge. → BAL13.
3. Rooms are "inventory, not composition": broad tiled floor with detail at the edges; one dominant incident per room
   still missing as a large readable shape in most rooms.
4. Overdrives and area powers share one visual climax (concentric rings); Napalm is a stamped disc.
5. Warden and Matron are highlighted family variants, not a visual tier; Stalker and Carrier weakest families.
6. New mechanics exist in rules more than on screen: siege radius undrawn, Matron pods look like crawlers, priority vs
   control-resistance unmarked, Warden stun unreadable, off-screen shooters uncued; fights dissolve into the same cleanup.
7. Marine loses material identity in play (pale patches in hard black outlines, black gun).
8. Reward, shop and route screens are text-dense and repetitive; reward illustrations are one canister form.

Keep (all reviewers): the UI system and rig-select diagrams, per-room colour identity, honest attack geometry and
death receipts, Railbreaker and Overload as the effects benchmark, the four rig vocabularies.
