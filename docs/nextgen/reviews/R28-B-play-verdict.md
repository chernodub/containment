## 6. What the genre leaders deliver better, in order

1. **A readable destination under pressure.** A gap in an *Enter the Gungeon* volley can tell me where to roll and where I can resume shooting. Containment’s first red fan frequently answers only the timing question.
2. **A stronger reason to withhold an attack.** In *Hades*, committing a heavy attack at the wrong moment can forfeit the next opening. Here, sustained fire and continued movement can carry an entire expedition with six exposed hits.
3. **Consequences visible where the player is aiming.** A shutter flying out of a firing line accomplishes this. A distant grille closing while four Crawlers vanish from a future schedule seldom does.
4. **Builds that redirect the next several rooms.** Ice Road and Live Wire have that potential. A staged cast at a receiver establishes what they do. Repeated use under different pressures would establish why the choice matters.
5. **Failure feedback early enough to change the next input.** The charge-death diagram shows an excellent, concrete escape. A player needs that same spatial insight once while the fight is still recoverable.

## 8. Five investments for one month

1. **Make one late dodge worth learning.** Give Manual Control’s short-side landing a shot unavailable from the retreat line. Watch a fresh player identify the landing, take it and fire back. Use that encounter to revise the Gallery and Shaft.
2. **Rebalance consequences across the run.** Put a small, recoverable cost on a missed early lesson. Transfer some of Coolant’s later pressure onto its featured decision so killing the leader buys a recognizable respite.
3. **Make three world changes readable during movement.** Finish the blocked Coolant approach, the saved Matron asset and the sheltered face at the final bank. Each should let the player point to newly usable ground without reading the HUD.
4. **Give the exclusive a second ordinary encounter.** Follow Transmission with one room where the loss of shatter, chain lightning, fixed cover or long aura changes the preferred position. The player should feel what they gave up as well as what they gained.
5. **Put fresh desktop players on mouse, keyboard and headphones.** Observe a crowded dodge, the supply choice and a failure or narrow escape. Ask where they meant to stand, which warning they heard and what they would do on retry. Those answers would settle the most expensive design uncertainties left by this gallery.
