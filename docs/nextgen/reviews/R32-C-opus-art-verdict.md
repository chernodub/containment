# R32-C — art-director second opinion (Claude Opus, 2026-11-28, gallery final27)

Evidence: 44 images (6 screens, 3 sheets, `heavies`, `rigs-in-play`, 2 crowd pairs; from `review480` 4 fights, 3 incidents, 7 abilities, 6 windups, 2 bosses, 1 elite pair, 2 overlaps, 1 condition; 3 native frames, 2 motion sheets). I took fewer fight, incident, ability and condition frames than asked, to stay under the cap.

## 1. Scores

- **First impression: 7.** A confident cel illustration, but the background creatures are grey cut-outs and the centre is an empty light hole.
- **Interface: 7.5.** Disciplined Barlow type; excellent boon cards, diagrams and death debrief. Every screen is the same teal box over blur, and the shop icons are generic.
- **Combat readability: 6.5.** The red telegraphs are unmistakable and the marine stays legible beside heavies (`relay-racks-01`). Peak crowds become dashes, rings and bars (`flashfire-peak`).
- **Environments: 5.5.** Each room has its own hue and the landmarks are good: the reactor, the annulus ring and the relay racks. But the floors are large flat dark plates, and one identical octagonal pool sits in all twenty rooms (`twenty-arena-calm`).
- **Ability effects: 6.** Railbreaker, Cryo Nova and the Arc beams are good. Seismic is a faint tan ring, Meltdown is a soft red bloom, and Whiteout is invisible inside the crowd.
- **Characters and creatures: 6.** The Brute's banding and planted pose read as heavy, and the silhouettes are distinct (`heavies.png`). The Warden is still camouflage patches with floating white plates at native size. The Matron is a pink cloud.
- **Cohesion: 6.5.** Marine, creatures and UI share one inked language; the stamped pool reads as a system, not lighting.

## 2. Tier

This is a polished early-access indie: its menus and marine sit above its floors, bosses and half of its effects. I would pay $15.

## 3. Seven changes, ranked

1. **Motivate the light** (`twenty-arena-calm.png`). Hang a visible fixture over each pool and vary its shape per room (a strip in the freight hold, a reactor cone on the overload floor). Add one coloured practical per wall bank and a cold rim light on characters.
2. **Finish the Warden** (`bosses/ktx2-warden-crew-checkpoint-charge-windup`). Apply the Brute's three value bands. Seat the white plates into the hide with an ink edge, add bone-white spines in a dorsal row, and give it a dark throat and lit eyes.
3. **Give the Matron a skeleton** (`creatures36--matron-release-now-480`). Build a dark carapace frame with readable legs and a head, and let the pink stay only on the distended egg sac.
4. **Quiet the crowd** (`fx37--flashfire-peak-now-480`). Show bars only on damaged or targeted enemies, put rings only on attackers, and add a 1 px dark under-shadow to crawlers so they stop reading as confetti.
5. **Give the weak effects height** (`fx37--seismic-slam-peak`, `whiteout-peak`, `meltdown-peak`). Seismic should throw floor plates up as lit chunks with a dust wall. Whiteout needs a pale cyan frost crust and white breath on the bodies. Meltdown needs a white core and heat ripple, not a red fog.
6. **Add floor detail at play scale** (`rooms36--armory-incident-now-480`, `twenty-native.png`). Break the plates with stencils, cable runs, grime toward the walls and wet patches that catch the pool. Edge-light the wall tops so the room has height.
7. **Bring the title backdrop up to the hero** (`title.png`). Render the background creatures at the foreground finish and fill the hole with a bulkhead silhouette.

## 4. Keep

1. The marine's identity: gold or rig-coloured "07" pauldron, cyan chevron visor, white plates (`rigs-in-play.png`).
2. The red-only telegraph language, with the Railbreaker beam as the effect benchmark.
3. The UI system: the boon vignettes, the before/after diagrams, the death debrief and the route cards.

## 5. Value hierarchy, identity, the decision

The hierarchy is almost complete. The ship is dark and the marine is the brightest value. Creatures sit second as mid slate, tan and lilac. Lit landmarks come third, though the armory incident frame lets the debris pool outshine the marine. The gaps: crowds, where bars and rings outshout him, and bosses finished below the rest of the cast. The marine keeps one identity from title to play, though he is small in play. The decision holding the picture back most is **lighting as a stamp rather than a design**. One unmotivated octagon pool on flat-fill floors does all the work in every room, and the cel forms get no key light, rim light or material response. The frames read as staged diagrams, not as a dying ship.
