Calibration: "budget commercial indie with a release-quality interface", US$10 (Astra visual); "a commercial-indie
combat system; its biggest gap to premium is how often a player can recognize the consequence of a decision before
making the next one" (Astra play); "good early-access indie with a commercial-grade interface, but the actors are
prototype-grade", US$12–15 today and US$20–25 "with the creatures and the marine resolved" (Opus).

**Honest reading: still the plateau.** Waves 17 and 18 (subtract and enlarge, creature repaint, title, cut-outs,
audio) moved Opus up half a point on readability, environments and effects and Astra play up half a point on rigs and
feel; Astra visual went down half a point on first impression, environments and characters. All of it is inside
reviewer noise. The game is a solid indie; it is not premium.

What changed in the diagnosis, and it matters: the complaints about characters are no longer about paint, size or
value. All three verdicts now describe **body design**:
1. **Creatures have colour and no anatomy.** "A pudgy tan plush toy" (Opus); "the Brute's head gets lost in its
   shoulders", "identifiable without looking alive" (Astra). Elites repeat the regular contour; the Warden reads as a
   scaled Brute; the Matron as a pink capsule. The models are rounded SDF masses: no jaw, no hard plates, no leading
   joint that loads before a strike. Repainting cannot fix that.
2. **The marine is two characters.** The title sells a cyan visor behind a deep rim and one enormous yellow shoulder;
   the selector and the game show a black slit, white plates with black patches and a small yellow cap. Named by all
   three reviewers, three rounds running.
3. **The owner of the next hit is still lost in the busiest second**, now specifically for ranged attackers: Arc
   branches, damage numbers and the objective ring cross the shooter (Safety Interlock); the lower weapon tray hides
   bodies approaching from below (Crew Checkpoint, Relay Racks); Emergency lighting loses small bodies while their red
   shots stay bright.
4. **Rooms**: Shielding Gate, Breached Loading Bay, Armory and Freight Hold still keep their incident at or beyond the
   frame edge; floors between the player and the incident are clean tile.
5. **The first clear can be erased by its own tutorial.** On seed 2718 a competent bot killed all four scheduled
   enemies and then died to the bonus practice Brute (five hits, 150 damage, in 29 seconds). The practice target takes
   damage only after a registered evade, so a newcomer's shots do nothing. A real design fault, found by the play
   reviewer from the trace.
6. **Effects end on the floor, not on the victim**; the ordinary rifle hit is still the weakest contact.
7. **A second run repeats its appointments**: four Wardens at the same rooms, salvage at every early junction.

Wave 19 (from this): two bounded Opus modelling passes on the only things three rounds agree cap the picture — one
marine (title silhouette carried into play and selector) and creature anatomy (head, plates, leading joint; Warden and
elite silhouettes) — plus Sol/Astra streams: safe practice drill, lower-HUD and camera so approaching bodies stay
visible, shooter ownership under effects and in darkness, four rooms recomposed with dressed floors, victim reactions,
and a junction that owns a later fight.
