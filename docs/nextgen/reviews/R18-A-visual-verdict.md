## 2. Calibration

**I would pay US$10.** This looks like a budget commercial indie with a particularly good expedition interface. The first choice already meets my premium-indie benchmark. “Ram a pursuer into your barricade” tells me what the rig will feel like in one sentence. My *Hades* benchmark is recognising the attacker by its pose while choosing a way out. My *The Ascent* benchmark is recognising a workplace from the part of it under my feet. Containment meets both in places. A Cryo duel in the Armory and the track bed at Service Shaft Landing are good examples. A crowded Arc fight against the loading-bay wall and the generic centre of Swarm Junction keep the premium claim out of reach. These are visual judgments from stills and sampled poses. Timing, input feel and performance fall outside the evidence. [Rig inspector][inspect] · [Twenty campaign fights][native] · [Twenty arena centres][arena] · [Capture provenance][manifest]

## 3. Ten most damaging defects, ranked

1. **A readable warning can still have an unreadable owner.** At the right wall of Breached Loading Bay, two red shapes, violet energy and the marine occupy one knot. In Dark Atrium I see the wedge before I see the animal at its point. Dodging is possible, choosing an interrupt is slower. Preserve a gap around the attacking head and first attacking limb through the entire wind-up, including where actors overlap. [Native Arc fight][bayfight] · [Dark Atrium and mixed crowds][crowd]
2. **The smaller Brood lose their facing.** The regular Stalker is a violet knot with two feet at quarter size. The Crawler’s pink stripe tells me its family before its jaws tell me which way it will turn. Give each a projecting leading plate and a visible gap on the striking side in the loaded pose. [Stalker pair][stalker] · [Crawler pair][crawler]
3. **The lower approach can disappear.** In the Armory the dock shows part of a Brute through the ammunition segment, but several attackers extend below the picture while the upper arc of the room fills much of the shot. A player needs to see their commitment. Bias framing toward the nearest approaching pack within the marine’s clearance, keeping the opaque backing directly beneath the numerals. [Armory Cryo fight][dock]
4. **The heavy enemies have weight without a strong face.** The Warden’s shoulders swallow its forehead as it prepares to charge. The Matron’s abdomen and hanging joints tell me she breeds before I can find her gaze. Build a lower jaw into each outside contour and give it a distinct pose when charging or releasing a pod. [Warden and Matron][boss] · [Matron opening pods][pod]
5. **Too many rooms tell their story at the edge.** In the incident-facing shots, the breach is cut off above its scars, and most of the gate sits above its guides. Players spend the fight on the aftermath and can miss its cause. Bring one bent gate shoe and one peeled hull lip into the lane-side composition with a light that joins each to its damage trail. [Incident views][inc] · [Native rooms][native]
6. **Fire loses heat between ignition and expiry.** The first flash is a soft, nearly opaque ball. The mature burn has upright tongues around a dark disc, with much of the middle looking safe enough to stand on. Break the flash into rising lobes, then let two or three irregular patches flare inside the scar away from winding-up enemies. [Napalm sequence][rest] · [Resting and native pools][nap]
7. **Two finishers lose their victims.** Railbreaker’s flashes and airborne Brutes sit well above the line and the two 420s. At Arc’s endpoint the eye returns to the polygon floor and the caster. Keep a short directional skid under the rail victims and give the final linked victim a held, fork-shaped contraction. [Rail impact][rail] · [Arc endpoint][arcend]
8. **The weapon carries more motion than the person firing it.** The stop has a knee dip, but rifle, shotgun and rocket samples largely retain the same helmet-to-stock relationship. This makes very different weapons look similarly light. Carry the shotgun kick through the shoulder and planted hip, then hold the recovery silhouette a beat longer. [Brake and shotgun][marine] · [Rocket poses][rocket] · [Coupled shotgun hit][brute]
9. **The three late decisions lean on the same small sign.** Diagnostic, Interlock and Manual Control each put amber text above equipment. “Passengers alive / purge is fatal” is consequential, but the consoles and deck do little to show *whose* lives are at stake. Carry a berth-status motif from Passenger Vault onto the manual station and link it visually to the authorisation control. [Incident views][inc] · [Passenger berths][blast]
10. **Specific consequences recede into secondary UI.** The supply stop names the booked Warden in a thin line. The self-blast receipt occupies the far lower left while the large health number still says 100. Both facts change the next decision. Put the guardian silhouette beside the supply heading and echo the blast source and loss beside the armour meter for one beat. [Booked supply stop][book] · [Self-blast receipt][blast]

## 4. Five things to preserve

1. **The marine is easy to recover by eye.** White plates, one coloured shoulder and a dark gun make a distinct figure even among chained targets. [Native Shielding Gate][arcpeak]
2. **The encounter lessons teach geometry.** Crawler, Spitter and Stalker death cards respectively show retreat, cover and lateral evasion, with a retry action close at hand. [Three debriefs][debrief]
3. **The rigs suggest different ways to stand and fight.** Bulwark divides a lane, ice arrests a front, Flashfire spreads and Arc links bodies. Preserve those shapes when increasing spectacle. [Ability and build frames][families] · [Full builds][od]
4. **The first route choice pays off later.** The player can distinguish plate-breaking from a brood encounter on the cards and see the chosen threat named on arrival at Coolant Plant. [Route choice][routes] · [Native arrivals][rest]
5. **Quiet rooms can tell a story without instructions.** The ruptured pipe, ice at its mouth and reflected spill lead my eye across Coolant Plant in the same direction the accident travelled. [Coolant Plant incident][inc]

## 8. Highest-return investments for one month

1. **Rework the attack poses of the regular Stalker and Brute, then apply the lesson to the bosses.** Give each a clear front edge, a dark gap behind it and one exposed attacking limb. Judge success by whether a viewer can point to the attacking end in the existing mixed Relay crowd. [Mixed crowd][crowd] · [Boss contact][boss]
2. **Keep the enemy that owns the next hit inside the readable part of the frame.** Tune the lower Armory approach and right-wall Arc fight together. Frame the nearest committed attacker, thin overlapping friendly effects locally, and keep ammunition and health readable. [Armory][dock] · [Loading Bay][bayfight]
3. **Finish four representative room centres before spreading more detail across twenty.** Give Gate a broken guide shoe, Freight a clearly torn restraint, Workshop a machine-to-deck infestation and Manual Control an unmistakable passenger connection. Each should identify the room with its stencil covered. [Centres][arena] · [Incidents][inc]
4. **Give each ultimate one memorable consequence on a victim.** Prioritise uneven Napalm flame height and local heat, a rail landing skid and an Arc endpoint pose. Preserve the clear red warning through each. [Napalm pair][nap] · [Rail][rail] · [Arc endpoint][arcend]
5. **Animate one convincing heavy-weapon cycle from feet to muzzle.** Use the shotgun’s coupled hit as the reference for a planted brake, torso compression and recovery. Carry that weight into the rig portrait so the selector promises the same person seen in play. [Coupled shotgun hit][brute] · [Marine poses][marine] · [Rig inspector][inspect]
