## 2. Calibration

**I would pay US$8 on the visual pitch.** This looks like a small commercial indie with a finished interface and uneven combat art. Relay Racks justifies the purchase in four pictures. I can point to the slatted shutter, see a charge lined up through it, and find the scattered bars afterward. The Warden itself is the disappointment. Its white lamp is easier to understand than the shoulder driving the blow. Against *Hades*, I miss being able to anticipate an attack from the distribution of weight in the body. Against *The Ascent*, I miss being able to identify a room's purpose after its main machine leaves the frame. The motion score covers the poses visible in the strips. ## 3. Ten defects, ranked

1. **The Warden has a headlight where it needs a face.** The white disk gives excellent direction, but the surrounding pale plate ends against an almost uninterrupted green mass. At wall stun I can find the lamp sooner than the vulnerable animal. Recess the light between two jaw plates, expose a dark mouth opening and separate the loaded shoulder from the planted limb. [Native crop][warden] · [Charge through recovery][cycle]
2. **The Brute's release and recovery say nearly the same thing.** Its fists overlap the face and one another. The gold arcs are doing the work of telling me when to retaliate. Extend one fist beyond the head at impact, plant it wide, then drop the skull below that elbow for recovery. Keep the arcs as reinforcement. [Five poses][brute] · [Exposed in play][exposed]
3. **Too many rooms tell their story at the edge.** The Freight crate, Diagnostic bed and Transmission feed leave broad central lanes identified mostly by stencils. The camera loses the room's identity precisely when a player starts moving. Carry one physical consequence of each accident through the playable lane, such as a crate skid, a snapped bed rail or a burned cable channel. [Incident sheet][inc] · [Greyscale centres][grey] · [Campaign][native]
4. **The final bank choice has no single readable shelter.** The preview puts “Cover” across the scene from the player. In the campaign, cyan arrows bend away while gold arrows run down the deck and red circles occupy the departure point. Choosing a bank entails mentally joining three marks. Put the cover glyph on the first protected foothold and show the preview shot stopping against its lip. [Preview][fork] · [Live choice][bank]
5. **Rig identity disappears into the shared firing stance.** In the selector the Breacher's upright shield defines the near arm. From the game camera its broad face collapses to a small plate beside the white suit and long gun. Give the Breacher a braced, shield-leading shoulder and let the camera see the shield's outside edge throughout the ram. [Selector][selector] · [Fight pair][marine] · [Four rigs][rigs]
6. **Small enemies lose their attack end in a pack.** The salmon stripe locates the Crawler and the blue blade locates the Stalker, but when several surround the marine their heads and feet merge into the floor or one another. Widen the lit leading claw on the Crawler and give the waiting Stalker a gap between blade and torso that survives reduction. [Ordered crowd][crowd] · [Mixed crowds][mixed] · [Stalker poses][stalker]
7. **Whiteout spends too much contrast on its rim.** The deck is visible through the field and the hostile red sector survives. The brightest part, however, is a continuous scalloped edge running out of the picture, while ice on individual targets is comparatively faint. Break the rim around obstacles and move some of that contrast to ice accumulating on struck joints. [Native Overdrive][ice] · [Dense aftermath][white]
8. **A successful reserve cut has a better sentence than image.** “3 Stalkers cut off” explains the result, but the two dark husks sit on warm halos like objects awaiting collection. The dim checkmarks are detached from them. Collapse the luminous opening into the husk and leave a bright split edge pointing to the sealed entry. [Workshop cancellation][reserve]
9. **The small shop cards hide the reason to buy.** Nine similarly weighted boxes fit on screen, yet the free doctrine, healing and paid upgrades compete at the same scale. A player can find the exit faster than the value of the next purchase. Give the free selection its own row and use the largest text in each paid card for the amount gained and the price. [Supply comparison][shop]
10. **The title's foreground threat looks composited.** The visor, shield bevels and gun barrels have hard, specific highlights. Across from them the animal has a soft orange rim and several black shapes that merge with the broken hatch. Light one jaw plane and an overlapping claw from the hatch, using the same edge sharpness as the gun. [Native title][title] · [Small desktop][smalltitle]

## 4. Five things to preserve

1. **One filled warning earns the eye.** The red sector stays legible under a Cryo Overdrive and among waiting enemies. Preserve the distinction between its filled area and queued outlines. [Cryo][ice] · [Ordered crowd][crowd]
2. **Railbreaker has a visible consequence.** The straight rails, damage count and bodies displaced above the line describe one action from muzzle to victim. [Native Railbreaker][rail]
3. **The death card gives me a reason to retry.** Naming the fatal attack, drawing its footprint and showing the room's damage sources answer different questions on one screen. [Debriefs][death]
4. **The Matron communicates a rule through anatomy.** The raised sac, paired clusters and open pink rims make reproduction understandable before I read a prompt. [Brood cycle][matron]
5. **The ending gives the outcome priority.** The lit dish and “All aboard lost / New Earth warned” land before the invitation to inspect statistics. Keep that order. [Ending][ending]

## 8. Five investments for one month

1. **Finish one heavy from calm through impact.** Start with the Warden's jaw, shoulder and planted foot, then animate charge, collision and slump against the Relay shutter. Reuse the resulting joint and light treatment on the Brute. This improves the boss reveal, common fights and route art together. [Warden][warden] · [Relay][relay] · [Route][route]
2. **Put three room stories under the player's feet.** Give Freight a torn restraint and skid, Diagnostic a visibly occupied or abandoned patient cradle, and Transmission a severed feed with a local light source. Compose each from the position where the fight actually happens. [Incident sheet][inc] · [Campaign sheet][native]
3. **Make one mixed pack readable from posture alone.** Animate a Crawler turning its leading claw, a Stalker unfolding its blade, and a Brute planting after the blow. Judge them beside the marine at room scale, then restore the existing red warning over those poses. [Crowd][crowd] · [Brute][brute] · [Stalker][stalker]
4. **Give tactical success a physical location.** Spend the encounter-art pass on the first sheltered step, the shutter fragments and the spent breach. A player should be able to point to where the shot stopped, where the boss hit and where reinforcements were cut off. [Bank][bank] · [Relay][relay] · [Workshop][reserve]
5. **Carry one suit and its hit response through the whole game.** Use Breacher as the standard. Match the shield face and bracing angle from title to room, then drive the leading shoulder back as Railbreaker sends its victims away. That single action can sell both the character and the weapon.
