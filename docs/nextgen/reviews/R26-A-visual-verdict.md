## 2. Calibration

**I would pay US$10 on the visual pitch.** This looks like a small commercial indie with a finished front end and uneven combat art. The strongest four pictures are the Cryo run through Service Shaft Landing. The same winch establishes the room, divides the fight and is still standing when the sector clears. What holds it below the best premium indies is visible in the pressure frame. I can identify the winch more confidently than the anatomy of the large creature being thrown beside it. Against *Hades*, I miss the weight shift that lets me anticipate the blow from the body alone. Against *The Ascent*, I miss evidence that people used the ship before the fight arrived. The motion judgment is about the pictured poses and their sequence. [Shaft storyboard][shaft] · [Brute poses][pose] · [Room incidents][incident]

## 3. Ten defects, ranked

1. **The Warden's head still reads as a work light.** The lower jaw now cups the white patch, but the light consumes the opening, the pale brow has no visible orbit, and the neck disappears behind a square shoulder. The boss has a direction and no expression. Narrow the luminous aperture, expose one cheek hinge and carry a dark neck crease back to the leading shoulder. [Warden close-up][jaw] · [Lineup][heavy]
2. **The common Brute looks assembled from pads.** Its slotted bone mask is expressive enough to find, yet the near arm ends in a rounded mitt and the opposite support tapers to a peg. Repetition in a pack magnifies the toy quality. Model the elbow, wrist and two weight-bearing knuckles as one articulated chain, then fit fewer plates around it. [Heavy lineup][heavy] · [Walk][walk] · [Campaign fights][native]
3. **Whiteout hides the deck and camouflages the frozen.** In the dense aftermath I can count the big outlined Brutes, but small blue-gray enemies vanish among pale cells and health bars. This costs me the next target and the ground underfoot. Keep opaque snow in broken leeward banks, open channels of bare deck around feet, and put the sharpest frost highlights on struck joints. [Peak comparison][snowpair] · [Current aftermath][snow]
4. **The Brute's arm gives an ambiguous end to the strike.** The loaded diagonal opens into a broad forward punch, then the same fist occupies almost the same space as the head sinks away. The gold recovery marks have to announce the opening. Plant the striking fist at contact, let the shoulder travel past it, then pull the elbow clear of the skull in recovery. [Attack strip][pose] · [Recovery in play][exposed]
5. **Several room accidents end at a tidy pile.** The cold-pipe break, tipped freight and bent transmission feed establish their source. Beyond them the same clean panels resume, so it is hard to tell which room I am fighting through. Follow the forces across two or three panels with refrozen runoff, restraint gouges or a burned cable trench. [Incidents][incident] · [Centres][centre] · [Campaign fights][native]
6. **The small attack ends disappear precisely at contact.** The Crawler's salmon back stays visible against the red sector while the hooks touch the outline of the marine's boots. The fan tells me where to go but the body teaches me little for the next encounter. Splay one leading hook outside the boot silhouette and give it a short pale underside during load. [Boots comparison][boots] · [Ordered crowd][pack]
7. **Pyro's flames can take credit for their own hits.** In the dense overlap the bright uninterrupted orange tongues and the orange victims share edges. Large bodies survive, but I have to search the flames for the small ones. Break the tongues where they cross silhouettes and let the brightest ember travel briefly with the struck flank. [Pyro overlap][fire] · [Native Overdrive][melt]
8. **The last bank gives a direction without a convincing refuge.** Gold arrows pass through an incoming red lane. The staged sightline gives me a trajectory, but no unmistakable first protected foothold. Put a lit cover lip at that first step and terminate the preview tracer visibly on its outside face. [Preview][fork] · [Campaign choice][bank]
9. **Rig selection promises four stances and combat delivers one.** The selector's Breacher leads with an upright shield. In play the shield becomes a small plate behind the head while all four suits lead with much the same gun and bent knees. Rotate the shield outboard and brace the front leg for the Breacher's approach. [Selector, calm and fight crops][rigs]
10. **The death diagram is too small and can illustrate a different exchange.** In the Relay example the fatal hit is a Spitter shot, while the only illustrated attack is a Brute sweep that did the most damage. Both labels are accurate. A player looking for the fatal sightline gets a melee lesson. Put the fatal footprint next to the fatal hit and reserve the source chart for cumulative loss. [Relay death card][death] · [Crawler comparison][deathpair]

## 8. Highest-return investments for one month

1. **Build one Warden from the jaw backward.** Give the mouth an opening, the skull a hinge and the neck a visible load path into a planted shoulder. Pose it at charge, collision and recovery from the game camera before propagating the joints to the common Brute. This would improve the most exposed weak asset in fights and route art alike. [Jaw][jaw] · [Charge poses][wpose] · [Route cards][routes]
2. **Rework the area effects around what happens to the victim.** Start with Whiteout in Service Shaft. Preserve the red fan, open bare deck beneath small attackers and carry the brightest frost onto joints. Apply the same contact principle to Flashfire and Meltdown. A player should be able to point to the hit after the flash. [Shaft pressure][shaft] · [Whiteout][snow] · [Pyro overlap][fire]
3. **Animate the three frames a player can act on.** Give the Brute a visibly planted recovery, the Crawler a hook outside the marine's boot, and the Breacher a shield-leading stance. These specific silhouettes would teach attack, opening and response across ordinary fights. [Brute strip][pose] · [Boots][boots] · [Rig lineup][rigs]
4. **Finish three accident trails at fighting scale.** Carry a restraint scar across Freight, an ice flow from the pipe in Coolant, and a scorched feed through Transmission. Use the shaft winch as the standard for how a room stays recognizable from arrival to clearance. [Incidents][incident] · [Native fights][native] · [Shaft sequence][shaft]
5. **Illustrate the decisive square of floor.** Show where a shot stops at the final bank, where a reserve breach has collapsed, and where the fatal attack met the marine on the debrief. Each can replace several small marks or lines of copy with one teachable image. [Bank][bank] · [Reserve cut][cut] · [Death card][death]
