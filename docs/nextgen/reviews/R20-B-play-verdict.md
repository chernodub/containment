Make the earliest impact readable on the attacking limb through neighbouring bodies, then transfer that emphasis at strike. The player should be able to aim at the first recovery. ## 6. Where the genre leaders pull ahead

1. **An escape the eye can trace while firing.** In *Enter the Gungeon* the gap between threats is a target for the player’s movement. Containment often communicates danger earlier than it communicates the safe step.
2. **Rooms that change which risk is worth taking.** The Warden’s wall and Interlock’s shutter do this. Repeated priority, crossfire and flank beats too often let the marine solve a new room from its perimeter.
3. **A build that changes the destination the player wants.** The strongest *Hades* run decisions change the reward a player seeks next. Containment’s exclusive augments can support that depth, but a player with Ice Road and one with Shard Harvest can make much the same route and supply decisions.
4. **Physical consequences that teach the follow-up.** The shotgun catch and the Warden’s slump have this quality. A cancelled clutch, a section of missing armour or the last victim of a chain should be as easy to recognise during ordinary fire.

## 8. Five investments for one month

1. **Finish the first-attacker read.** Start with Swarm’s half-token pair, Diagnostic’s consecutive lunges and the dock-edge Spitter. Give the imminent limb and escape edge priority through other effects. Watch desktop players point to the next attacker while they continue firing.
2. **Give the middle and final acts distinct movement decisions.** Pair an early advance-to-interrupt room with a later hold-and-cross room. At the finale, show the safe approach and the exposed shortcut together before failure. A hurt marine should have a reason to take the long way.
3. **Teach each rig’s defensive trade in one live exchange.** Start with Cryo’s choice to keep Glacier, shatter or move. Follow with a room where the player can deliberately choose between the counters. Use the same approach for Bulwark’s moving front, Pyro’s fuse and Arc’s tether.
4. **Finish the armour and explosive lessons on the deck.** Spend the art and audio time on the first chipped panel, the exposed shoulder, the follow-up hit and the edge of the marine’s own blast. Watch for an unprompted weapon switch or safer second rocket.
5. **Carry one build decision through the expedition.** Put the booked guardian and preparation purchase on one card, offer a later room whose geometry rewards the chosen augment, and show the actual outcome at debrief. A player should remember why they spent the credits.
