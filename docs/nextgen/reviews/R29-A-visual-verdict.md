## 2. What I would pay

**US$10 on the visual evidence.** This looks like a small commercial action roguelike with a particularly good decision interface. The gap to premium indie or AA is visible in ordinary play. In *Hades* I can remember a heavy by how it gathers itself for a blow. Here I remember the red fan more readily than the arm that made it. Against *The Ascent*, the telling comparison is a few metres of floor with the landmark offscreen. Containment’s reactor sells the finale on arrival. At peak pressure on the far bank, the floor could belong to another encounter. These scores cover photographed poses and sequences. They do not measure timing, responsiveness or performance. ## 3. Ten defects, ranked

1. **The common heavy still walks too lightly.** The shoulder and mask travel, but the near hand keeps much the same spread through much of the stride. Repetition across a pack makes the animal look suspended. Pin one wrist for the support phase, flatten the loaded digits, pass the shoulder over them and delay the opposite heel lift. [Stride][sheet-w] · [Silhouettes][sheet-z]
2. **Crawler visibility changes with heading.** From above, its separated straw bands say “shell.” End-on, the bands nearly vanish, and its body becomes another dark notch in the deck. This costs a threat at contact. Wrap a short light plane down each forward shoulder and open the gap between the forelegs. Keep the dark back. [Pale floor, native crop][detail-p] · [Dark floor][detail-c] · [Boots][boot]
3. **Too much room identity lives outside the fighting lane.** Ship hardware has specific functions, but long strips of deck share the same grate, stripes and scratches. A player can lose their sense of progress between sectors. Carry one physically plausible trace from each landmark through both banks, starting with offset berth runners in Passenger and a ruptured conductor in Transmission. [Centres][sheet-a] · [Native fights][sheet-r] · [Transmission pair][tc]
4. **Warnings explain space better than bodies explain ownership.** In the waiting pack, outlines cross the marine and each other. At the Interlock edge the targeting line is easier to find than the shooter. Give the imminent attacker a brief visible accent on its striking limb or muzzle, and terminate its leading line at that point. Keep the single filled footprint. [Feint][sheet-f] · [Edge shot][edge] · [Ordered pack][sheet-o]
5. **The Warden’s exposed mouth takes too long to find.** The recoil, overhead brackets and red wedges register before the little opening on the far side of the boss. That hides the reward for baiting a charge. Turn the muzzle into three-quarter view during the held recoil and light the inner lower jaw against a dark throat. [Wall stun][sheet-x] · [Charge load][sheet-y]
6. **The most expensive powers have inconsistent visual reach.** Meltdown’s bright centre suggests a smaller hit than its faint perimeter. Whiteout’s banks compete with the feet of small blue enemies. Put the strongest heat accent on a front that reaches the last affected victim, and thin the cold facets immediately behind small bodies. [Heat peak][heat] · [Heat decay][fade] · [Whiteout in a pack][snow]
7. **The final refuge is difficult to recognize on approach.** The thin edge, tracer, reactor cracks and charging prompt meet at the player. I understand the shot before I understand what will stop it. Give the barrier a continuous shooter-facing vertical plate, a visible end cap and an impact spark on the outside corner. [Preview][bank] · [Campaign impact][hit]
8. **Some incident lights wash out the action they frame.** At Coolant the boots cross a pale fitting in a pale pool. At Swarm Junction the large greenish spill draws my eye beyond the pack. Break the first pool behind the footing and concentrate the second under the ruptured pipe. [Arena centres][sheet-a] · [Swarm crowd][detail-c] · [Greyscale kiting][sheet-k]
9. **The title has clean figures but unresolved staging.** The marine’s shield and visor look finished at full size. Opposite them, the Warden’s paws meet a narrow rail and the two background creatures appear suspended against the soft opening. Put one paw over a broken flange, give the two foreground figures a common breach light, and tuck one background animal partly behind the hull. [Full title][sheet-t]
10. **Death cards still ask the player to decode a legend.** The safer step is visible. In the Crawler example I need the small key to work out which outlined helmet marks the past. Use a hollow, receding earlier pose and a solid impact pose, then attach the escape arrow directly to the latter. [Crawler diagram][dp] · [Other fatal hits][sheet-u]

## 4. Five things to preserve

1. **One filled imminent warning.** The red arc survives the frozen floor and gives a direction out of a mixed pack. [Cryo Overdrive][ice] · [Ordered pack][sheet-o]
2. **The marine’s visor, pale legs and local light.** I can reacquire the operator on the purple workshop deck and at the dark reactor bank. [Campaign fights][sheet-r] · [Rigs][sheet-h]
3. **The Matron’s lifted abdomen and opening pods.** A player can infer what is being laid and what is about to hatch from the silhouettes alone. [Pod stages][sheet-p]
4. **The illustrated choices.** The first junction shows two different adversaries, and the boon cards show what a weapon will do. That makes a build decision intelligible before reading the numbers. [First junction and boons][sheet-j]
5. **The ending’s order of attention.** The illuminated dish, the cost to the ship and the warning received on New Earth land before the invitation to inspect statistics. [Ending, lower left][sheet-e]

## 8. Five investments for one month, in order

1. **Animate one heavy convincingly from the floor up.** Put an animator and creature artist on the common Brute’s planted stride, then reuse the contact mechanics on the elite and turn the Warden’s open jaw toward camera in recoil. This improves ordinary encounters as well as the boss. [Stride][sheet-w] · [Stun][sheet-x]
2. **Give small attackers an identifiable front, side and rear.** Start with the Crawler’s shoulder planes and Stalker’s planted blade. Grade each against the darkest grate, the palest incident light and the marine’s legs. Keep the successful dorsal markings. [Dark pack][detail-c] · [Pale pack][detail-p] · [Contact][boot]
3. **Finish four fighting lanes end to end.** Give Passenger a displaced runner, Checkpoint a buckled barrier track, Transmission a continuous broken conductor and Overload a raised feeder terminating in real cover. Judge them from the far bank with the landmark cropped. [Passenger][pc] · [Checkpoint][cc] · [Transmission][tc] · [Finale][story]
4. **Spend the effects budget on the last body hit.** Carry a short burst of heat or frost onto the recoiling limb and clear a little deck contrast behind it. Use Railbreaker’s visible displacement as the benchmark for Meltdown and Whiteout. [Railbreaker][rail] · [Burn][fire] · [Heat][fade] · [Cold][snow]
5. **Finish the title as one illustration.** Preserve “Wake up,” the shield crop and the readable Warden silhouette. Integrate their footing, overlaps and reflected light so the image holds together at store-page size and on a full desktop. [Full title][sheet-t] · [Reduced pair][tp]
