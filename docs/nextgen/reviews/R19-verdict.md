Calibration: US$10 (Astra visual); "Solid commercial indie. The largest gap to premium is whether I can identify the
attacker that deserves my next input while I am still firing" (Astra play); "a polished early-access indie: AA-grade
menus around gameplay art at upper-prototype level", US$12–15 (Opus).

**Honest reading: a plateau, at a higher level than R15's.** Wave 22 moved nothing beyond half a point and the
moves cancel. Across five rounds the Astra reviewers have drifted up (interface 7.5 → 8, readability 6.5 → 7.5,
first impression 7.0 → 7.5, play floor 6.5 → 7); the Opus reviewer has not moved at all outside noise and still
prices the game at US$12–15. Interface and rig design are at commercial quality. The picture of the game in play
(creatures, room centres, effects) sits at 5.5–6.5 and has resisted five waves of work on exactly those things.

What the three verdicts agree on:
1. **Who owns the next hit** — first in both Astra verdicts for the fifth round. Now with a number: of 385 warnings
   that led to enemy hits, 74 began outside the visible window (65 of them projectile attacks). Overlapping melee
   swipes "have distinct clocks but look like one red patch". The camera is not the answer (CAM22 was measured and
   rejected; the play reviewer agrees it should stay steady).
2. **Attack poses read from the warning, not from the body**, even in the new `windups/` frames: "the small
   predators have stronger backs than faces"; boss vulnerability "is carried by symbols".
3. **Room centres and materials**: Freight's painted value steps were noticed, but "large props still share one
   material"; several centres still lose the cause of their accident; Opus still calls the centres empty plates.
4. **New work that did not land**: the interlock flank controls were walked past in all six campaigns; the final
   crossing's two banks ask the same thing; the re-posed rig portraits read as "leaning back… awkward, falling"
   (both visual reviewers); Meltdown is "a flat orange decal" to Opus and Pyro's sustained fire is "cool in the
   middle" to Astra; Opus still sees soft title creatures and stray strokes.
5. **Smaller**: full-screen teal objective rings compete with red; the HUD can give two orders at once; ignoring the
   plasma prompt makes a plated enemy a dull firing exercise; consequences arrive as small annotations.

The reviewers contradict each other and themselves on creature surfaces (R18-C credited the angular shells and
plates; R19-C calls the same creatures "noisy, mottled"), so creature surface work is no longer steered by scores.

Wave 23 (from this): make the fight itself readable by rule rather than by effect — an attack-token rule that caps
simultaneous committed attackers, an edge tell for off-screen shooters, one objective at a time; fix what did not
land (interlock staging, crossing choice, portraits, utility rings, Meltdown); one bounded Opus pass on loaded poses
judged only in `windups/`.
