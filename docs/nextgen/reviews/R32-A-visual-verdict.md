## 2. Tier and price

**I would pay US$9.99.** This looks like a small commercial indie with a finished front end. I would buy it on the strength of the shield-bearing marine and the death card that shows me a better move. Against *Hades*, the gap is the instant when an enemy takes weight or a hit. Against *Ruiner*, it is knowing where I am from the strip of floor I am actually fighting on. Both gaps recur throughout a run. ## 3. Ten most damaging defects, ranked

1. **Small enemies drop out of the headcount.** In Transmission I find both lavender Carriers and the large Brutes immediately. Several waiting scouts resolve as flecks until I trace their legs. At the boots, the Crawler’s hook is visible in the crop but merges with the shield at playing size. This changes target priority. Give the Crawler daylight inside its hook, the Spitter a blunt sac end and the Stalker a persistent gap between its blades. Check those three shapes together in greyscale. [Dark pack][crowd] · [Grey pack][grey] · [Boot strike][strike]
2. **Expensive powers lose their victim.** Nova’s rim wins my first glance. Slam’s pale victim can leave the top of the frame, and Meltdown’s caster carries the brightest sustained orange. I understand their radius sooner than their force. Pick an on-screen victim, turn its struck flank across the impulse and keep one lit edge tied to that flank through recovery. Overload already shows where the energy went. [Nova][nova] · [Slam][slam] · [Meltdown][melt] · [Overload][arc]
3. **Too many fighting lanes lose the room’s identity.** The snapped mechanisms tell good stories in the incident views, but under the player a sequence of clipped light pools turns them into exhibits on similar decks. Let Passenger’s berth sockets, Checkpoint’s pivot, Transmission’s cables and Overload’s torn bus continue through the retreat lanes, with distinct shapes where each breaks. [Centres][ctr] · [Campaign fights][nat] · [Checkpoint lane][pivot]
4. **The Brute carries more weight than its joints appear to support.** The palm stays low through the transfer, but the pointed head, stacked back and cylindrical elbow still look like separate rigid parts in the close lineup. Give the loaded shoulder a sliding overlap over the upper arm, flatten the bearing palm and delay the head a little as the shoulder rises. Players see this animal repeatedly. [Heavy lineup][heavy] · [Stride][walk] · [Planted hand][plant]
5. **The hero marker restores position but confuses depth.** During Warden stun the visor and gold shoulder are findable inside the brown chest. The slate torso looks printed on the boss, and I cannot place both boots. Keep the facing cue, darken only the contact patch behind it and give the last visible boot a short, grounded contour. [Wall stun pair][stun]
6. **The final path is clearer than its destination.** Before the preview volley I can trace the pale tread around the corner. I would run along it, but I would still hesitate over the exact square to hold. In Arc’s native near-bank shot the hero and bright tread are on different sides of the incoming line. Widen the terminal bay by a boot on either side and show the preview shot breaking on its outer lip while the interior stays quiet. [Fork pair][fork] · [Near bank][bank] · [Breacher finale][story]
7. **Carrier colour now costs anatomy.** Its broad lavender back helps me count the family, but in the raised and recovered poses the sac loses the divisions that explained how it bent. Restore two or three large overlapping ribs on the turning side and keep the dorsal colour continuous. [Carrier poses][carrier] · [Matron and pods][boss]
8. **The edge shooter competes with interface marks.** The long red line tells me the direction, then leads into a cropped animal beside the hotbar and status feedback. That slows the decision to approach or break sight. Set the warning pin one icon-width inside the playing field and give it a clear margin from the bottom bar. [Interlock edge][edge]
9. **The grille hit does not show what it bought.** In the native closed frame the Brute and recovery brackets are easy to see. The escorts held behind the bars are hard to pick out, so the reward looks like a single stagger. Backlight two gaps behind the closing bar and let a queued silhouette stop in each. [Native grille][grille] · [Staged comparison][grillepair]
10. **Decision text loses to headings and thumbnails.** The route order now names a later threat, but the illustration gets a large panel and the consequence gets a narrow line. In the shop, similarly sized cells give price and benefit similar emphasis. Give each offer one prominent outcome line and align its cost with the action. The infirmary’s 42-to-77 HP forecast is a good model. [Order pair][order] · [Shop][s6] · [Forecast][forecast]

## 8. Five investments for one month

1. **Finish the small enemies at actual fighting size.** Have the creature artist and animator work on the same three end-on silhouettes, then stage one Crawler striking across each rig’s near boot. The deliverable is a pack whose waiting families and imminent attacker can both be identified. [Dark pack][crowd] · [Strike][strike]
2. **Rebuild three signature hits around their victims.** Start with Nova, Slam and Meltdown. Keep the victim in frame, carry a directional impulse into its recovery and let the deck respond at that animal’s feet. Use Overload’s visible endpoint as the reference. [Nova][nova] · [Slam][slam] · [Meltdown][melt] · [Overload][arc]
3. **Put room identity and safety under the player’s feet.** Finish four outer fighting lanes and the final sheltered bay. Give each one a mechanical function visible after both the landmark and the text leave frame. [Lane pair][pivot] · [Final fork][fork] · [Native finale][story]
4. **Finish one heavy from skin to stride.** Use the improved Brute contact as the foundation. Refine shoulder compression, head lag, wrist shape and actor overlap on the ordinary animal, then transfer the result to elite and Warden. [Stride][walk] · [Lineup][heavy] · [Stun][stun]
5. **Give the opening minute the same authorship as the cover.** Lead the eye from one visibly breached pod to a creature in profile and then to the marine’s first usable space. The empty deck can build suspense if the player can see what crossed it. [Opening][s1] · [First sector’s campaign panel][nat] · [Capsule][cap]
