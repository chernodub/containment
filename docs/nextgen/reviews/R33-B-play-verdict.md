## 2. Calibration

**I would pay US$9.99 for Containment as a compact desktop action campaign.** The strongest reason to replay is the prospect of solving a familiar room with a different rig. The biggest gap to premium is the moment immediately after contact. I can see where danger was and still lose the creature that caused it, the target my upgrade helped, or the ground I just won. That costs the player their next decision. This is a design judgment from the desktop gallery, native input traces and source at `cbdcffd7`. Sound design is assessed for cue coverage only. ## 6. What premium genre leaders deliver better

1. **A threat I can recognize during the follow-up shot.** In *Hades*, a distinct recovery pose often tells me where to spend the next attack. Containment’s best equivalent is the wall-stunned Warden. Its ordinary Crawler needs that clarity more often.
2. **A build I could recognize without seeing its name.** *Enter the Gungeon* makes a changed firing pattern evident while I am aiming. Glacier’s manual shatter reaches that standard. Seeing orange bodies in Transmission tells me much less about Ember Relay.
3. **A second run with a different memory of the same room.** I would remember moving into the shutter lane to catch the loader. I would struggle to recall which Shielding entrance opened or how that affected my stance.
4. **A climax readable from the character’s position.** The final bank crossing supplies a destination and stakes. Its last safe standing spot deserves the same visual priority as the charge meter.

## 8. Five investments for one month

1. **Finish the Crawler’s strike and recovery in a real mixed pack.** Use the Arc Swarm encounter and Cryo’s fatal approach. After one dodge, a fresh desktop player should be able to point to the animal they would shoot and say why.
2. **Make the first console crossing teach the firing position.** Reuse Atrium’s door motion, let the first escort reveal the notch, and show the remaining bodies react to the return shot. Watch players who deliberately stay behind cover as well as those who step out.
3. **Give each exclusive a two-encounter payoff.** In Transmission, make its distinctive action free a route or remove a threat. In the next hold, expose what the player surrendered. Start with Pyro’s secondary ignition and Arc’s moving pylon.
4. **Connect early crossfire to late interception.** Put the same recognizable shooter crossing a gap in the opening and in the late escort exchange. In the opening, allow time to see the interrupt reverse the door. Later, make the player choose between that shot and maintaining a hold.
5. **Spend the final encounter-design week on ground the player can claim.** Keep the retreating guardian escort within the fighting view, give Shielding’s two entrances opposite effects on one shelter, and finish the bank tread at a visible standing spot. Each change makes the player’s next position the reward.
