## 1. Scores

**Containment has substantial game design underneath a presentable interface. It has not yet demonstrated the reliable combat readability, learning curve or sustained payoff of a solid commercial release.**

I read the reports, relevant source and all five native campaign traces. I did not run the game, builds or captures. These are evidence-based design judgments, not claims of hands-on play, perceived audio quality, input latency or hardware performance. Scores were written before opening R11; `DEFECTS.md` was read last. Only this verdict file was written.

## 2. Calibration

**This is a substantial late prototype / early commercial alpha with several release-quality interface elements.** It is below the 7/10 solid-indie tier overall and materially below the premium ambition in `D/00-VISION.md`. Its single biggest obstacle is **an unreliable learning loop: the player must consistently recognize danger, understand the available answer, execute it and recognize the reward**. The rules increasingly support that sequence; the visible fight and tuning do not consistently support it together. Against the design standards associated with *Hades*, *Enter the Gungeon* and *Nuclear Throne*, the missing ingredient is not another catalog of content. It is confidence that repeated failure teaches something specific and that mastery makes ordinary exchanges satisfying.

## 3. The ten most damaging first-hour problems, ranked

1. **Small-enemy damage can erase the player before the lesson becomes useful.** Standard Stalker lunges deal **44 damage**; Pyro takes three at **47.883, 51.183 and 53.517 seconds**, losing its remaining 129.84 vitality in **5.63 seconds**. Cryo later loses **100 health in 3.27 seconds** to three Crawlers and one Stalker; Arc loses its remaining **70 in 1.10 seconds** to two Stalker hits. Those are authoritative receipts, not inferred proximity. A nominal warning is insufficient if recognition and escape must already be fluent. **Tomorrow:** introduce one isolated Stalker before the room-three pair, force a clear reveal during commitment/recovery, and soften early lunge damage or lengthen the first few commitments before restoring full pressure. Evidence: `G/play/{pyro,cryo,arc}/{trace.ndjson,report.json}`; `S/game/enemies/meleeAttacks.ts`; `S/game/difficulty.ts`.

2. **The view does not consistently present the whole immediate decision.** Native Cryo Armory040 shows a readable close Crawler fan, but the frost field, lower enemies, projectile and action bar compete within the same area. In the Armory sheets, threats repeatedly sit on or beyond the lower boundary while usable floor occupies much of the view. ENC12's measured opposed Spitters can have a **168°** clear-shot separation while both fail the HUD-safe visibility predicate; its best simultaneously safe pair is only **3.6°** apart late in cleanup. This undermines the intended “break one firing lane” choice. **Tomorrow:** prioritize the currently committed attacker and its escape space in framing, reduce friendly-effect detail around that exchange, and give off-screen shooters a clear source/direction cue before their shot arrives. Evidence: `G/native-heavy/cryo-play-r6-armory-040.png` and its reduction; `G/play/cryo/storyboard-armory.png`; `D/ENC12-REPORT.md`, `CAM12-REPORT.md`.

3. **The tutorial can be completed without learning its intended exchange.** Every supplied first room clears in **7.05–7.93 seconds**, with zero incoming damage and no dash use. Breacher's sheet shows an early Slam, an empty interval, then a quick pack clearance. That is a welcome gentle start, but it demonstrates successful damage output more than recognition–evade–punish. The next rooms introduce crossfire and flanking before a comparable Stalker lesson exists. **Tomorrow:** make a brief optional practice encounter reward one observed dodge and one recovery punish, then one rig combination; preserve a fast skip for returning players. Keep the persistent contextual prompts. Evidence: `G/play/*/report.json`, `G/play/breacher/storyboard-awakening-bay.png`; `S/ui/onboarding.ts`; `S/game/waves/authoredEncounters.ts`.

4. **Difficulty is calibrated to a model that does not see the shipped fight in the same way.** The reported **42.5% Standard** completion combines Breacher **13/20** with Pyro **5/20**; Story ranges from **16/20** Breacher to **7/20** Pyro. The simulation uses an analytic camera and fixed visibility bounds, while native play uses the real camera and HUD viewport. A target aggregate can therefore conceal both rig imbalance and presentation-dependent failure. **Tomorrow:** stop treating the aggregate as a player-facing acceptance result; observe fresh desktop players on the opening four rooms, separately by rig, and tune early damage/recovery from their actual mistakes. Repair the known bot healing threshold before using it for further balance decisions; do not compensate for that bot defect by indiscriminately weakening enemies. Evidence: `D/ENC12-REPORT.md`; `T/{sim-core.ts,policy.mjs}`; `S/render/DepthRenderer.ts`.

5. **New tactical objectives exist more clearly in rules than in the scene.** Siege asks for **12 seconds inside a 220-unit radius**, but the boundary is not drawn; the inspected Transmission frame mainly shows an exit panel and small objective text. Matron pods reuse stationary Crawler bodies, and their warning can sit beneath them. Priority and control resistance are separate simulation properties without sufficiently distinct presentation. These omissions make new encounter design feel arbitrary. **Tomorrow:** draw the hold area and charge progress in-world; give pods an egg silhouette and hatch countdown; distinguish “kill this first” from “resists control”; visibly collapse a wall-stunned Warden. Evidence: `G/review480/play--breacher--r10-transmission-chamber-mid.png`; `G/champion-mechanics/g2-champions-armory-{05,16,29}.png` reductions; `D/ENC12-REPORT.md` integration requests.

6. **Most tactical setups still dissolve into the same cleanup.** Swarm, pincer, interception, crossfire and protected priority are real scripts, but almost all beats still end by killing every body. Armory's opening charge is different; much of the following sheet returns to circling scattered enemies. Repeated target cleanup consumes the distinction created by the opening. **Tomorrow:** make one priority kill immediately cancel more remaining pressure, have one pincer decisively open an escape lane when its anchor dies, and shorten low-threat tails. Preserve the director's real clear-gated breaks. Evidence: `S/game/waves/{authoredEncounters,EncounterDirector}.ts`; `G/play/{breacher,cryo,arc}/storyboard-armory.png`; `D/ENC12-REPORT.md`.

7. **Some buttons still add more of the rig's existing effect rather than a new decision.** Flashfire's **25 direct damage plus ignition** overlaps Afterburn, Napalm and Meltdown; its inspected pale cone is less forceful than the surrounding promise of fire. Glacier's shatter is automatic; Static Step supplies another small electrical hit. Meanwhile native Cryo and Arc each use only **one Overdrive**, and Pyro/Story novice use none before dying. **Tomorrow:** prototype Flashfire consuming existing burns for an immediate directional payoff, allow an intentional Glacier cash-out, and arrange an early earned Overdrive opportunity before the first major difficulty spike. Evidence: `S/game/abilities/catalog.ts`; `G/review480/abilities--v2-flashfire-crew-checkpoint-crew-checkpoint-03.png`; campaign `final.stats.abilityUses`. Low use includes bot-policy effects; it is not proof humans will hoard V.

8. **The player makes several abstract decisions before acquiring useful experience.** Difficulty, four rigs with four abilities each, and a starting boon precede the first fight. The screens are legible, but diagrams and numerical descriptions cannot establish how placement, control or burn actually feel. Repeated canister artwork also makes distinct boons look less distinct than their rules. **Tomorrow:** offer a clearly labelled recommended first rig with an immediate start, keep full selection available, and demonstrate the first transformation during a safe post-draft beat. Put the excluded augment and lost capability directly on transformative cards, rather than relying on inspection for the consequential tradeoff. Evidence: `G/difficulty-selection.png`, `rig-select.png`, `starting-boon.png`, `augment-draft.png`; `S/game/abilities/augmentCatalog.ts`.

9. **Recovery and investment arrive after some of the harshest early failures.** The first supply stop follows room four; native Pyro dies in room three and Story novice during room four. They never experience the richer shop. The upgrade service also selects the first supported owned transform, and only the eight new transforms support that service. A potentially interesting recovery/build decision is both delayed and narrow. **Tomorrow:** add a modest free recovery/preparation beat after room two on Story, and make the upgrade service explicitly select among eligible owned augments with the exact effect visible. Retain limited stock and explicit departure. Evidence: `D/ENC12-REPORT.md`; `G/shop.png`; `G/play/{pyro,story-cryo}/report.json`. The fixture's zero-credit balance is not evidence that natural income is zero.

10. **Ordinary weapon contact still lacks the clarity and weight of the best moments.** The motion overviews contain distinct hit and kill poses, especially on larger bodies, but light hits on small creatures are modest, the shared overhead reload remains conspicuous, and dense fights still communicate much through numbers. Ultimate spectacle cannot carry every repeated shot. **Tomorrow:** finish one rifle and one shotgun exchange in the actual crowded room: legible contact, mass-appropriate displacement, a decisive lethal pose and a clean return to locomotion; simplify competing particles rather than adding another layer. Evidence: `G/motion/{crawler,stalker,brute,marine}-overview.png`; `G/play/breacher/storyboard-armory.png`; `D/FEEL12-REPORT.md`. Sound and real-time responsiveness remain unjudged.

## 4. Rigs, augments and enemies

**Breacher:** The demolition fantasy is substantially delivered. Ram, **90-damage Slam**, a blocking front and **420-damage Railbreaker** give it a coherent relationship with walls. **Dullest base button: Bulwark**, because its effect is mostly passive after placement. Missing is an intentional conversion of a successful defense into an execution. **Siege Plow versus Fault Line is a real choice:** a moving blocking/pushing front versus an aimed persistent damage/stagger lane. Plow materially changes F; Fault Line adds another area-control tool to an already forgiving Q. Their opportunity cost is meaningful, but the former has the more obvious behavioral transformation. Evidence: `S/game/abilities/{catalog,augmentCatalog}.ts`; `G/play/breacher/storyboard-armory.png`; `G/review480/builds--v2-full-breacher-armory-armory-04.png`.

**Cryo:** The setup-and-execution fantasy is strong: trail/chill → Nova freeze → controlled kills. **Dullest base button: Glacier**, whose automatic expiration owns the payoff. Missing are a voluntary shatter decision and clearer distinctions between chilled, frozen and resistant targets during heavy frost. **Shard Harvest versus Ice Road is the strongest conceptual pair:** frozen kills propagate damage/chill, while the alternative gives up blocking and shatter for a traversable **1.6× movement** strip. That is a genuine sacrifice, not an extra percentage. It needs a proper strip preview and recognizable road; removing the misleading wall preview is necessary but insufficient. Evidence: catalog; `D/ENC12-REPORT.md`; `G/play/cryo/storyboard-armory.png`; `G/native-heavy/cryo-play-r6-armory-040.png`.

**Pyro:** Area denial is visible, but the fantasy is less differentiated internally and less forgiving. **Dullest button: Flashfire**: its ignition repeats the kit's common output without a strong consume/spread decision. Missing is an immediate way to turn established fire into survival or burst. **Ember Relay versus Meltdown Core is mechanically real:** sustained ignition propagation versus replacing the eight-second aura with a **two-second fuse and 240-damage blast**. However, this is an uneven early offer when V has not yet become usable: Relay affects routine play, Core can remain a promise until death. The native Ember Relay run's failure does not prove Relay weak; its resource policy is demonstrably flawed. Evidence: catalog; `G/play/pyro/{report.json,trace.ndjson}`; `G/review480/play--pyro--r3-residential-gallery-mid.png`.

**Arc:** Chains, marine–pylon links and time dilation support a strong electrical identity. **Dullest button: Static Step**, another small arrival zap rather than a special interaction with an existing circuit. Missing is a dash/link maneuver that rewards deliberate route planning. **Pilgrim Pylon versus Live Wire is real:** following constructs versus replacing a chain with a **four-second, one-target tether** whose crossing line matters. The costs are substantial: pylons can stick behind architecture; target death, blocked sight or separation breaks Live Wire, and Forked Overload adds no recipients. Surface that incompatibility before selection and give a broken tether a clear explanation. Evidence: `S/game/abilities/augmentCatalog.ts`; `D/ENC12-REPORT.md`; `G/play/arc/storyboard-armory.png`.

**The transforms are actual rules, not cosmetic labels. Their equal selection in the balance cohorts is not evidence of equal attractiveness:** the controller explicitly values the alternatives equally and uses offer order to break ties. The supplied native runs also do not demonstrate every branch. The implementation earns credit; universal balance and visual payoff remain unproven.

Final Standard uses **2× incoming damage, .8× rest, 1.5× authored counts and .7× pickups** relative to underlying values. The evidence establishes mechanisms for divergence; it does **not** quantify how much of the completion gap each mechanism causes. Nor does it establish that a real player will reproduce these five deaths. The practical concern is more specific: a first-time player can encounter substantial burst punishment before understanding flanking, while the reported completion target provides little reassurance about that experience.

**The kits are used; their peak experience is inconsistent.** Native Breacher casts Q **77** times, F **25**, V **5**; Cryo uses **25/15/1**, Arc **33/16/1**, Pyro **7/4/0**, Story Cryo **12/7/0**. The last two runs die before experiencing an ultimate. All five acquired a transformative augment, so the early draft successfully exposes build identity; it does not guarantee enough survival time to enjoy it.

**No proven infinite exploit emerges.** The nearest recurring pattern is retreat–shoot–cast control, amplified by automatic effects and following constructs. Scripted equal-valued augment selection can also manufacture apparent choice diversity. Native spending is less degenerate than a huge unused balance: Breacher spends **370 of 653 earned credits**, Cryo **90/280**, Arc **90/322**; Pyro and novice Cryo die before shopping. None reaches the Matron's room sixteen or the finale. Late-room fixtures establish available content, not a satisfying completed run.

## 6. What the commercial benchmark visibly has that this lacks

In player-experience order:

1. **A dependable contract between danger and response.** Immediate threats remain recognizable through spectacle, with enough visible space and time to answer them; Containment's crossfire framing, camouflage and mixed-effect hierarchy remain inconsistent.
2. **A deliberate progression from learning to mastery.** The next encounter recombines something the player has understood; here the isolated opener can disappear before teaching its lesson, followed quickly by punishing multi-role pressure.
3. **Encounter consequences that change behavior throughout the fight.** The new scripts supply distinct openings, but need more decisive target-priority payoffs, visible objective state and shorter cleanup.
4. **Run choices whose cost and payoff are unmistakable in play.** The eight transforms are a credible foundation; replacement previews, incompatibility communication, branch-specific effects and opportunities to use scarce powers are unfinished.
5. **Ordinary actions and major events with consistently satisfying physical presentation.** The motion work supports this ambition, but small hits, shared reloads, guardian punish states and finale payoff remain less convincing than the menu presentation.

These judgments are grounded in the cited gallery, catalogs and campaign records. I am not claiming absent audio, requiring permanent stat progression, or treating the legacy Queen as missing content. A premium audiovisual judgment still requires direct listening and real-time play, which this assignment deliberately excludes.

Status of R11's ten ranked problems:

1. **Dangerous small attacks subordinate to other information — partly fixed.** Loaded poses and quieter warning treatment help; frost competition, camouflaged lunges and rapid small-enemy damage remain.
2. **Controls taught before survival — partly fixed.** The requested isolated Crawler/Brute beats, persistent lessons, pack-Q, priority and cover prompts exist. They still do not demonstrate that a novice learns the needed responses before room-three/four pressure.
3. **Rooms ask the same question — partly fixed.** Six grammars, opposed breaches, lulls and siege objectives are substantive. Most fights still converge on killing all remaining bodies while retreating.
4. **Routine elite saturation/control resistance — fixed structurally for the current story.** Late ordinary elite probability is **21% Standard**, and control resistance is reserved for guardians and at most one marked priority elite per beat. The missing resistance marker is a remaining communication defect, not the former blanket rule.
5. **Camera wastes visibility while threats reach the HUD — partly fixed.** Full-marine/weapon framing has substantial documented work, and the inspected native frames do not reproduce the old left-edge marine crop. Nearby/distant threat placement, wall occlusion and safe-view crossfire remain unresolved; `G/DEFECTS.md` additionally reports a Manual Control warning guard failure, which I did not independently open.
6. **Ordinary gunfire lacks physical consequence — partly fixed.** The all-family hit/kill sheets show stronger reactions and collapse; gameplay-scale light hits and shared reload presentation still fall short.
7. **Too few transformative rig choices — fixed as requested.** Eight exclusive transforms, an early guaranteed opportunity and actual replacement rules directly address the complaint. Their equal desirability and bespoke visual communication are separate unfinished work.
8. **Economy leaves money functionally unused — partly fixed, substantially.** Five fixed stops, multi-purchase shopping, consumables/rerolls/upgrades and reduced income create real uses. Native spending is meaningful; early access, service selection and upgrade eligibility still limit the experience.
9. **A route screen without a decision — fixed.** Forced transitions skip it; five boundaries offer direct entry versus an extra escorted group for **150 credits**. The story route remains fixed, but the presented preparation decision is real.
