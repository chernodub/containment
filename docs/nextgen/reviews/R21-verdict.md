Calibration: US$10 (Astra visual, "a small commercial action roguelike with a convincing front end and several
saleable combat moments"); "Solid commercial indie, with a better rig system than its crowd combat" (Astra play);
"polished early-access indie… menus are AA, in-room art is upper indie on its best frames and prototype in dense
fights", US$15–18 (Opus).

**Honest reading: the plateau, seventh round.** Nothing moved more than half a point, and the two visual reviewers
moved combat readability in opposite directions (Opus 6 → 7 for the ordered attacks, Astra 7.0 → 6.5 for the same
build). Play is flat to the tenth. The game is a solid commercial indie and is not approaching premium.

What wave 24 did, from the evidence:
- **Ordering works for the dodge, not yet for the punish.** "In the ordered Swarm frame I can name the first attacker
  and choose a side"; the 450 ms spacing held in every trace. But the cue leaves the first body as soon as it strikes,
  so the opening it earned has no owner.
- **The rule has a visible cost:** "The waiting crowd exposes the turn order… three Brutes hold much the same upright
  pose… the pack looks tame."
- **Camera:** Opus calls composition "mostly done"; the play reviewer still finds corners that hide the way out.
- **Rig equipment in play:** Cryo's tanks, Pyro's stacks and Arc's mast are credited; "the Breacher loses its defining
  piece of kit".
- **Rooms:** the Relay bus and the Transmission feed are credited by name; the other twelve rooms' lanes are not.

What the three verdicts agree on:
1. **Creature finish.** Opus's top item and its "decision holding the picture back most": creatures render as "milky,
   semi-translucent cut-outs with ragged painted edges" on an ink-lined world. Confirmed by the lead in a dark-room
   Cryo frame (flat pale bodies, no shading): a rendering fault being fixed (LOOK25); the wider point — creatures do not
   share the marine's finish — stands. Astra: "scratchy combat silhouettes".
2. **Attack intent lives on the floor, not in the body**, and the Warden "could be another large Brute with a floor
   marker". Elites depend on rings.
3. **Fighting lanes** in most rooms still lose the room's identity; landmark light exists in a few rooms only.
4. **Player effects over the hostile layer** in Pyro and Arc bursts; Meltdown looks like Napalm; the marine shares his
   brightest patch with Arc and frost.
5. **Play, from the traces:** mid-run rooms test combinations before rehearsing them (Cryo lost 153 in Relay Racks);
   the final fork explains its cover too late and no bot takes the covered route; the plate break is read from the
   prompt, not from the Brute; the death card mixed two lessons; a teaching plaque covers the thing it teaches.
6. **Three finishes** (glossy portraits and title, ink-toon marine and set, washed creatures).

Wave 25 (from this): one actor finish for creatures (opaque, two-band, the marine's ink line); a recovery cue and
pose that owns the opening, and waiting enemies that brace, circle and feint; a Warden that is not a Brute; the
Breacher's plate turned to the camera; the remaining rooms' lanes; rehearsal before combination in the middle act and
a final fork that shows its cover.
