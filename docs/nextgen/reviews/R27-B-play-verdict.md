## 2. Calibration

**Solid commercial indie. The biggest gap to the genre leaders is how rarely the player can predict where a good dodge will let them stand and shoot.** Diagnostic’s opening gives a good answer. The broken equipment is on my right, the Brute commits to my left, and the clear strip below the equipment gives me a shot back. In Workshop I can see the red sweep and a file of Crawlers bearing down on the marine against the broken bench. I can say when to leave. I would struggle to name the spot worth reaching. That distinction survived a considerable amount of good work on the individual attacks. I judged the desktop traces and gallery at the heads identified in the manifest. Input feel and the audible mix remain unverified. [Gallery exchange][gallery] · [Workshop warning][workshop] · [Manifest][manifest]

## 3. Ten first-hour problems, ranked

1. **The second counterattack exists on the schedule and disappears in play.** The director delays one following arrival to at least 1.05 seconds after the lead request. Across six traces, every exposed hit in Gallery, Workshop and Manual Control landed in beat zero or two. None landed in beat one, the beat that changed. A delay between spawns buys little if a body already occupies the firing position. In Manual Control, hold the waiting attacker behind the console until the player can cross the first sweep and fire once along the console’s short side. [Scheduling rule][director] · [Manual Control warning][manual] · [Room totals][exchanges]
2. **A novice can graduate without using what the room teaches.** All five Standard campaigns recorded the Residential plate shove. The separate novice checkpoint replay got one too. The full novice campaign skipped it and left with more armour than it entered with. Put the optional armour directly behind the shell’s starting footprint, with an ordinary follow-up target visible through the gap. Keep the bypass for players willing to forgo the pickup. [Plate receipts][coverage] · [Novice trace][novice] · [Replay report][play34]
3. **Ordinary enemies collect too much of the bill for missing a featured decision.** Crawlers and Spitters dealt 74.3% of Cryo’s loss. In Swarm Junction Cryo spent 181.2 suit and Pyro spent 13.0. A player who chooses the correct pod deserves a moment to use the space just saved. Have the saved relay or supply lane repel its nearest screen group once, visibly, before the next attack. [Loss by family][sources] · [Sector costs][suit]
4. **Some of the best tactical consequences happen offscreen.** Cryo cut four Coolant Crawlers at 458.383 seconds. The body reached the grille at 462.583. The foreground fight wins both pictures and most of the closed grille sits above the viewport. Put the next waiting body at the visible end of the closed approach, facing the grille, so the player can turn into that lane with confidence. [Cut receipt][coverage] · [Delayed body][grille]
5. **A family mark identifies the creature before its body explains the attack.** In Workshop, the coral file is easy to count. At the front of that file, bodies overlap the marine and bench. In dense Whiteout the deck is visible again, yet small joints disappear against pale shell and ice. Keep the leading attacker’s striking limb and its contact shadow readable through the first frames of recovery. That gives the player something to aim at after the flash. [Workshop warning][workshop] · [Effects and overlap][fx] · [FX32 observations][fxreport]
6. **The finale’s cover works better than it advertises.** The long route broke the incoming line and all three far-bank users finished without further suit loss. In Arc’s preview, the small lip impact competes with bodies, gunfire and a bright web underfoot. Let a short burst of preview rounds strike the same face in succession so the obstruction reads while the player is moving. [Cover impact][lip] · [Bank outcomes][banks]
7. **The repeat Matron presents two targets more clearly than two stakes.** Arc’s opening shows both pods on the right, vertically separated before the escorts arrive. At review size I can pick either with the mouse. I cannot readily say which one threatens the supply. Arc let both pods hatch in each of two clutches. Put one beside the actual pickup and orient the other visibly toward the relay. [Marked pair][pods] · [Hatch receipts][coverage]
8. **Protected enemies sometimes appear to ignore the strongest input.** Plate strips immediately and rig damage now pays at recovery. Breacher’s 34.1-point Gallery bank and payout still occupy one input sample, while the Brute finishes the authored swing. Give the wound a visible onset at contact and a distinct body recoil on release. The player should recognize the Q they spent even when the swing must finish. [Banking rule][enemy] · [Native and replay receipts][play34]
9. **The biggest build decision gets too little space to teach its cost.** The supply screen explains both free options, and the game already has augment drills. Both Arc campaigns still demonstrate the following pylon. Put a shooter behind a corner and a second enemy across the approach in the next ordinary fight. Dragging Pilgrim around the corner and sweeping Live Wire across the approach should produce visibly different solutions. [Free pair][shop] · [Augment contracts][aug]
10. **The death screen offers a faster retry than diagnosis.** In the staged Relay death, the Spitter dealt the final point and the Brute dealt 135. Keeping both facts is excellent. The picture shows a generic cover lesson. The source supports a larger recorded diagram with the earlier position, fatal footprint and a traversable clearing step, but final22 has zero natural deaths to demonstrate it. Give the impact and earlier pose a brief alternating emphasis before the player presses Retry. [Relay death card][death] · [Diagram implementation][diagram]

Two separated freeze targets would make carrying the next pulse a decision. | **Real, strongest pair.** Shard Harvest changes which frozen victim to kill first. Ice Road gives up the wall and shatter for a five-second, 1.6× movement lane. |

Meltdown Core replaces eight seconds of mobile aura with a two-second fuse, making the marine’s final position decisive. |

## 5. What the campaigns reveal

**Standard has credible failure pressure and a poorly distributed opening.** All five competent native runs and novice Story cleared twenty rooms. The dated final manifest gives Standard **34/48** on the usual seeds, with Breacher 7/12 and the others 9/12, and Story **23/24**. The brief’s 41/48 belongs to an earlier tuning pass. The fresh **39/48** covers Cryo and Pyro only, with seven Cryo deaths in sectors 9–14. Across the five native Standard runs, successive four-sector blocks cost **30, 620.5, 860.8, 1,001.4 and 926.6** suit. Passenger, Residential and Atrium cost zero throughout. Story reached 10.5 health after Safety Interlock and later finished. Repair, pickups and recovery make cumulative loss a measure of pressure, not remaining life. A blanket damage increase would hit the wrong rooms. [Manifest][manifest] · [Suit ledger][suit] · [Difficulty table][difficulty]

**Ordering hits buys fairness. Converting the dodge into an attack remains optional.** Each native campaign maintained a 450 ms minimum melee-hit gap despite two or three simultaneous melee wind-ups and up to two ranged. Breacher’s Gallery warning at 297.833 led to a strike at 298.433 and an exposed hit at 298.983. The Shaft winch also gives the player a shoulder from which to turn, although Standard still spent 20.0–56.3 there. Across the manifest’s five Standard runs, **84 of 13,326 weapon hits** landed on exposed enemies, about 0.63%. That is a share of all hits, including shots at ineligible enemies. It measures how peripheral the punish is to winning. The stronger evidence is zero second-beat exposed hits across all eighteen revised room visits. Arc 2718 cleared Workshop with zero loss and zero exposed hits in 183 contacts. Sustained fire and movement remain a sound answer. [Gallery exchange][gallery] · [Shaft storyboard][shaft] · [Room contacts][exchanges] · [Manifest][manifest]

**Reserve cancellation is real. Cleanup is a narrower problem than room totals suggest.** The six traces contain seven explicit reserve-cut events. Cryo, Pyro and Arc each cut four Coolant Crawlers, Story cut one, and Breacher, Cryo and Arc 2718 each cut three Workshop Stalkers. A clean Workshop leader kill also redirects the next flank to the far hatch and removes an arrival. Three Standard Workshop runs paid zero suit, Pyro paid 4.0, and Breacher paid 34.7, all to Stalkers in the later flank. I compared incoming suit changes after the last Brute, Carrier or Spitter corpse within each mixed beat. Breacher’s 34.7 in Workshop and Arc 2718’s 25.2 in Coolant were the exceptions among Standard runs. Across Coolant’s five Standard visits, the guardian opening itself cost 245.0 combined, including escorts. Later scheduled fights and departing stragglers are different problems. The three-body withdrawal works in sampled cases. Breacher’s Workshop tail is still expensive enough to undo the sense of winning the room. [Breacher trace][breachtrace] · [Arc 2718 trace][alttrace] · [Reserve receipts][coverage]

**The fourth guardian and the final fork earn their places.** Seed 1729 runs Warden in Armory, shutter Warden in Relay, Matron in Coolant and destination-pod Matron in Swarm. Seed 2718 runs Matron, shutter Warden, plated Warden and finishes with three guardian appointments. The fourth fight on 1729 asks which asset to save. The covered final bend is defensible too. Breacher and Pyro went far on Standard, paid 46.6 and 0 crossing, then zero before finishing about four seconds later. Cryo and the two Arcs went near, paid 0, 0 and 24.0 crossing, then 48.6, 49.8 and 52.3 in the following ten seconds. Story went far for zero in both windows. Cover can buy a quiet finish at genuine travel risk. These unmatched runs and shorter far-bank observation windows cannot establish a universally better route. ## 6. What the commercial leaders deliver better

1. **A destination inside the attack pattern.** In *Enter the Gungeon*, a gap in a volley often tells me both where to roll and where my next shot can come from. Containment reaches that standard at the Gallery opener.
2. **An enemy sequence worth waiting out.** In *Hades*, I will give up damage now to earn a safer commitment afterward. In Containment, shooting throughout the exchange usually works and scarcely costs the player an alternative.
3. **Consequences that change what the player sees immediately.** Breaking a shutter does this. Canceling a reserve whose grille is across the room still feels closer to receiving a notification.
4. **Builds that change the next minute of play.** The exclusive augments have the necessary mechanics. Too many subsequent rooms let a new owner carry on shooting from the same positions.
5. **A death that suggests a specific next attempt.** Containment already distinguishes the fatal point from the larger prior loss. The remaining challenge is making the earlier, still-reversible mistake the first thing a player notices.

## 8. Five investments for one month

1. **Finish one late encounter before touching another.** Use Manual Control. Give the bait, sidestep past the console and return shot distinct places. Watch a fresh desktop player point to the landing before the attack commits, then let them try it.
2. **Move some pressure from late screens into an early optional decision.** Make the Residential armour worth stripping a plate for. Spend the same encounter budget on one fewer late screen attacker when the player makes the right call.
3. **Show the room changing at the player’s point of aim.** Start with Coolant’s closed approach, Swarm’s two threatened assets and the finale’s cover face. Each already has a consequence worth seeing.
4. **Give every exclusive a first fight that uses its trade.** Begin with Pilgrim versus Live Wire around one corner and Shard Harvest versus Ice Road across one firing lane. Keep the ordinary enemies and let the different movement explain the choice.
5. **Put a mouse and headphones in new players’ hands.** Watch one busy dodge, one supply decision and one natural death per player. Ask where they expected to stand, which warning they heard and what move they will try on Retry. Those answers settle the largest uncertainties left by the bot evidence.
