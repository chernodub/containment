## 2. Calibration

**I would pay US$10 on this visual showing.** The decision screens and warning system reach solid commercial indie quality. The cast falls between a competent prototype and that standard. In the four-frame finale I can follow the whole encounter, from the charged machine to the last attack on the far bank. What stays with me is the machine. Against *Hades*, I want to remember the attacker by the way it carries its weight. Against *The Ascent*, I want the few metres of deck under that attacker to tell me where the fight happened. The motion judgment covers photographed poses and their sequence. Timing and game feel require playing. [Finale storyboard][story] · [Walk][walk] · [Native fights][fight]

## 3. Ten most damaging defects, ranked

1. **The Brute’s weight stops at the wrist.** The back rises and the mask pitches, yet the two dark knuckles look like beads on the forearm. The far foot often looks like a peg. Repeating that figure across a pack makes the colony’s strongest ordinary animal look light. Flatten the contact face of the planted knuckle, compress the wrist above it and carry the shoulder over that hand before lifting the opposite heel. [Walk][walk] · [Heavies][heavy]
2. **The title exposes the least finished creature art at the largest scale.** The hero’s shield has a clean rim, insignia and fasteners. Opposite it, the Warden’s jaw shading breaks into little sawteeth and its lower limbs disappear into the opening. This is the image selling the game. Redraw the opposing silhouette at title resolution, give one hand a clear overlap with the hatch lip, and carry the breach light across the skull and planted limb. [Full title][title] · [Title comparison][tp]
3. **Most fights leave their room’s strongest idea behind.** Passenger Vault has an excellent diagonal of displaced berths. Its incident camera looks into a blank foreground wall. Overload has a beautiful generator and a nearly anonymous far bank. Extend each accident through the part of the lane where the player fights, starting with berth-width gouges and a scorched, raised feeder end at the refuge. [Incidents][inc] · [Native fights][fight] · [Finale][story]
4. **The Warden’s best damage opportunity has its least legible face.** From the side I can see opposed bone plates and a luminous gap. At wall stun the sail, gold brackets and red marker all register before the weak point. Rotate the exposed jaw toward the camera on impact, hold it open through stun and give the lower lip one narrow light edge. [Side crop][jaw] · [Wall stun at 480][stun]
5. **Fire and frost often describe the surface better than the victim.** I can trace Napalm’s remaining coals and Whiteout’s snow banks, but small burning creatures resemble sparks and frozen Brutes become blue cutouts. Tie the last hot tongue or ice fracture to a moving joint, preserve one dark anatomical marking through status, and reduce the deck highlight immediately behind it. [Native burn][burn] · [Napalm aftermath][nap] · [Whiteout][white]
6. **The attachments sell four portraits better than four fighting stances.** Breacher braces convincingly. Pyro’s exhausts appear suspended on either side of the shoulder, and Arc’s raised coil gesture becomes a conventional forward gun pose. At combat size I end up reading shoulder colour. Give Pyro a visible dark yoke and Arc a distinct elbow-to-mast gap while aiming. [Selector, calm and fight][rig]
7. **The smallest attackers can look like objects.** A waiting amber Crawler beside a pickup or inside Pyro fire resembles a cartridge or cinder. The cyan Stalker has a superb strike silhouette but very little body before release. Reserve a larger dark triangular gap under the Crawler’s hook and give the waiting Stalker one visible planted foreleg. [Loaded crowd][load] · [Native burn][burn] · [Stalker poses][stalk]
8. **Repeated triangular light pools make different incidents look staged the same way.** At Checkpoint the beam has more area than the broken bar. At Coolant and Manual Control the marine walks into pale machinery and loses an edge. Give each source a characteristic falloff, occlude it with the damaged part, and keep the brightest reflection off the crossing line. [Checkpoint pair][check] · [Centres][centre] · [Greyscale incidents][grey]
9. **The final refuge remains hard to identify before the shot.** I can follow the tracer to the player, but the arrows and bright deck patch do little to show the height or orientation of the stopping face. Give the lip a dark vertical side visible from the approach and put the first contact flash on its shooter-facing corner. [Final fork][fork] · [Impact, lower-left panel][impact]
10. **The death diagrams have room to explain a mistake, but their symbols use too little of it.** In the Spitter card the impact and escape figures nearly touch, while the generic “Scripted damage” card offers a few symbols in empty space. Enlarge and separate the positions, draw the blocking edge across the sightline where relevant, and give an unknown cause a factual fallback. [Spitter card][death] · [Generic results card][generic]

## 4. Five things to preserve

1. **The single filled imminent attack.** The red arc stays readable through a mixed swarm and across a frozen floor. It tells me where to step while I am still identifying the attacker. [Ordered crowd][order] · [Ice][ice]
2. **The marine’s local light and visor.** Together with the dark gun, they let me reacquire the player against both teal metal and purple infestation. The HUD leaves that space open. [Native fights][fight] · [Rigs][rig]
3. **The Matron’s raised, striped abdomen and split pods.** The organ, the clutch and the open husks make a sequence a player can understand without a caption. [Pod countdown and opening][pods]
4. **The first route choice.** The Warden’s broad back and the Matron’s raised abdomen make the two routes visibly different before I read either reward. The boon cards likewise show what a choice does. [Routes and boons][cards]
5. **The ending’s restraint.** The dish and torn hull carry the image, the message lands before the statistics, and the tiny operator gives the wreck a human scale. [Ending][ending]

## 8. Five investments for one month, in order

1. **Give the common Brute a convincing planted stride, then carry it into the elite and Warden.** Start with three drawings at actual camera scale showing load, support and release. Build the wrist, sole and compressed shoulder around those shapes. Finish the Warden’s stun with the mouth turned toward the player. This work pays off in ordinary packs, the boss, the route card and the title. [Walk][walk] · [Stun][stun] · [Routes][cards]
2. **Commission one finished title illustration using the new equipment design.** Keep the visor, shield and type. Stage the Warden bracing on the broken hatch and light both opponents from the breach. Judge the final contours at 1920 and the face-off at 480. [Full title][title] · [Title comparison][tp]
3. **Carry four accidents across a whole fight.** Start with Passenger, Checkpoint, Transmission and Overload. Give each a distinct raised profile and a floor consequence that remains identifiable when the source leaves the frame. Build the final refuge into the reactor feeder. [Incidents][inc] · [Native fights][fight] · [Final fork][fork]
4. **Spend an effects pass on what the hit did to the body.** For Pyro, keep one ember attached to the recoiling flank. For Cryo, keep a dark moving joint inside the frost. Give Meltdown one short travelling front so its reach is visible without raising the brightness of the whole area. [Native burn][burn] · [Whiteout][white] · [Meltdown][melt]
5. **Finish the silhouettes people aim and dodge with.** Connect Pyro’s pipes to its pack, recover Arc’s asymmetrical arm, and separate the Crawler’s hook and Stalker’s supporting leg at rest. These changes should still explain the next action with colour and warning graphics removed. [Rigs][rig] · [Crawler contact][hook] · [Stalker poses][stalk]
