Calibration: "budget indie with a release-quality decision interface and an uneven combat presentation", US$8 (Astra
visual); "late commercial alpha — I would want to try another rig after one run" (Astra play); "strong early-access
indie", US$12–15 (Opus). Not yet solid commercial indie (7 across the board); not premium.

What moved: everything on the play side (pacing +1.7, onboarding +1.1, loop and enemies +0.8); environments, effects,
interface and cohesion +0.5 to +1. What did not: characters/creatures and animation (5.5). What one reviewer says got
worse: combat readability in crowds on the darker ship (attacking limbs and gaps between bodies lost contrast).

Lead reading — what holds it back now, in order:
1. The next hit is not readable in a crowd: creatures are as dark as the floor, hostility is signalled by one hue
   (orange) shared with fire, rigs, credits and UI; friendly effects, damage numbers and foreground props cover the
   committed attacker and the escape route. (→ VALUE14, extended)
2. The marine loses his identity in play: reads beige/flesh-toned, overlit solid white, black gun. (→ HERO14)
3. Poses lack weight: marine torso static over a huge stride, rifle hits barely change shooter or victim, creature
   attacks hold one pose then snap; Overdrives lack a consequence at the target. (→ MOTION14)
4. Rooms repeat one composition (a flat trail across tiled deck to a prop at the edge); incident shapes are flat
   decals, not lit physical things; middles are empty. (→ ROOM14)
5. Encounters open differently but converge on the same cleanup; no consequences for breaking a pincer, killing a
   leader or interrupting a brood; Story has an attrition cliff at room 11; the opener can be cleared at range without
   learning to dodge; Glacier's second press is easy to miss; money has no purpose at either end of a run; the death
   screen names the killer but not the counter. (→ ENC14)
6. Boss tier: Warden reads as machinery, Matron is too small; small elites show little rank.
7. Four rendering styles (title, glossy card renders, line-art shop icons, toon world); markers need decoding; some
   decisions still look like paperwork.

Keep: the UI system and before/after diagrams, twenty distinct room palettes with dark perimeters, the marine's light
pool, Railbreaker/Napalm/Seismic/Glacier as effect benchmarks, honest attack geometry and death receipts, the four
rig kits and their new decisions (burn cash-out, on-demand shatter).
