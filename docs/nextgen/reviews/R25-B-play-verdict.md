## 2. Calibration

**Solid commercial indie. The biggest gap to the genre leaders is what a successful dodge lets the player do next.** At the Diagnostic recorder I can describe the whole move in advance: cross the end of the sweep, turn around the fixture and fire back at the exposed Brute. At the east wall of Service Shaft Landing I can identify the attack that will hit Arc. I cannot confidently point to a landing from which to deal with the next attacker. That difference matters more to the premium claim than another lighting pass. I judged the six recorded desktop campaigns at `f562e310` and the manifest-labelled presentation studies. Input response and audible impact remain unverified by this still-and-trace review. [Gallery storyboard][gallery] · [Shaft storyboard][shaft] · [Build provenance][manifest]

## 3. Ten first-hour problems, ranked

1. **The late pack takes away the firing position the opening taught me to earn.** Arc 1729 clears both Shaft lunge introductions without losing suit, then loses 116.7 entirely to Crawlers. The pressure frame puts the marine between a red sweep, several bodies and the east wall. The director checks sixteen walkable endpoints 65 units away against overlapping melee footprints. That establishes an escape from the swings, while projectiles and the next approach can still claim the same ground. Give the Shaft pincer one shoulder of the winch that breaks the shooter’s sightline, and delay the next approach across that shoulder until the player can turn and fire once. [Shaft storyboard][shaft] · [Attack admission][director]
2. **The first four rooms let the player leave the lesson behind.** Standard spent 30 suit across twenty opening-room observations, all in Awakening Bay. Cryo’s first Atrium Brute swings offscreen while the marine is already beyond the central obstruction. Its first return hit lands 2.30 seconds into the aftermath. Stage the side-on attack across the player’s route to the Residential pickup so that earning a clean clear involves a deliberate lateral step. Keep the early damage forgiving. [Opening receipts][swings] · [Sector ledgers][suit]
3. **A fresh wave of small bodies can cost more than the room’s advertised encounter.** Arc 1729 spends 0.7 suit in Coolant’s Matron beat, 43.1 in crossfire and 91.5 in the subsequent pincer. A player who handles the boss well can still remember the room chiefly for repeated swipes. Give the successful brood interrupt a practical effect on that later pincer, such as a delayed arm the marine can see forming behind a hatch. [Arc campaign trace][arc]
4. **The player has to look away from the target to understand a reserve kill.** Cryo really does cut off four Crawlers. The native frame puts the count in the top-right HUD, the temporary grille prominently on the left, and the persistent closed entry partly outside the right edge. I know how many enemies I stopped if I read the caption; I have little reason to choose the right-hand lane next. Carry the same directional mark from the priority target to the sealed entry and retain it until the next pincer arrives. [Native cutoff][cut]
5. **The repeat guardians change the stakes more clearly in the rules than in the picture.** Arc 2718’s shutter impact fits the Warden and shattered pieces on screen, but the red targeting ring finds the exposed Spitter faster than its body does. At the repeat Matron, two hatches are visible in the log while the relay-side and supply-side stakes are hard to distinguish under the escorts. Put the Spitter in the opening against bare floor. Give each threatened pod its destination’s existing floor icon through hatch or destruction. [Shutter impact][shutter] · [First clutch][clutch] · [Hatch receipts][coverage]
6. **The protected arrival swing breaks an otherwise learnable weapon rule.** For the first Atrium and Gallery Brutes the damage routine rejects the entire hit until recovery. A player who correctly switches to plasma can get silence from the gun’s best target. Preserve the full swing and let incoming shots visibly strip plate or bank a visible wound that resolves on recovery. [Arrival and damage rules][enemy]
7. **Knowing which gun breaks plate rarely costs the novice anything to forget.** The four competent rigs fired two or three plasma shots in Passenger Vault, then three to six in Residential across five runs. Story Cryo fired three and zero and took zero damage in both. Put the second plate between the marine and an optional reward, give a clear flesh target beside it, and let a clean strip buy the chance to switch back for the kill. [Residential receipts][coverage] · [Novice trace][novice]
8. **The brightest part of a cast can hide the survivor that should get the next shot.** Whiteout leaves enough deck visible to read hostile red, a real success. In the full Pyro build, fan roots, the detonation ring and a struck body still coincide at the marine’s feet. Give a surviving target’s striking limb priority over the residual flames just after impact. Preserve the bright leading edge that establishes range. [Cast and overlap sheet][effects]
9. **The first shop makes a free build commitment look like ordinary stock.** In the 180-credit view Pilgrim Pylon ends one row and Live Wire starts the next. Both fit, both say zero credits, and neither visually reads as half of an exclusive pair. Give them a single comparison row above consumables, with the displaced ability pictured once. [Supply stop][shop]
10. **The debrief can name the killer without showing the mistake worth correcting.** Pyro’s real death card correctly names a Stalker lunge and plots the room’s three sources. Its isolated lateral-step diagram cannot explain how the near-bank hold put a second firing lane across that step. Reuse the final committed footprints and marine position for a small last-exchange diagram beside the existing retry action. [Pyro death card][death]

**Difficulty.** One clear in five native Standard campaigns and a Story novice clear with 1,077.9 cumulative damage establish that recovery matters. They do not estimate a human win rate. The manifest’s named post-change Standard cohort cleared 37/48 (9 Breacher, 8 Cryo, 10 Pyro, 10 Arc), with Story at 23/24 and zero reported stalls. Its prior cohort was 44/48 with one stall. The supplied evidence also quotes 41/48 and 22/24, and disagrees on fresh Standard seeds: 40/48 in the brief, 44/48 in the PLAY32 report. Those figures should stay attached to their sources. Smaller paid repairs, now 25 health and 20 armour on Standard, are one of several simultaneous changes. Standard is survivable and capable of producing an explicable loss. Its weakness is where it spends that difficulty: five quiet Residential Galleries, then as much as 69.8 suit in Crew Checkpoint and 95.4 in Transmission. [Sector ledgers][suit] · [Cohorts][manifest] · [Revision report][changes] · [Death evidence][alt]

**Control of the next exchange.** I matched the first ordinary weapon hit on each arrival Brute’s ID to its recovery timestamp. In Gallery the delay across all five Standard runs was 0.03–0.28 seconds. In Atrium four were at or below 0.28 seconds; Cryo’s target was offscreen near recovery. Story took the Gallery swing for 30.9 and landed its first return hit 1.75 seconds later. The authored shoulder works. Across the whole Standard sample, only 64 of 10,122 weapon hits landed on exposed bodies, with 35 recovery staggers. That denominator includes ineligible targets and shotgun contacts, so it measures how much of the fight uses the mechanic, not how often a player succeeds when offered it. Maximum simultaneous warnings reached three melee and two ranged, closest melee hits were 450–533 ms apart, and the marine spent 9.78–21.36% of samples outside the central 60%. A packed fight can obey every timing rule and still offer an obscure landing. [Opening receipts][swings] · [Gallery trace][cryo] · [Campaign measures][manifest]

**Cleanup and room identity.** Summing room-source ledgers gives 2,388.6 of 3,373.3 Standard suit loss to Crawlers and Stalkers. It would be a mistake to call all of that cleanup. In the sampled Standard Coolant and Workshop mixed beats I found zero further suit loss after the last observed Brute, Spitter or Carrier in that beat. In Workshop, Cryo paid 13.2 during the brood-leader beat, Pyro 34.9 during the flank and Arc 2718 split 40.3 between those two beats. Coolant’s later all-light pincer still collected 91.5 from Arc 1729, 25.2 from Arc 2718, 12.6 from Cryo and zero from Pyro. The withdrawal rule addresses the tail of a won fight. A separate scheduled wave remains the expensive part for some runs. [Arc trace][arc] · [Cryo trace][cryo] · [Withdrawal rules][director]

**Choices changed the room.** Cryo and Pyro cut off four and three Coolant Crawlers respectively. Story Cryo cut off two and has a later closed-entry frame. Pyro and Arc 2718 each cut three Workshop Stalkers. Four Standard marines used an Interlock pad, with Arc 2718 taking breach 2 and the other three breach 1. On the full 1729 Brood itinerary, Armory and Relay Racks field Wardens, followed by Matrons in Coolant and Swarm. The fourth encounter asks whether to protect access or supplies. Cryo’s first clutch yielded two observed hatches, Pyro’s zero; both had another clutch. On 2718 the sequence ends after three guardians: Armory Matron, shutter Warden, plated Warden. No recorded itinerary demands either species more than twice. [Encounter receipts][coverage] · [Alternate itinerary][alt]

**The covered bend is defensible.** Cryo reached the fork with zero dash charges, an active near-lane warning and nearby melee pressure. It went behind the reactor, paying 27.7 over 3.5 seconds. Story Cryo crossed far for zero. The near route cost Pyro zero and Arc 2718 12.6, and both died after arriving. The core breaks the original shooters’ line of sight around the bend. That is a reason to choose cover even though the longer crossing can cost more and arriving does not win the hold. These are different fights, so none supplies a same-fight price for the other bank. ## 6. What the commercial leaders deliver better, in order

1. **A gap the player can see before committing.** In *Enter the Gungeon*, identifying a gap can determine both the dodge and the firing position after it. Containment achieves that in Gallery’s opening and loses it at Shaft’s east wall.
2. **A punish window worth standing still for.** In *Hades*, recognizing the end of a sequence can justify starting a slower attack. The Warden stun earns that confidence here. An ordinary missed swing in a late pack seldom does.
3. **An encounter victory visible in the room.** A dead leader should make a particular door safe enough that the player turns toward it. The cutoff counter is more certain than the view through the marine’s sights.
4. **An immediately legible cost to a build commitment.** Ice Road has the right design. The player should see the lost cover and the new escape route as plainly in the next room as in the card text.
5. **A death that changes the next attempt.** A correct damage ledger invites a retry. Showing which angle the player surrendered would give that retry a plan.

## 8. Five investments for one month, in order

1. **Rebuild one late Shaft pincer around a second firing position.** Keep its present enemies. Make a player who reads the first lunge able to name a landing, turn and interrupt the next attacker. Have fresh desktop players point out the landing before revealing the designer’s answer.
2. **Redistribute pressure from ordinary contacts into early, recoverable decisions.** Keep Awakening Bay gentle. Give Residential’s plate and Atrium’s shoulder a contested reward, and trade one late Crawler wave for a visible, breakable crossfire.
3. **Make encounter consequences point to the next target.** Start with Coolant’s persistent grille, the Relay Racks gunner and the two Swarm clutch destinations. In each case the player should see where to move or aim while continuing to shoot.
4. **Follow a build choice from the shop into a room that uses it.** Present the two deferred exclusives together, then use the next encounter’s existing geometry to offer two solutions, such as holding a lane with Fault Line or advancing with Siege Plow.
5. **Make the next retry about one decision the player remembers.** Show the last attack angles on the death card and let the relevant enemy’s loaded pose carry the lesson. Check those transitions with human eyes and ears during sustained fire, where the stills and cue inventory cannot settle the question.
