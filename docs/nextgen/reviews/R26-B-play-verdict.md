## 2. Calibration

**Solid commercial indie. The biggest gap to the genre leaders is how seldom a correct read gives the player control of the next exchange.** At the Shaft winch I can name a move before seeing the outcome. Step across the Stalker’s locked line, keep the machinery on one shoulder, turn and shoot during the gap. At peak pressure in Diagnostic I can see two red lanes converging near the lower wall. Knowing which one lands first tells me less about where I can finish the dodge with a shot. That difference matters more to the premium claim than the finish on the armour. This judgment uses the desktop campaigns and studies at `bc56c43c`. Stills and traces support judgments about timing, staging and consequences. Audible impact and input feel require a person at the controls. [Shaft storyboard][shaft] · [Diagnostic storyboard][gallery] · [Gallery manifest][manifest]

## 3. Ten first-hour problems, ranked

1. **The promised counterattack usually belongs to the opening actor.** Gallery’s opening works for all five competent runs. Over their whole campaigns, just 84 of 12,460 weapon contacts hit an exposed enemy. That denominator includes ineligible targets and measures how much this mechanic figures in combat, not the chance of succeeding at an offered punish. Give one later Diagnostic exchange the Shaft treatment. Stage the second attacker across the recorder, leaving the player a visible shoulder from which one return shot is viable. [Gallery storyboard][gallery] · [Attack scheduling][director] · [Campaign measures][manifest]
2. **The player can pass the first lessons without learning the second action.** Residential’s side-on Stalker cuts an intelligible line across the route to armour. Every Standard and Story marine left unhurt. Story Cryo fired three plasma shots in Passenger and zero in Residential. Give the plated body a stagger that visibly opens access to the optional pickup, then leave a flesh target in the same sightline for the switch back. Preserve the forgiving exit for someone who declines. [Residential lock][residential] · [Encounter receipts][coverage] · [Novice trace][novice]
3. **Winning the advertised fight can buy too little relief.** Breacher lost zero in Workshop’s brood-leader beat, 50.4 in the rush and 83.2 in the flank. Story Cryo paid 117.7 in that flank alone. The player who prioritized the leader has good reason to wonder what the kill bought. On a clean leader kill, move the next flank’s entry to the far side of the vacated lane so that the player can use it. [Breacher trace][breachtrace] · [Novice trace][novice]
4. **A successful reserve cut can happen outside the player’s attention.** Pyro cut four Coolant Crawlers at 399.067 seconds. The entry that stayed closed was outside the viewport at the native cutoff receipts. Story’s later gathering hatch makes the delay visible, but a close view of the persistent seal needed a staged replay. Put the closed-entry bearing on the defeated target for a beat, pointing along the deck to the existing crest. Then let the first delayed silhouette advance to the grille and stop there. [Cut and staging receipts][coverage] · [Capture limitations][defects]
5. **The finale can hide its best decision among its warnings.** In Pyro’s fork frame, line, capsule, projectiles, destination and path marks all compete near the marine. The first protected step is a sound destination. Its shelter is harder to recognize than the nearer red threat. Make the cover lip itself catch the first preview shot and hold that impact long enough to see from the relay. Keep the first foothold as the only shelter mark. [Played fork][fork] · [Route costs][cross]
6. **A protected swing still changes the rules of a rig attack.** Weapon hits strip plate immediately and can bank health damage. All six native campaigns recorded zero bank payout on the sampled arrival Brutes. The staged shot banks 45. In the same damage routine an ability hit on that protected body exits before armour or health is touched. A player who spent Q has less useful feedback than one who fired plasma. Give damaging rig hits the same deferred-health accounting while retaining the committed swing. [Damage rule][enemy] · [Native and staged coverage][coverage]
7. **The fourth guardian’s choice competes with the bodies protecting it.** Cryo’s first Swarm clutch produced two hatches. In its before-frame the Matron is at the right edge, the lower pod is partly clipped, and several larger bodies occupy the centre. The destination icons are accurate but small. Place the first marked pair on opposite, visible sides of the relay and armour, then let the escorts enter between them. The player should be able to pick what to save before firing. [First clutch][clutch] · [Hatch receipts][coverage]
8. **Damage numbers group hits better than the effects identify the survivor.** The full Pyro fan has a strong direction. At its target end, hot floor, struck hide and victims share orange values. The number establishes a hit while the creature about to act becomes hard to pick out. Fade residual fire locally behind the surviving attacker’s loaded limb after the cast front passes. [Full-build sheet][buildsheet] · [Cast/contact sheet][fx]
9. **The terminal shot can explain the killer too late to explain the mistake.** Breacher’s card correctly attributes the entire fatal room to Stalkers and draws overlapping footprints. Its advice to move before overlap is fair. The actionable instant was earlier, before the two lines converged. Give the diagram one previous position from the same exchange and enlarge that diagram within the existing card. [Native death card][death] · [Diagram logic][diagram]
10. **A defining build choice gets a good sales pitch and an uncertain first demonstration.** The free pair now reads as a pair, even at compact desktop size. Both Arc campaigns picked Pilgrim Pylon. A first-time player comparing it with Live Wire has little evidence of how differently movement will feel. In the following room, stage two existing enemies either side of a blocker so that steering the tether or pulling a pylon around the corner each pays off once. [Supply comparison][shop] · [Alternate Arc build][alt] · [Augment contracts][aug]

I use the manifest’s seed-listed measurement. Four clears in five native Standard runs across only two seeds cannot establish a human win rate or justify a global damage increase. **There is rhythm inside the crowd.** Standard recorded at most two or three concurrent melee warnings depending on the run, two or three ranged warnings, minimum melee hit gaps of 450–800 ms, and 7–14 feints per campaign. The marine spent 14.88–21.62% of sampled positions outside the central 60% of the frame. Off-centre placement alone does not prove a camera failure. Diagnostic gives an actual return shot in 0.12–0.30 seconds on Standard. The Shaft gives a defensible stand at the winch shoulder and another 1.2 seconds before the next pincer approach. All five Standards cleared it for 9.1–64.5. These are local successes. The native pressure frames show how readily later packs turn the same maneuver back into continuous retreat. **Reserve cancellation changes play.** On Coolant, Breacher and Pyro cut four Crawlers each, Cryo and Arc 1729 one each, and Story two. Workshop cutoffs removed three Stalkers for Cryo, Pyro and Arc 2718. Killing the reserve leader can close a later entry. Interrupting the brood can separately delay an open pincer arm by 3.5 seconds. Every run also used an Interlock pad. Standard selected three distinct breach IDs across the two seeds. [Receipts][coverage] · [Encounter director][director] · [Capture limitations][defects]

**Cleanup depends on what is counted.** In the native traces I grouped incoming damage by authored beat and compared hit times with the last Brute, Carrier or Spitter corpse in mixed beats. Workshop had zero subsequent loss by that measure in all six runs. Its scheduled late flank still cost Breacher 83.2 and Story Cryo 117.7. A projectile can outlive its shooter. Cryo also has a receipt of three Crawlers actually withdrawing. **A late wave can outweigh the featured fight. ## 6. What the commercial leaders deliver better

1. **A firing position the player can anticipate.** In *Enter the Gungeon*, reading a pattern often tells me where to roll and where I will be able to shoot afterward. Containment achieves that at the Shaft shoulder and loses it in later Gallery pressure.
2. **A reason to wait for the opening.** A *Hades* enemy finishing a sequence can earn the commitment to a slower attack. Here a full magazine into the crowd is usually productive, and few weapon contacts exploit recovery.
3. **A room that visibly changes when its plan fails.** A leader’s death should make the player turn toward a newly safe entrance. Coolant often communicates that consequence in a counter or edge bearing while the player is aiming elsewhere.
4. **A build decision that gets a memorable first use.** Ice Road and Live Wire deserve a room where the player can immediately experience what was gained and sacrificed.
5. **A retry with a particular move in mind.** The right fatal source and room ledger are already present. The missing moment is the last point at which the player could still choose a different position.

## 8. Five investments for one month

1. **Author the second exchange in three late fights.** Start with Diagnostic, Workshop and Manual Control. Keep the existing enemies. For each, design an attack the player can bait, a reachable firing position and one enemy they can interrupt from there. Ask a fresh desktop player to point out the landing before the attack resolves.
2. **Move pressure into decisions with visible stakes.** Make Residential’s armour worth a deliberate strip-and-switch. Fund that early risk by shortening one later Workshop flank when its leader falls. Preserve Story’s recovery and the option to decline the pickup.
3. **Give target priority a visible consequence in the same view.** Show the line from cut leader to sealed grille, stage both Matron stakes together, and let the final cover lip visibly stop a shot. These changes answer the same player question in the room where it arises.
4. **Follow each exclusive from the comparison row into combat.** Use the next room’s existing geometry to demonstrate both solutions. Begin with Pilgrim Pylon versus Live Wire, then Shard Harvest versus Ice Road. Judge whether the player changes where they stand.
5. **Review ten seconds around a mistake with human players.** Use sustained-fire encounters to hear whether the family warning survives the mix, observe which survivor draws the eye, and put the last actionable position on the death card. Success is a player describing one move they intend to try on Retry.
