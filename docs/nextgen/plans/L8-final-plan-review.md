Reviewed 2026-11-15 against frozen gameplay `9a44e604bf644b6cf0ab8b2637913e19ca3e4740`. Scope: plan design, not balance or final rendered art. Read R10, the withdrawn/replacement standard ledger, L8 report, activated registry (base twenty + V1.1 + ingress overrides), strict checker and final-combined receipt. No simulation, source edits or browser runs performed.

**Verdict:** the replacement three-room standard satisfies the lead's measurable structural rules and establishes a usable minimum language. It does not justify accepting the whole ship as spatially authored. Diagnostic and Workshop still count open floor zones as enclosed subspaces/doorways; those are blocking semantic issues for L8's architectural claim. The repeated cross-shaped plan grammar also needs a deliberate ship-level decision before acceptance. Navigation evidence is strong and supplies no basis here for alleging a gameplay failure. Balance remains unaccepted and outside this review.

## What is real

- **Residential:** six furnished rear recesses, short hull-connected separators, side service connections and a common/exit bay replace the old four equipment islands. The cabin separators only reach from y24–140 (southern equivalents y820–936); their y140–300 side passages and broadly open fronts make these cutaway cabin bays, rather than six fully compartmented rooms. That is a coherent desktop combat compromise, provided rendered furnishing/thresholds carry their domestic function. The plan alone must not be described as six closed rooms.
- **Crew:** the inspection cheeks/back walls and two-ended guard cage are actual solids. North/south bypasses around the cage give genuinely different paths and the crossed hull establishes vestibule and exit lock. It is still a broad scanner hall, but it has materially more authored structure than the rejected island arena. Accept as the minimum checkpoint standard.
- **Relay:** four hull banks and a long central rack create real port/starboard aisles, with generous north/south returns. The central rack is honestly the one permitted island. End returns are intentionally broad industrial circulation, not doorway sequences. Accept this functional vocabulary.
- **Coolant V1.1:** split central pump reservations leave a real 160-unit transverse service gap; north/south circuits and port/starboard pump zones have a physical machine relationship. This is a plausible plant floor, although it closely repeats Relay's plan. Safety's two-ended central cage and Annulus's retained core similarly provide real circulation obstacles. The repaired Loading scar and retained shaft balcony are useful ship-specific exceptions visible in the twenty-up.

## Blocking design issues

1. **Diagnostic is still one open hall with a scalloped equipment frontage.** In `shipArchitectureAct3.ts`, the instrument/nave boundary y300 and nave/return boundary y660 have no separating solid anywhere across their declared spans. The short rear teeth end at y160/180 and begin at y780, respectively. Consequently the green “openings” at x200–520 / 560–1080 and x200–520 / 600–1080 merely annotate uninterrupted floor. The individual PNG makes this unambiguous. The 100% connected-solid statistic comes from rear header strips and backed equipment; it does not create a working gallery and separate return route. Add an actual connected partition/working bay arrangement with discrete wide thresholds and a physical return, or explicitly redesign/justify this as a single diagnostic hall without claiming the missing enclosure.

2. **Workshop's named room sequence is mostly metadata.** The live V1.1 closes the unused rear strip and gives machinery sensible hull attachment, which is a real correction. However, most of y300 (tool gallery/work floor), x260 (vestibule/work floor), x900 (work floor/gantry) and y760 (work floor/service gallery) has no partition. The separated green spans do not correspond to separated doors; the small wall gantry near x1020–1120 does not enclose the named gantry alcove. The result remains an expansive central rectangle with machines moved to its edges. Require at least one actual loading-to-work threshold and a connected tool/service enclosure that preserves two clear exits and the central crowd floor. More floor marks or names cannot resolve this.

3. **Do not propagate “three spaces + cycles” as sufficient ship design.** Diagnostic, Safety, Coolant and Workshop repeatedly use a west/east lock, central fight floor, top equipment band and bottom return; the twenty-up extends this cross/banded grammar through much of Act4. Removing islands has improved the negative space, but repeated hull ears and rear notches do not by themselves give distinct inhabited departments. Safety in particular labels a single open upper band “Recorder and AI booths”; the two solid machinery blocks at x280/820, y0–180 are not two walkable booths. Its cage is real, so this is not an empty-plan failure. Require a spatially specific booth/recorder arrangement or an accurate open instrumentation-gallery concept, and review the revised Diagnostic/Workshop/Safety together for distinct threshold and work-space rhythm. This is an architectural acceptance issue, not a request for decorative variety everywhere.

The inspected plan checks also report clear R144 fight circles, at least 60% connected solid area, no more than two islands and empty opening/sealed-opening errors. The checker validates legal anchors, named neighboring polygons, width, threshold sweeps and actual R32 reachability after inserting a temporary barrier. Two segments on one broad open boundary can therefore satisfy graph cycles and sealed-segment reachability by walking around the inserted barrier on that same floor. This is valid clearance evidence but insufficient proof of two architecturally independent routes. Standard Relay/Crew have real alternate circuits despite this weakness; Diagnostic/Workshop exploit the weakness in effect, regardless of author intent.

Ingress overrides bind raw points to named rear lockers/headers/doors, and the receipt supports native approach legality. Those are meaningful mounting choices. The CLI's feature test only requires a nearby nonwalkable point and a name containing vent/door/tear; it does not prove an authored visible aperture or a convincing ship breach. No breach blocker is established by these plan images. Final rendered review must establish aperture/readability, especially rear headers; do not silently relabel a marker as that proof.

## Nonblocking limits and disposition

Plans cannot adjudicate waist-height actor occlusion, furnishing quality, light-stamp repetition, warning readability or the final material finish. Those remain with the art review and guarded native captures. A calm plan cannot establish pacing, difficulty or hardware performance; the current balance rejection is not overturned here. Industrial Relay/Coolant similarity is tolerable in isolation; it becomes material when the same circulation recipe replaces most departmental identities.

Keep the replacement standard as the minimum implementation reference. Withhold **ship-wide semantic plan acceptance** until Diagnostic and Workshop gain real spatial separation and Safety's booth claim is resolved; then inspect their labeled plans together with the twenty-up. Preserve the numerical, navigation, crowd, arena, visibility and area guarantees. This review changes no source/test and does not independently recertify the existing receipt.
