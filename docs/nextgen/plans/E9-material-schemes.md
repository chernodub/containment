# E9 — material schemes for twenty ship interiors

Each room has two dominant material families and a smaller, physically sourced accent. The floor differs from the main wall face in **both hue and value**. A lighter version of the same grey is insufficient. Wall caps are a third value within their wall family: broad, light and readable above a darker near face and contact-rich baseboard.

All twenty schemes are active after the three-room standard review. Crew Checkpoint, Diagnostic Gallery and Swarm Junction follow the owner's requested directions. The palette is presentation metadata; separately, the owner authorised shallow wall furniture and scanner-post colliders with a maximum48u wall depth and at least140u clear passage.

Hex values are sRGB material targets before room lighting. `deck` is the main floor; `zone` is its neighbouring sub-space treatment; `trim` is the wall-cap/inlay reference; `key` belongs to visible fixtures. A wall-face target is an explicit runtime material direction, not a request to tint the entire room. Preserve the original models' dark recesses, bevel highlights, emissive primitives and baked AO.

## Application

- Keep the main floor matte and relatively calm. Different named sub-spaces use the adjacent treatment, a broad panel orientation or a restrained material change; their seams remain below actors and telegraphs.
- Give walls a broad cap, a darker face and a contact base. Do not wash the face and floor into the same hue with ambient lighting. Light can connect the two families without erasing their material separation.
- Colour comes from visible fixture geometry: lamp bars, screen faces, scanner emitters, reactor windows and rooted growth. A white disc of light does not replace a fixture. Avoid global emissive tinting of ordinary panels.
- Keep saturated red/orange graphics out of quiet floors. Checkpoint's red/green gate indicators are small physical status lights. Its yellow-black bands are muted lane paint. Overload's warm colour comes from the core and hardware above the blue deck.
- Preserve the original kit's finish hierarchy. Recolour by finish role and room purpose; retain AO, roughness, dark recesses and glass alpha. A colour scheme must survive Low through geometry, base materials and visible emissive sources.

The shared and per-act profiles encode these `deck`, `zone`, `trim` and `key` targets. `INTERIOR_WALL_FINISHES` applies the wall/casing and cap families to the original Blender finishes without overwriting baked AO or recess values. Residential's pendant, Transmission's uplink dish, Freight's hoists, Workshop's tool bridges and Swarm's airway exchanger carry the same room scheme into wide crops where wall furniture falls outside the camera. They occupy ceiling space and cut away as opaque assemblies when projected over an actor.
