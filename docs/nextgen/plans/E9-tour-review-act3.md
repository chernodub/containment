## Diagnostic Gallery

**Read:** a recognisable diagnostic nave, with dark rubber-blue flooring, a quiet ECG inlay and pale clinical hardware. Its identification depends more heavily on the suspended diagnostic sign and ECG than on instrument silhouettes in some close crops.

- **Wall face and cap:** broad pale caps and dark faces at the side bays read as substantial enclosures. Near-side vertical faces and their shadows are visible where the crop includes them. The large pale gantry/casing forms are present, but the close framing does not always show their complete arch silhouette.
- **Flooring:** dark rubberised fields and the low-contrast waveform keep the fight floor quiet and function-specific. The floor remains clearly darker and cooler than the off-white hardware at both sizes.
- **Clusters:** benches and sample/instrument trays are most useful at the edges of 03 and the later return views. They give the bays a clinical function, although much of the nave can still be seen without a close instrument cluster.
- **Visible sources:** long physical strip housings, cyan instrument sources and their shaped pools are visible. The suspended warning framework is a real object with depth and shadow; it does not read as a floating text overlay.
- **Landmark:** the diagnostic gantry/sign group and waveform inlay are the consistent identifiers. The capture metadata includes `clinical-scanner-arch` in all five frames; this review does not infer an absent asset from a clipped silhouette.
- **Weakest crop:** **04**. It contains a large dark floor field, the sign to the right and a bench largely at the lower edge/HUD. Its diagnostic identity remains readable, but relies too much on text rather than an unmistakable instrument vignette. The ECG in 02 does more useful work.
- **Geometry/material findings:** no definite missing wall, detached source or furnishing penetration seen. No clear palette regression: dark floor and light instrument casing separation is intact.

## Safety Interlock Station — partial evidence

**Read:** a safety/control room with a strong recorder landmark when it enters the crop, but only partial tour coverage. The first two views rely heavily on the suspended local-record signage.

- **Wall face and cap:** warm khaki broad caps, darker faces and cyan-lit bases clearly enclose the side bays. These have visible height and contact rather than reading as floor stripes.
- **Flooring:** dark petrol flooring and restrained circuit/lockout graphics distinguish it from Diagnostic. The value and hue separation from the warm wall tops survives 480×270.
- **Clusters:** right-side instrument benches make the bays credible. The large recorder/reel machine and authored instrument array are much more evident in the failure image than in 01/02, where they are largely outside the crop.
- **Visible sources:** cyan equipment windows and strip fixtures have physical housings. The pools beside the bays are attached to visible hardware. The local-record sign is supported by a real suspended rail.
- **Landmark:** the recorder headed “LOCAL RECORD / BEFORE AWAKENING” is the strongest actual machine silhouette, visible in the upper-left of the failure image. The first two accepted views instead identify the room through “LOCAL SAFETY RECORD / RESCUE IMPOSSIBLE / RECORD LOCKED.”
- **Weakest available crop:** **01**, with the marine far left and a large central floor field under the rail. View 02 is better framed by the capped bays. The failure image is compositionally more furnished, but its warning bounds prevent acceptance.
- **Geometry/material findings:** no definite broken enclosure or major penetration seen in these three images. Fine lines under the sign in 02 do not establish a particular surface defect. **Full-room acceptance is withheld because the evidence is incomplete.**

## Coolant Plant

**Read:** a coherent utility interior. Pale mineral-green deck, dark pump machinery, large grates and seafoam instrument light form a distinct material and function combination.

- **Wall face and cap:** 03/05 show broad caps, readable dark near faces and a grounded base. In the central views the pump banks and their heavy foundations provide much of the enclosure depth.
- **Flooring:** quiet pump-loop/inspection arrows and the broad drain grates establish maintenance circulation without covering the marine with busy graphics. The lighter mineral deck remains separate from the cool dark machinery.
- **Clusters:** paired pumps, connected pipes, the valve manifold and twin-screen console form useful service groups. The wall bank in 03 and the pump pair in 04 are especially legible; the exit-side maintenance units support 05.
- **Visible sources:** instrument windows and small seafoam/cyan sources are visible. Their rectangular pools have clear hardware origins, although a few pools are bright enough to dominate nearby floor value.
- **Landmark:** the paired pump/pipe machinery is the reliable large identifier. In less furnished crops the grates and inspection diagram maintain utility-room identity.
- **Weakest crop:** **01**, where the marine is far left and the pumps are clipped at the edges. The diagram and grates carry most of the central read. It remains more specific than a generic tiled hall, but has less nearby machine interest than 03/04.
- **Geometry/material findings:** thin dark floor/zone-edge lines occur in several views; the stills do not establish an actual open seam or missing floor. No definite furnishing penetration or clear palette regression seen. Empty calm stills do not establish crowded-combat readability on this pale deck.

## Service Shaft Landing

**Read:** the strongest architectural landmark in this group. The retained deep shaft, pulley/guide assembly, cross-braces and landing curb communicate vertical transport immediately in 01/03.

- **Wall face and cap:** cool enclosure faces and pale cap surfaces have real thickness. The shaft rim, amber rail tops and braced sides give convincing depth. This is recognisably built around a void rather than drawn onto the floor.
- **Flooring:** warm soot/olive-brown anti-slip and grated panels contrast with the cool shaft enclosure. The repeated small pattern is much busier than Coolant's deck; it remains readable as industrial flooring at reduction, but loses some of its material subtlety.
- **Clusters:** the near-shaft control cabinet and luminaire in 01, and the twin-screen cabinet/three-valve service group in 04, fit the landing function and stay associated with the enclosure.
- **Visible sources:** cabinet windows, wall-side lamps and the shaft-side fixture hardware are visible. Their cool pools have plausible sources; amber rails remain painted structure rather than unexplained glowing lines.
- **Landmark:** the deep braced shaft is clear in 01/03. The “LOCAL ACCESS ONLY” exit and service hardware take over in 05 when most of the void is cropped away.
- **Weakest crop:** **05**, because the actual shaft is largely outside the frame and the local scene becomes a more generic service return. It still retains more structural identity than Workshop's central floor views.
- **Surface finding:** fine angular/horizontal interference is visible within the legacy floor panels, particularly the lower-right panel of 05 and areas of 02/03. It is less conspicuous at 480×270 but the floor remains visually busy. Source inspection finds near/coplanar layered surfaces: legacy panel top is `.005m`, the new hull floor is `.005m`, named zones are `.006m`; a legacy zinc slot lip and new chart seams both reach `.010m`. These heights make surface overlap/depth precision a plausible cause, but do not identify which pair produces every observed stripe. Preserve the floor panel's grain shader hook during any fix.
- **Other geometry/material findings:** no definite missing shaft enclosure or major raised-furnishing penetration seen. The warm floor/cool structure scheme remains distinct.

## Infested Workshop — original tour before overhead revision

**Read:** the edge bays are credible converted workstations, but the original central nave fails the “something of interest in every approximately 40% window” aim. Views 01/02 contain mostly floor, drain grates and hazard lanes.

- **Wall face and cap:** pale broad caps and deep dark faces are particularly clear in 03/04/05. Side dividers have height, shadow and real end thickness. In 01/02 the small amount of wall in frame cannot compensate for the empty centre.
- **Flooring:** dusty plum rubber/industrial fields, a restrained circular workshop inlay, hazard-edged lane crossings and dark drains distinguish the floor from the pale mint hardware. The inlay is quiet, but its large low-contrast symbol cannot name the room by itself. The floor is coloured rather than a bare tile grid; composition remains the problem.
- **Clusters:** 03 shows three wall-related working surfaces/instrument groups, with growth crawling over the upper bench. 05 has a larger work surface, bench, visible lamp and invasive strands. These are coherent local scenes rather than scattered boxes. 04 clips much of its workbench to the lower edge.
- **Visible sources:** pale green bench lamps, cool wall-mounted cylindrical sources and the growth's small violet points are visible in the furnished views. Rectangular pools are attached to the hardware. In 01/02 much of the light originates outside the crop, weakening the visible-source requirement there.
- **Landmark:** the growth-covered bench/large equipment group works in 03/05. **No strong workshop landmark is visible in 01/02.** Sparse strands across the floor suggest infestation, but are too slight to replace a machine silhouette.
- **Weakest crop:** **02**, followed closely by 01. The marine stands in a large central floor field; the inlay, grates and hazard boundary could belong to many industrial rooms. At 480×270 the room function is carried mainly by the HUD title. These original views do not satisfy the room-window brief. The pending overhead tool-bridge revision must be assessed from its own images.
- **Geometry/material findings:** no definite missing wall or major furnishing penetration seen. Growth strands cross drains and surfaces as authored decoration; these stills do not demonstrate collision changes. The plum floor/pale mint casing separation is coherent, with no clear global palette regression.

## Communal Atrium 01 — source follow-up only

The earlier four-room review records visible fine striped patches behind/right of the garden. Read-only source inspection now establishes a definite coplanar overlap that matches that area:

- `src/render/world/RoomInteriors.ts:100` draws every named space at `Y=.006m`; they share the `e9-interior-floor` material (`:143`). The base hull lies at `.005m` (`:97`).
- `src/game/roguelike/shipArchitectureAct1.ts:43` defines North service gallery as `(192,170,690,150)` and East garden turn as `(698,170,184,246)`. Their rectangles overlap across `x=698..882`, `y=170..320` behind/right of the garden.
- `InteriorDeckChart.polygon` in `src/render/world/InteriorDeckGeometry.ts:38` clips each polygon to the legal deck; it does not subtract previously submitted named zones. The overlapping zones therefore retain differently coloured triangles at the same height.

The geometry overlap is established by source; its correspondence to the visible stripe is a source-supported diagnosis, not a claim that a particular GPU depth comparison was measured. No shared floor code was edited during this review.
