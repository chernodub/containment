## Five requested density layers

**Functional floors and story debris:** the old large untouched deck has been broken into purposeful aisles, bay fronts, work surfaces, drains and worn circulation. Hatches, scuffs, scattered fragments and interrupted tracks are present beyond seams. At native size, multiple feature classes are visibly present in the inspected views. At480, broad surface blocks dominate while many small marks collapse into the same fork-and-dots symbol. Presence alone does not establish a used place. Crew, Relay, Transmission, Annulus and Manual Control particularly need a distinct interrupted activity or aftermath that survives this scale.

**Continuous perimeter:** the visible left walls and selected far walls have equipment faces, repeated structural supports and connected services. This addresses the isolated display-island problem. Repetition is still conspicuous across environments, but these edges now read as part of a ship. The selected views do not prove every unseen perimeter segment.

**Overhead and shadows:** the selected far-edge views show pipe runs, high suspended elements and long shadows. They add a vertical envelope while leaving the marine visible. This is a successful density layer in the inspected views; no request to move the camera follows. Very long straight transverse elements in entry views retain a diagrammatic rhythm, especially around communications/engineering.

**Existing obstacle footprints:** Residential's bed, desk, chair and scattered domestic items now form a readable inhabited silhouette; the missing-major-furniture problem from the primary review is visibly corrected. Cargo stacks are believable. Other technical footprints gain secondary heights and displaced pieces, but repeated drum/device/case additions still sometimes read as accessories on an otherwise untouched platform. More components per rectangle are not needed merely to increase counts. Their grouping must explain the room's work.

**Flat organic mats and veins:** Workshop, Swarm and the inspected boss arena now have broad connected growth around machine masses, with human floor visible between and within them. This substantially addresses “infestation only on machines.” Flatness is an explicit part of the requested solution and is not a defect by itself. The weakness is the regular graphic treatment: large nearly uniform mauve lobes, tidy isolated polygon holes and single thin veins can look like a stencil. Irregular planar fringes, less uniform openings, branching veins and restrained local value variation can improve this without raised terrain or additional collision.
