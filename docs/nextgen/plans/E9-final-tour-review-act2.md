# E9 frozen final tour review — Act 2 rooms

Reviewed Crew Checkpoint, Armory, Freight Hold, Breached Loading Bay, Relay Racks and Transmission Chamber, frames **01–05**, from `artifacts/nextgen/e9-final-captures/` (frozen build `b7b3d7882d5b4a7892f2f684e55b243040e4981c`). All **30 native 1920×1080 PNGs** and all **30 exact 480×270 reductions** were opened individually. Reductions use the existing `review480` helper and matching filenames under `artifacts/nextgen/e9-final-captures-review/review480/`. The final gallery will relocate these files under `artifacts/nextgen/e9-final/native/` and `artifacts/nextgen/e9-final/review480/`. This is a saved-image art review, not a gameplay or performance conclusion.

## Crew Checkpoint

- **Function:** Spawn (01) and Fight circle (02) read immediately as a security checkpoint. The manufactured scanner roofs, large upward **05**, scanner glyphs, lit posts and directional/hazard floor bands survive reduction. North bypass (03) has a recognisable baggage roller station and a roofed guard booth. The booth still presents more roof than operator equipment.
- **Walls and palette:** pale caps, dark teal panel faces, real end thickness and grounded shadows create substantial enclosure. Warm pale circulation floor and cooler inspection fields separate from those faces. The main hall remains broad and quiet, with arrows rather than a busy tile grid.
- **Sources:** scanner-post diffusers, baggage status lamps, housed strip lights and the paired exit terminals are visible. Long strip fixtures remain conspicuous repeated objects. Exit (05) has a thin isolated upright light/shadow near the middle-left; its attachment is not visually obvious in this crop.
- **Actor:** the marine's helmet, weapon, torso and legs remain visibly separate from the structures in all five stills. No architecture crosses the visible body. This does not establish clearance during movement.
- **Remaining weakness:** **04 South bypass**. Large pale/cool floor fields dominate and its lower working equipment is mostly hidden behind the foreground wall/crop. It reads as a service passage inside the checkpoint, but has much less security-specific interest than 01–03. Exit (05) is also sparse away from its terminal destination. No definite missing wall or major furnishing penetration is visible.

## Armory

- **Function:** the radial rifle carousel and horseshoe issue counter identify Spawn (01) and Port magazine (03) at both sizes. Fight circle (02) retains the carousel/counter at its upper edge, with a quiet rifle/inspection floor diagram under the marine. Counter trays and small stores support issue/maintenance, though several broad work surfaces remain lightly stocked.
- **Walls and palette:** broad pale counter tops, dark panelled near faces and contact shadows give the landmark real mass. The cool blue deck stays distinct from the pale hardware. Right-side hull caps and faces in 04/05 provide a physical enclosure rather than floor stripes.
- **Sources:** housed linear lights and wall-mounted sources are visible. The exit-side conveyor/locker bank receives shaped cyan and warm light, although some pools have rounded ends and the common strip-light language is more evident than task-specific workshop lighting.
- **Actor:** the complete visible marine silhouette remains clear of the counter, carousel and exit hardware in these five stills. Bright exit light changes local floor value without washing out the body outline.
- **Remaining weakness:** **04 Starboard fitting bay**, followed by **05 Exit**. The carousel is mostly outside these crops, and the local scene relies on the counter, generic conveyor and lockers; the fitting function is less specific. At native size, especially **02**, the carousel's near cylindrical face has conspicuous mottled/striated cyan patches. These look uneven against the otherwise clean casing treatment; the stills do not establish their runtime cause. No definite major geometry penetration is visible.

The clearest remaining composition issue in this reviewed pair is Crew 04. Armory has the stronger room-scale landmark, but its outer fitting/exit windows are weaker than its magazine views. Neither tour shows an idle orange dashed placement rectangle; the visible exit plates/corner trim belong to the destination hardware.

## Freight Hold

- **Function and final hoists:** cargo cases, restrained storage pads, the **07** dispatch lane and the new hoist headers now establish cargo handling in all five saved windows. The hook is an especially useful silhouette at 480×270. The formerly empty Spawn (01) now contains a complete hoist/header group, and Fight circle (02) keeps cargo plus handling equipment beside the marine.
- **Walls, floor and sources:** the amber deck, pale machinery tops and darker blue hull/casing faces stay separate. Booth (04) has a substantial cap, near face and contact shadow. Visible cargo-bank lamps, hoist task-light housing, consoles and strip fixtures motivate warm/cool pools; several pools still have soft rounded ends.
- **Actor and remaining weakness:** no hoist/header crosses the visible marine body in these stills, including the close approach in 02. **04 Loading control booth** remains weakest: the local enclosure has no obvious operator desk in the crop, and the hoist's broad roof is more prominent than its handling mechanism. The hoists improve identity, but do not make every part of the amber floor an inhabited working scene. No definite missing wall or major furnishing penetration is visible.

## Breached Loading Bay

- **Function:** the large peeled hull scar, riveted repair platform and mounted work lamps identify a damaged docking space immediately in 01/03/04/05. The pressure-lock side adds consoles, valves and a real threshold. At 480×270 the damage remains distinctive, although its broad folded surface can read as draped gold metal more strongly than a deep rupture.
- **Walls, floor and sources:** the subdued olive/slate deck and quiet circular loading-pad graphic leave the marine clear. The right hull has pale caps, dark faces and visible depth; the scar's platform has a grounded edge. Repair lamps visibly cast shaped warm light, with cooler hull/service sources supporting the pressure-lock approach.
- **Actor and remaining weakness:** the full visible body stays clear of architecture in all five views. **02 Fight circle** is weakest: most of the scar is clipped high, and the local scene is largely a pad and grate. Spawn/Port apron also leave substantial quiet deck between the marine and the landmark. No definite major geometry break is apparent; the main limitation is furnishing distribution and the soft folded scar silhouette.

## Relay Racks

- **Function:** the central U-crowned relay spine, attached bright console and nearby service cabinets give 01/02 a credible communications-machine read. Equipment silhouettes survive reduction, while front-panel detail largely collapses into black. Exit (05) retains grounded hull equipment and clear destination hardware.
- **Walls, floor and sources:** caps, bank faces and base shadows are physical at native size, but their dark teal values merge with the deck at 480×270. Faint service markings keep the floor quiet without supplying much local sub-space distinction. Screens and housed linear fixtures are visible sources; the repeated bright strips carry a disproportionate amount of the composition.
- **Actor and remaining weakness:** no hardware covers the visible marine silhouette in these stills. **03 North cross aisle** and **04 Console bay** remain weak: specific working equipment is pushed against the top/bottom margins, leaving a large dark floor field around the actor. At reduction these could be generic service-room windows. No definite new surface break is visible; darkness and cropped equipment are the substantive shortcomings.

## Transmission Chamber

- **Function and final dish:** the new suspended dish is clearly readable in 01/02/05, with radial panels, receiver and luminous ring surviving 480×270. It materially improves the nave's identity over the baseline empty-floor views. Uplink chancel (03) retains the original dish, rack bank and paired console. The large newer dish is an effective object silhouette, though its ceiling attachment is not apparent in these crops.
- **Walls, floor and sources:** low barriers have real caps, dark near faces and illuminated base detail. The quiet antenna/radiation diagram fits the room, while blue/plum deck and cool hardware remain a restrained palette. Dish-ring/receiver light, equipment windows and housed strips are visible; strips still dominate large areas outside the dish views.
- **Actor and remaining weakness:** the new dish stays beside the marine's visible body in 02 and away from it in 01/05; none of these saved views shows body occlusion. **04 Service airlock** remains the weakest crop of this room: neither dish is visible, and the scene is mostly floor, a low barrier and repeated light strips. It does not clearly communicate an airlock's operating function. Some quiet floor bands show fine diagonal patterning at native size, but these stills do not establish a rendering fault. No definite major furnishing penetration is visible.

## Remaining weaknesses across all six frozen tours

The least specific final windows are **Transmission 04**, **Relay 03/04** and **Crew 04**. Freight's final hoists resolve the absence of a cargo-handling landmark in its earlier central views, while its booth still lacks visible controls. Armory 04/05 and Breached 02 remain weaker than their landmark-facing views. Armory's uneven cyan carousel-face treatment is the most conspicuous material concern in this set.

All six tours preserve a visibly readable marine body in the saved calm frames. None shows the idle orange dashed ability-placement rectangle. These observations do not certify movement, ability use or heavy-combat readability, and do not claim that every approximately 40% window meets the interior brief.
