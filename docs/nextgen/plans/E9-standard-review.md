Reference inspected: original L8 Residential, Diagnostic, Crew Checkpoint and Swarm native calm PNGs, plus the twenty calm/kiting and old/new-plan sheets in the read-only L8 converged gallery. Residential has believable cabin contents and warm thresholds; its fight gallery still has a large blank crop. E9 must exceed its spatial legibility, not simply copy its hue.

## Original kit — Round 1: better objects, standard still not accepted

Actually opened all fifteen native1920×1080 PNGs in `artifacts/nextgen/e9-original-round1/`: frames01–05 for Crew Checkpoint, Diagnostic Gallery and Swarm Junction. Also opened every corresponding exact480×270 reduction in `artifacts/nextgen/e9-original-round1-review/review480/`. The original Residential reference was opened at both sizes from the read-only `l8-final/converged/native/combat-calm-residential-gallery.png`; its reduction and a complete six-column Residential/room-tour comparison are under `e9-original-round1-review/`. This is an art review of the saved frames, not a performance assessment.

**This round does not yet exceed Residential or meet the every40%-window standard.** The new manufactured wall faces, pale caps, base contact and cabinet fronts are a substantial improvement over floor stripes. But too many gameplay crops still contain a large smooth empty floor with important machines clipped at the perimeter. Residential also has an empty centre; its advantage is that the beds, desk, lockers, trim and warm practical form an immediately recognisable cabin vignette even at480. The new original assets need that same composed relationship to their surrounding space.

- **Crew Checkpoint01/02, spawn/fight:** the scanner appears as a long slab along the left screen edge. Its roof beam and cyan spill are visible, but the opening reads sideways rather than as a clear inspection passage. Hazard bands, floor arrows and two long fixture shapes carry almost all the security identity; the centre and right half are largely empty. **03, north bypass:** wall panels and a locker front have believable volume, yet the bypass still has little to identify an inspection or guard workspace. **04, south bypass:** the low illuminated partition is stronger than before, but the large machine at the bottom is mostly cropped away and the surrounding floor remains anonymous. **05, exit:** the pair of bright terminal screens finally gives a readable equipment station. That useful scale/composition is missing from the first four views. At480 the room still reads primarily as a marked arena with perimeter barriers.
- **Diagnostic Gallery01/02, spawn/fight:** the subdued ECG trace helps name the function, but the repeated warning gantry remains the dominant object. No substantial diagnostic machine is readable in either crop; small beam/cutaway fragments also add dark visual strokes across the centre view. **03, Recorder workbay:** the new two-well instrument bench is recognisable and the rear recorder is visible at the upper edge. This is the strongest diagnostic view, although it still feels like separated counters rather than one assembled working station. **04, Return workbay:** the gantry obscures much of the right bench, the left station is mostly below the crop, and the central floor has little interest. **05, exit:** one wall-backed bench is clear, but the sign is more prominent than the equipment and the left two-thirds remain sparse. At480 the repeated text is consistently easier to recognise than the laboratory hardware.
- **Swarm Junction01/02, spawn/fight:** the new plenum is clipped at the left/top edge while broad blank machine roofs, flat purple seam ribbons and a faint segmented circle dominate the scene. The industrial drain gratings help establish a serviced deck, but they do not make the crossing feel occupied or consumed. **03, north airway:** this is the best Swarm view because the root-covered unit and the opposing manifold are both in frame. The plenum is seen mainly along its narrow crown/side; its three round duct mouths are not a readable landmark. **04, south airway:** this is the weakest of all fifteen frames. Almost the entire crop is empty floor, two gratings and seam graphics; the useful machinery is cut off at the lower corners. **05, exit:** the root-covered cabinet and green source are visible, yet the round-duct identity is still lost and the remaining floor is very sparse. At480 the infestation reads mostly as drawn strands with occasional purple points, rather than a thick growth mass embedded in a recognisable ventilation bank.

The floor is quieter and actor contrast is good, so the answer is not to raise graphic contrast across the fight circle. The next composition pass should put the existing original stations on camera-facing, wall-backed edges where their working faces stay visible from spawn, centre and the named spaces. Turn the scanner to communicate an actual passage; give its opening a readable threshold. Show the plenum's duct face and one strong root/cyst mass from the crossing, and bring a complete service cluster into the south-airway crop. Assemble the diagnostic bench, screen, probe/recorder and nearby source as one station, with the repeated warning supported around that station rather than covering its useful surface.

Lighting still shares much of the same linear-spill language across these three functions. Several long luminous objects read as flat grilles lying over the floor, with weak attachment to the neighbouring equipment. Tie their casing/support and spill direction to the actual bench, scanner or plenum, and use a restrained service apron or connected trench to explain the remaining quiet floor. Preserve clear combat space, wall/camera cutaways and the calmest values under the marine. The first priority is to make the existing machinery visible and purposeful in these fifteen crops before propagating the original-kit standard.
