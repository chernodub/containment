## Awakening Bay

**Read:** clearly a cryogenic recovery compartment. The retained open berth and occupied-looking sealed banks are much stronger identifiers than the new generic service equipment. The pale machinery against the cool blue deck remains readable at 480×270.

- **Wall face and cap:** the right hull in 02/05 has a broad pale upper surface, a visibly dark face and a grounded lower edge. The low service divider in 04 has real thickness and shadow. Most of 01/03 derives its height cues from the pod banks rather than a prominent bulkhead, so those frames do not independently demonstrate every wall treatment.
- **Flooring:** broad blue deck plates, quiet inspection markings, the open berth's local pad and the striped crossing establish circulation without a busy tile grid. The large quiet surfaces remain deliberately sparse; the sub-space distinction is stronger at the marked crossing than within the blue floor itself.
- **Clusters:** clinical trays, service cabinets and the recovery chair/kit station fit the room function and stay associated with its banks or dividers. The recovery-chair cluster in 04 is a useful local scene rather than random debris.
- **Visible sources:** the long glass-strip housings are legible, and the service equipment produces shaped cool light. The floor strips are unusually conspicuous as repeated objects, but they have visible physical housings. No unsupported bright disc dominates these five views.
- **Landmark:** the open recovery berth in 01, with sealed pod rows bracketing the aisle in 01–03/05, names the place immediately.
- **Weakest crop:** **04**. The marine is close to the left edge while the interesting recovery equipment sits left of centre; the lower-right floor is a large quiet area. It still reads as a clinical recovery room, but the composition feels more like the side of a service zone than a complete authored vignette.
- **Geometry/material findings:** no definite missing surface, obvious furnishing penetration or material regression visible in these stills. The large blue floor and pale casings remain separate in value. Orange corners around the exit in 02/05 belong to the exit fixture shown in these frames; I am not reporting them as an idle ability preview.

## Passenger Vault

**Read:** the most consistent function identity across these four tours. The human silhouettes, pod windows and life-sign faces clearly communicate occupied passenger storage. That identity survives all five reductions.

- **Wall face and cap:** the pod foundations have dark vertical faces and convincing contact shadow. The right hull in 05 has a pale cap and readable height. The central views are framed primarily by equipment banks, so their architectural identity is a vault lined with banks rather than a wall-lined corridor.
- **Flooring:** cooler indigo-blue deck, long paired inspection lines and striped cross-aisles create a coherent transport/service floor. It is quiet under the marine and does not compete with the silhouettes inside the pods. The lanes are more specific than a generic tile field, although large stretches remain the same blue material family.
- **Clusters:** retained occupied pod groups carry the scene; the sample bench, service cabinet and console at the exit support them. Nothing in these frames suggests a new furnishing island obscuring the main inspection aisle.
- **Visible sources:** cyan life-sign displays, pod illumination and the physical glass-strip fixtures remain visible. Light falling below the banks appears attached to actual hardware. Their pale blue lighting is consistent with the cryogenic function.
- **Landmark:** repeated occupied pod banks are visible in every frame, including the service-return view. Frame 03 is the clearest broad statement of the room.
- **Weakest crop:** **04**. Most of its left and lower area is open return-floor space, with the pod bank pushed into the upper-right. A foreground cap takes much of the bottom edge. It retains the vault identity, but has less furnishing interest near the marine than 03/05.
- **Geometry/material findings:** no definite geometry break or clear palette regression seen. The repeated pod foundations are heavy, but their depth is coherent; this is an art-weight limitation rather than evidence that their geometry is wrong.

## Residential Gallery

**Read:** the furnished cabin views remain recognisable and warm. The five-frame tour is not equally successful: the central floor crop loses the residential identity.

- **Wall face and cap:** 01/03 show substantial cabin dividers with broad pale caps and dark near faces, rather than thin stripes. Their shadows reinforce height without hiding the marine. The right hull in 03/05 also reads as an enclosure.
- **Flooring:** warm brown cabin/gallery treatments separate this room immediately from cryogenics. Doorway mats, low-contrast graphic lines and the diamond inlay keep combat surfaces quiet. The zoning is visible, but becomes a set of broad brown fields when the camera excludes the cabin furniture.
- **Clusters:** 01 and 03 retain a made berth, writing desk/side unit and stool; these are readable as domestic furniture at 480×270. The larger berth silhouette fits the cabin view without an obvious visible overhang or penetration. Cabinets/desks at the edges of 04 support a furnished gallery, though only fragments remain in that crop.
- **Visible sources:** warm light from the cabin/desk frontage motivates the amber pools in 01/03, and the exit fixture in 05 is strongly lit from visible wall equipment. Long glass strips remain clear physical objects. The warm/cool balance is most successful beside the cabin furniture.
- **Landmark:** the made berth and adjacent desk are effective in 01/03. **They are absent from 02.** There is no residential landmark visible in that central crop.
- **Weakest crop:** **02, clearly**. At both sizes, nearly the entire frame is brown deck, faint lane/inlay lines and repeated strips around the marine. It could belong to many room functions. This frame does **not** meet the stated aim that every approximately 40% gameplay window should read as a recognisable residence. Frame 04 is the next weakest, with identifying furniture only at the lower edges.
- **Geometry/material findings:** no definite geometry break or clear material regression visible. The brown floor and cooler wall/furniture family remain distinct where both appear. The failure of 02 is the absence of identifying objects in the crop, not evidence that its brown floor material itself is broken.

## Communal Atrium

**Read:** the garden provides a strong inhabited-ship landmark in 01–03. The exit-side views remain a credible common space, but their square table/seating cluster is less specific than the garden.

- **Wall face and cap:** 04/05 are the strongest wall demonstrations of this group: broad pale caps, dark tall faces, a visible base and regularly mounted amber fixtures. The garden enclosure also has a grounded teal face and readable depth. These walls do not read as floor lines.
- **Flooring:** warm tan/brown zones and the large restrained leaf-like graphic distinguish the common floor from the garden area. The low-frequency treatment survives reduction, with a clear warm floor/cool wall relationship.
- **Clusters:** the planter/tree group in 01–03 is unmistakable. Wall-side desks, bench/seating and the table group give the room a human function. In 04/05 the large square table and blocky seats are simplified enough to resemble an equipment island at first glance; they are less immediately domestic than Residential's bed/desk grouping.
- **Visible sources:** amber wall lamps are easy to locate and their shaped light supports the enclosure. The garden has visible fixture hardware. A small cool round patch beside the table in 05 is less clearly motivated than the wall lamps; it is a local lighting weakness, not a demonstrated geometry defect.
- **Landmark:** the retained garden dominates 01–03 and remains legible at 480×270. The crop in 04/05 loses the trees; its table/seating group becomes the local identifier.
- **Weakest crop:** **04**. It is a broad open exit-side floor with the garden outside the crop and the table/seating cluster appearing quite abstract. It still has a clear wall and furnished common-space structure, so it is stronger than Residential 02.
- **Surface finding:** there are conspicuous fine horizontal/triangular striped patches on the floor **behind/right of the planter in 01–03**, and toward the **upper-left floor in 04/05**. Their abrupt angular boundaries and fine bands read as surface interference rather than convincing wear in the native images. They partly disappear at 480×270. This is a visible artifact to report; the stills alone do not establish whether its cause is coplanar geometry, another layered surface or authored detail. I am not certifying a z-fighting diagnosis.
- **Other geometry/material findings:** no definite missing wall, detached furnishing or major penetration seen. The warm floor/cool wall material separation is intact; I see no clear global palette regression.

## Honest remaining weaknesses

1. **Residential Gallery 02:** the only assigned frame that loses its room function almost completely. The cabin scenes themselves remain good.
2. **Communal Atrium's striped floor patches:** a visible surface artifact at native size; root cause unconfirmed. Its exit table/seating silhouette is also weaker than the garden.
3. **Passenger Vault 04 and Awakening Bay 04:** broad quiet return/service floor crowds the composition, although retained clinical landmarks keep both rooms identifiable.

This review reports the saved views as they stand. It does not claim that every 40% crop has achieved the brief, does not certify frame timing or resource budgets, and does not turn an uncertain surface cause into a confirmed geometry failure. The clearest strengths to preserve are the retained occupied pods, open recovery berth, made cabin berths, garden, substantial wall caps and visible practical fixtures.
