# Original K10 Crown-keel marine and arsenal

Run `sh tools/assets/k10-marine/rebuild.sh` from any working directory. Requires Blender 5.3 on PATH. The command rebuilds the six shipping GLBs, source maps, four-rig turnarounds, eight transparent UI PNGs and artist deformation studies. It uses Blender's bundled Python only. Metal/CUDA/etc is selected when available; CPU remains a supported fallback. No preferences are saved.

`anatomy.py` lofts a changing elliptical pressure-suit surface along the retained bones. Armour regions are copied from that surface, subdivided, offset/shrinkwrapped, solidified and bevelled with weighted normals. Rigid shells follow individual bones; the continuous undersuit blends across elbows, knees and waist. Finger phalanges retain their native bones. Rounded pauldrons, shaped thigh/shin guards, ankle hinges, sole/toe layers, service seams, locks, vents, hoses and rig hardware are modelled geometry. `marine.py` assembles the chosen Crown-keel helmet and the four visibly different shoulder/collar/forearm/back variants.

`helmets.py` contains three closed subdivision helmet studies. Option 3, Crown keel, was selected after actual 140px gameplay review: the wide dorsal return remains readable from behind. `weapons.py` extrudes designed side-profile outlines, then adds chamfered receivers, cylindrical bores, stocks, grips, magazines, rails, thermal openings and an open plasma chamber. The rocket uses a circular shoulder tube with a curved cradle. Existing weapon fitting and precise muzzle translations remain unchanged.

`baking.py` projects the higher-subdivision/bevelled source onto the lower game meshes. Matching components separate during tangent-normal projection; AO and geometric pointiness curvature bake on the assembled character. Flat colour and emissive zones bake from the actual low surface to avoid cross-part colour contamination. Breacher stripes are painted procedurally onto the curved pauldron and baked. AO modulation and curvature wear remain restrained. The shared marine atlas is 2048²: base, tangent normal, AO and emission ship; curvature is an authoring source. Delivery PNGs areRGB8. Weapons use 512² base/normal maps, with emission maps on plasma/rocket. No runtime texture exceeds 2k.

`marine-contract.glb` contains only the existing 123 skeleton nodes, 122 joint inverse binds and 17 animation clips. These retained Unvanquished data keep their separate Creative Commons attribution; they are **not** original MIT art. `contract.py` appends new meshes without round-tripping the skeleton or animation payloads. Face/control bones stay present for M10 animation-only merging by name. All new geometry, material/texture artwork and generating code are original repository-MIT work.

`portraits.py` imports the shipping GLB, samples retained standing/weapon pose data and attaches the actual shipping weapon. It renders four 1024² transparent busts and four 1024×1280 transparent full figures into `public/assets/portraits/k10/`. It never exports a skeleton or changes the production file. `pose_reviews.py` likewise produces artist-only twist/crouch images; native run/fire/hit are reviewed separately with `scripts/nextgen/m9-motion.mjs`.

Multi-object UV packing may change island layout on a rebuild. Freeze the exported assets before native captures/bench/campaigns; the report records the exact shipping hashes used for those runs. Review outputs are under `artifacts/nextgen/k10-final/`; they are intentionally ignored by Git.
